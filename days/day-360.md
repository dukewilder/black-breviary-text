# Day 360: Of the Father’s Bones

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Jacob made Joseph swear, with a hand under his thigh, not to bury him in Egypt. Carry me out of this country, the old man said, and lay me with my fathers in their burying place, and when Joseph had sworn it Jacob bowed himself on the head of the bed.

Joseph kept the oath and chose everything else. He had his servants the physicians embalm his father, forty days of the Egyptian art, and Egypt wept for Jacob seventy days. Pharaoh's servants and the elders of the land went up with the body, with chariots and horsemen, a very great company, and the Canaanites who watched the mourning at the threshing floor of Atad named the place Abel-mizraim, the mourning of Egypt. The father who asked to be carried out of Egypt was carried into Canaan wrapped in it.

A dying parent can bind the ground and no more, because a promise to the dying is kept by the living, with the living's hands and on the living's terms. A believing mother will want her rites at the end, the priest at the bedside or the body washed and buried within the day. She will not be there to see how they are done, and the funeral will speak for whoever arranged it, as Joseph's spoke for Joseph. Keep the letter of whatever you swore, and choose the rest yourself. Genesis sums up the burial in one line, that his sons did for Jacob exactly as he had commanded them, and then sends Joseph back down into Egypt to live out his own life.

Charge: Arrange only a funeral you can stand behind.

Rite: Write down what you have promised, or would promise, a believing parent for their funeral, and beside each item the way you will do it. Sign it and give it to the sibling or executor who will stand with you.

Question: Which of a believing parent's last wishes would you carry out in your own manner, and how?

Closing: I keep my word to the dying and my conscience at the graveside, and the dead have never told the two apart.
