# Day 342: Of the Sleeping Serpent

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A psalm sung in synagogue and church alike says that the keeper of Israel neither slumbers nor sleeps. The Throne Verse of the Quran, which Muslims are taught to recite at bedtime for protection, says the same of its god, that neither drowsiness nor sleep overtakes him. The Hebrew word the psalm uses for that keeper is shomer, the watchman who walks the wall while the town sleeps.

Genesis puts the same verb to work in Eden. The god sets the man in Eden to till it and to keep it, the watchman's word again, so that the first human being is given a post before he is given a wife. The serpent, the subtlest beast of the field, is given none, and when it speaks to the woman it speaks as a creature whose time is its own.

The modern vigil is kept on a phone beside the bed. Alerts arrive at every hour, the mind walks its perimeter from waking until sleep, and a whole trade sells the dark before dawn back to the faithful as discipline, given to the inbox and the cold shower. The watchers admire one another for it and ask the ones who sleep eight hours how they manage. A watch is always kept on behalf of whoever owns what is watched, and the god praised for never sleeping still drafted a man to guard his trees. Whoever answers the alert at two in the morning has taken up Adam's post, and every hour of it is spent guarding someone else's garden.

Charge: Guard only what is yours.

Rite: Tonight turn off notifications on every app that can reach you after dark and leave the phone outside the bedroom. If you wake in the night, do not go to it. As you lie down say aloud {This post was never mine.}

Question: What do you check in the night, and who profits from your checking?

Closing: The insomniac on the throne can count his own trees tonight, and I sleep coiled with the serpent.
