# Day 259: Of Ointment

![A Sinful Woman Repents](../plates/b177.jpg)

*A Sinful Woman Repents. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

She let her hair down in a Pharisee's dining room. Luke calls her a woman of the city who was a sinner and gives her no name, and his account of what she did is physical. She came in with an alabaster jar of ointment while the guests reclined at table, stood behind the guest of honor at his feet, wet them with her tears, wiped them with her hair, kissed them over and over and rubbed the ointment into them. The host said to himself that a real prophet would know what manner of woman was touching him. Jesus told Simon that his house had offered no water for the feet, no kiss and no oil, and that she had supplied all of it.

The Church could not leave her there. Preaching in Rome in five hundred ninety-one, Pope Gregory made her one woman with Mary Magdalene and Mary of Bethany, and explained that the ointment she had once spent on sin she now offered to God. The Golden Legend later sent her thirty years into a desert, and the engraving above kneels her among bare rocks beside a skull, the scent long gone. Rome dropped the identification from its calendar in nineteen sixty-nine.

The law of Exodus reserved one blend of myrrh and sweet spices for the tent, its furnishings and its priests, and forbade it on the flesh of anyone else on pain of being cut off from the people. Gregory's own sermon says the woman had used her ointment to perfume her flesh in forbidden deeds, so the scent changed owners twice.

Charge: Wear a scent for nobody but yourself.

Rite: Try scents on your own skin until one stops you, from the bottles you own or one you buy today because you want it. Put it on your wrists and neck, wear it wherever you go next, and say {Mine, and unrepented.}

Question: Which pleasure of the senses do you still deny yourself because someone once called it vanity?

Closing: The Church gave her ointment to God, and I keep mine for my own throat.
