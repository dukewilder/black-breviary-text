# Day 16: Of Exile

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Abraham rose early, took bread and a skin of water, put them on Hagar's shoulder along with the boy, and sent her away. It was Sarah's demand. She had seen Ishmael, the slave woman's son, laughing at the feast for Isaac's weaning, and told her husband to throw both of them out so that no son of a slave would share the inheritance. Abraham was displeased, and the god told him to do as his wife said.

The water ran out in the wilderness of Beersheba. Hagar laid the child under a bush and went and sat a bowshot off, so as not to see him die, and wept. The well that saved them was found in the wilderness, when the god opened her eyes to it, and Genesis records no one from Abraham's tent coming to look for either of them.

The boy grew up in the wilderness of Paran and became an archer, and his mother took him a wife out of Egypt. Later Abraham did it again, giving all he had to Isaac and sending the sons of his concubines away eastward with gifts, so that the house kept its single heir and the east country had them.

A house that never came looking gave up the right to call anyone back. Any contact you allow is a favor on your terms, and it ends the first time it is used to summon you home. Genesis brings Ishmael back once, to stand with Isaac at the cave of Machpelah and bury their father, and then counts his line, twelve princes by their towns and encampments, settled from Havilah to Shur.

Charge: Build where they sent you.

Rite: Name the return the people who cast you out still expect, the visit home or the standing call, and refuse it today in one sentence with no reason given. {I will not be coming.} Spend those hours on the life you built without them.

Question: What have you built since the door closed behind you?

Closing: What I raise in the wilderness they drove me into is mine, and the house that put me out has no share in it.
