# Day 289: Of Mockery

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Jesus was blindfolded, struck in the face and told to prophesy which of the men holding him had done it, and Mark adds that they spat on him. Luke has Herod and his soldiers treat him as nothing, dress him in a gorgeous robe and send him back to Pilate wearing it, and at the cross the chief priests passed the time telling one another that he had saved others and could not save himself. At none of these mockings does he answer his mockers.

At the mockings the accused raised no complaint. His defenders raised it for him, with indictments, for centuries. In sixteen seventy-six an English judge declared blasphemy a crime at common law, on the ground that Christianity was part of the law of the land, and in nineteen seventy-seven a London jury convicted the editor of a newspaper for printing verses about a centurion's desire for the body taken down from the cross. The offense was abolished in England and Wales in two thousand eight.

The judge said that to call religion a cheat dissolves the obligations that hold civil society together, and that Christianity is part of the law of England, so that to mock it is to speak against the law.

In nineteen seventy-seven the court told the jury that what the editor meant did not matter. The editor walked out of the Old Bailey with a fine of five hundred pounds and nine months in prison suspended over his head, for a page of verse, and the Lords upheld the conviction in a case brought by a private citizen, Mary Whitehouse.

Charge: Laugh at whatever demands to be taken seriously.

Rite: Copy out the creed, pledge or hymn you once said most solemnly. Read it aloud in the voice of the most ridiculous character you can do, laughing wherever it asks for reverence, then fold it into a paper airplane, say {Amen} and fly it into the bin.

Question: What did you once believe so seriously that it now makes you laugh?

Closing: Blasphemy stayed a crime in England until two thousand eight, and I commit it with the windows open.
