# Day 48: Of Tent Pegs

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A treaty stood between Sisera's king and Jael's husband, and for that reason the beaten general ran to her tent. She gave him milk when he asked for water, covered him with a rug, and when he slept drove a tent peg through his temple into the ground. The book names no wrong he had done her, and the song that follows calls her blessed above women and lingers where he fell at her feet.

Saul was sent to war on a grievance centuries old. Samuel sent him against Amalek for what that people had done to Israel on the road out of Egypt, with orders to spare no infant or suckling, and for sparing the king and the best of the sheep he was told the kingdom would be taken from him.

The heads of seventy sons of Ahab lay in two heaps at the gate of Jezreel, and afterward the god told Jehu he had done well in destroying the house of Ahab and promised him sons on the throne to the fourth generation. In the days of Jehu's great-grandson the god told Hosea that he would avenge the blood of Jezreel on the house of Jehu.

A grievance borrowed from a god stays the god's to settle, and the god settled with Saul for sparing one king and, through Hosea, charged to Jehu's house the blood Jehu had shed on the god's word. Hatred for someone who did you real harm has no sponsor and no one to settle it for you. Kept in the head it is rehearsed for years, the peg driven a thousand times in imagination and never once in the world. The one who did the harm is owed one full answer in the world, by law, and after it not another hour of rehearsal.

Charge: Answer your real enemy once, in law.

Rite: Write the enemy's name and the exact injury on a sheet. Under it write the one lawful move that answers it, a complaint or a claim, then file or send it today and say {I answer this once, and in full.}

Question: Which lawful move against the one who wronged you would cost them most?

Closing: Jael was blessed above women for killing her husband's ally, and I keep my hatred for the people who earned it.
