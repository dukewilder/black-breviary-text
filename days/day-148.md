# Day 148: Of Ransom

Hezekiah paid first. When Sennacherib of Assyria took the fortified cities of Judah, the king sent to him at Lachish to say that he had offended and would bear whatever was laid on him, and he was charged three hundred talents of silver and thirty of gold. He emptied the treasuries of the god's house, and then he cut the gold from the temple doors and pillars, gold he had overlaid himself, and sent it to the king of Assyria.

The same chapter calls him the king who trusted the god as no king of Judah before or after him. The next has the angel go out in the night and strike down a hundred and eighty-five thousand in the Assyrian camp, and the old English rendering has men rising early in the morning to find that they were all dead corpses. Sennacherib's own annals record the tribute reaching Nineveh and boast that he shut Hezekiah up in Jerusalem like a bird in a cage, and they mention no angel. Both records agree that the tribute was paid, and the book says where the gold came from, stripped off the god's doors by that same trusting king on the day he needed it.

Asa had sent the temple's silver and gold to one Syrian king and Joash its hallowed things to another before Hezekiah touched the doors. The Bible on the shelf, the siddur, the prayer mat and the crucifix are what that gold was, material held in reserve and sacred until the day it is useful. The book of Kings records each payment without a word of blame, and the Bible kept unopened on a high shelf is guarded more carefully than any of those kings guarded the temple.
