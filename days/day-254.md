# Day 254: Of Home Towns

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Nazareth was proud of him for about a sentence. Luke has Jesus come back to the town where he was brought up and go into its synagogue on the sabbath, as his custom was, and the attendant hands him the scroll of Isaiah. He reads the passage about good news for the poor and release for captives, sits down and tells the room the scripture is fulfilled in their hearing. They wonder at his gracious words, and then someone remembers that this is Joseph's son.

He answers with the record. Elijah, in a famine, was sent to a widow in Sidon, and of all the lepers in Israel Elisha cleansed only a Syrian. The room hears that the god's favor went to foreigners, rises, drives him out of town and leads him to the brow of its hill to throw him off. In Mark the neighbors recite the family to him, the carpenter, Mary's son, the brother of James and Joses and Judas and Simon, with sisters they did not trouble to name.

Mark writes that he could do no mighty work there beyond laying hands on a few of the sick, and marveled at their unbelief. A chapter earlier the same man had raised a dead girl in another town, and in Nazareth he was unable. He gives the reason himself, that a prophet has honor everywhere except in his own country, among his own kin and in his own house, and he went round the other villages teaching. Later in the chapter he tells the twelve to shake the dust off their feet at any place that will not receive them, as a testimony against it. The dust is the town's own, carried out on the soles, and the order is to leave it on the road.

Charge: Take your work where nobody recites your family.

Rite: Leave the group chat, mailing list or prayer chain of the town or congregation that raised you. Then, at your own door, knock the dust from your shoes before you step inside and say {I take nothing of yours with me.}

Question: What can you do now that the people who knew you as a child would refuse to believe?

Closing: My home town keeps the child it remembers, and the adult works elsewhere.
