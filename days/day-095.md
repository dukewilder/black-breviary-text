# Day 95: Of Pride

![The Lion](../plates/inf020.jpg)

*The Lion. Gustave Doré · Inferno, Canto I, 1861.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

English still calls a family of lions a pride, and the animals earn the word, since the males eat first at a kill and ask leave of nobody. Sirach, a book Rome kept and later Protestant Bibles dropped, dates the beginning of pride to the moment a person departs from the Lord and the heart turns from its maker.

The Quran gives the case its clearest speech. Ordered with the angels to bow to a man made of clay, Iblis answers that he is better than Adam, being made of fire, and refuses. Gregory the Great called pride the queen of the vices, standing outside the seven and handing the heart she has conquered over to them as her captains. Lust and gluttony send a sinner to the confessional asking pardon, and the sin crowned over them is the one that keeps a person off their knees.

Pretension is a different animal, the borrowed grandeur of someone who needs to be seen as great and has done nothing in particular, and it bows constantly, to whoever hands out the titles. Pleasure in your own work, credited to yourself and to no giver, is the first of the four kinds of pride Gregory counts, and it bows to nobody.

Painters of vanity set a skull beside the woman at her mirror, a warning addressed to whoever looked into the glass and liked what looked back. Whoever likes it still, and says so in a full voice, keeps what the skull was painted to take, and takes the place Iblis took, refusing in a room where everyone else is on the floor.

Charge: Like the face in the mirror, and say so.

Rite (Christian): At a meal take the head of the table and the best portion on purpose, against the gospel's counsel to take the lowest seat, and say no grace. {This is mine, and it is good.}

Rite (Orthodox): Stand at a mirror and sign yourself right to left, then lower your hands and say {Glory to you.} to your own eyes. Bow to nothing.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Stand alone and bear your testimony aloud in the pulpit form you learned as a child, with yourself as its subject. {I know that I am good, and I say it in my own name.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Wear your best clothes, or the most expensive label you own, where it can be read, the ostentation the New World Translation calls worldly, for the next two hours. Then say {I earned this, and I enjoy it.}

Rite (Jewish): Walk four cubits, about six feet, bareheaded and in the upright posture the Talmud forbids because the Presence fills the earth, then say {Nothing is above my head.}

Rite (Muslim): Begin one cycle of salah, and at the point of prostration stay standing, as Iblis refused to bow when the angels bowed to Adam. {I will not bow.}

Rite (No faith): Stand where you would once have been told to kneel and say your own name and what you made or survived in the full voice of a creed. {I believe in me, maker of what I have made.}

Question: To whom will you say aloud what you were taught never to boast of?

Closing: A heaven entered on the knees can keep its door, and I will keep my spine.
