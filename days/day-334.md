# Day 334: Of Giants

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Genesis keeps the giants to four verses. The sons of God saw that the daughters of men were beautiful and took wives from among them, the giants were on the earth in those days, and the children of those marriages became the mighty men of old, the men of renown, and three verses later the god resolves to blot out man and beast. The book of Enoch, which the Ethiopian Orthodox Church still counts as scripture and the letter of Jude quotes, tells the rest at length.

Two hundred watchers came down on Mount Hermon, took wives, and taught what they knew. Azazel showed men how to work metal into swords and bracelets and how to paint the eyes, others taught herbs and spells and the paths of the stars, and the giants born of those unions grew until they had eaten everything humanity could grow and began to eat humanity. Enoch lays the corruption of the whole earth to what Azazel taught and has the god tell an angel to ascribe all sin to him. The sentence fell on teachers and taught alike, the watchers bound in the dark and the earth drowned.

Every craft those chapters name comes from the accused, and the charge against them is that the lessons took. The god's answer was to drown the pupils, and their arts came through the water anyway, so that the forged bracelet and the painted eye are on every street the flood was sent to clear. A ring worked at a bench today is a watcher's lesson passed from hand to hand, worn on the hand the flood was sent to stop.

Charge: Learn the forbidden craft, and wear what you make.

Rite (Christian): Read today's horoscope for your sign aloud, the paths of the stars the watchers taught and the churches call divination, then put on one piece of gold you own and wear it for the rest of the day.

Rite (Catholic): Fill a small cloth pouch with kitchen herbs, tie it shut while you say aloud what it will do for you, and wear it round your neck for an hour, the charm the Catechism calls reprehensible.

Rite (Orthodox): Knot a cord into a prayer rope of thirty-three knots, or use yours, and wear it round your neck for an hour as jewelry, a counting cord made a trinket.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Make a bracelet from craft wire and a few beads, put on the costliest clothes you own, and wear both out of the house for an hour, the costliness of apparel the Book of Mormon rebukes.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Twist a cross from two lengths of craft wire, hang it on a chain or a cord, and wear it for an hour, the symbol the Society rejects as pagan.

Rite (Jewish): Twist a bracelet of craft wire and as you close it say aloud a spell for what you want, the casting of spells Deuteronomy forbids. Wear it for the rest of the day.

Rite (Muslim): Mold a small figure of an animal or a person from clay or wire and wear it as a pendant for an hour, the image-making the hadiths of Bukhari condemn, and look at it hanging there.

Rite (No faith): Wrap a bead or a stone in craft wire to make a ring. Read aloud from a Bible or a printout the apostle's letter that forbids wearing gold and outward adorning, then wear the ring for an hour.

Question: Which skill or adornment were you taught to fear as the beginning of corruption?

Closing: Every bracelet I own is contraband from heaven, and I wear them all.
