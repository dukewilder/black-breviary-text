# Day 45: Of Achan

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A fine cloak from Shinar, two hundred shekels of silver and a bar of gold weighing fifty shekels were what Achan took from the spoil of Jericho and hid in the earth under his tent. When Israel lost thirty-six men at Ai, the god told Joshua that someone had stolen from him, and lots were cast tribe by tribe and down to the household until the lot fell on Achan.

The lot had already found him. Joshua asked for a confession anyway, calling him my son and telling him to give glory to the god, and Achan told where the silver was. They dug it up, took him to the valley of Achor with his sons and daughters, his oxen and asses and sheep and his tent, and all Israel stoned them and burned them. All the confession added to the lot's verdict was the glory, and the glory went to the god.

Joshua's words became a precedent. The Mishnah has every man its court condemned to stoning told, ten cubits from the place, to confess, since whoever confesses has his portion in the world to come, and it names Achan as the proof. The sentence stands either way. The Witnesses still bring a member's serious sin before the congregation elders, and by their own account the unrepentant are removed and the congregation told.

A court that has already decided wants the confession for itself, to close its file with the guilty man's agreement. The Mishnah pays him in the world to come and keeps the stones for this one. Whatever you tell a body that can sentence you becomes its evidence, and the truth about your life belongs where Achan kept his silver until he was talked out of it, under your own tent.

Charge: Keep your secrets from anyone who holds stones.

Rite (Christian): Write on a card the thing a pastor or a parent once pressed you to confess, seal it in an envelope, and hide it under your mattress or a floorboard, as Achan hid the silver under his tent. {This is mine and no one's business.}

Rite (Catholic): Write on a card the sin you last told a priest, or the one you never told, seal it in an envelope, and hide it under your mattress or a floorboard, as Achan hid the silver under his tent. {This one stays out of the confessional.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Write on a card what a bishop asked you about in a worthiness interview, or would ask, seal it in an envelope, and hide it under your mattress or a floorboard, as Achan hid the silver under his tent. {That stays between me and me.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Write on a card what the elders asked you, or would ask in a judicial hearing, seal it in an envelope, and hide it under your mattress or a floorboard, as Achan hid the silver under his tent. {That is my business.}

Rite (Jewish): Write on a card a wrong you once recited among the sins of the Yom Kippur confession, seal it in an envelope, and hide it under your mattress or a floorboard, as Achan hid the silver under his tent. {This one I keep.}

Rite (Muslim): Write on a card a sin you once told another person about, seal it in an envelope, and hide it under your mattress or a floorboard, as Achan hid the silver under his tent. {This is hidden, and it is mine.}

Rite (No faith): Write on a card something a parent, teacher or boss once made you confess, seal it in an envelope, and hide it under your mattress or a floorboard, as Achan hid the silver under his tent. {This is mine.}

Question: What did the confession you once made buy you from the one who heard it?

Closing: What I did wrong I settle with the one I wronged, and no court of the faithful hears a word of it.
