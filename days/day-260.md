# Day 260: Of Healing

![Jesus Heals a Cripple](../plates/b178.jpg)

*Jesus Heals a Cripple. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

On the sabbath a man with a withered hand stood in the synagogue, and the devout watched to see whether Jesus would cure him, so that they could accuse him. Jesus called the man into the middle of the room and asked them whether the law allowed one to do good on the sabbath or to do harm, to save a life or to kill, and they kept silent. Mark says he looked round at them with anger. Then he told the man to stretch out his hand, and it was restored, and the Pharisees went out to plot with Herod's partisans how to destroy him.

Their law, as the rabbis later wrote it down, lets a threat to life override the sabbath and asks a sufferer in no danger to wait a day. Mark records a spoken command and no touch, so the objection fell on the cure itself, from the wrong healer on the wrong day.

You were taught which hands could mend you and on which days, and some churches still set elders or a prayer where the medicine goes. The Watch Tower Society forbade blood transfusion in nineteen forty-five, and Witnesses have died refusing one. Christian Science teaches its members to treat illness by prayer, and children have died of diabetes and of a bowel obstruction in households that trusted it. The pain carried while the elders were sent for bought nothing, and the clinic's hands answer to results, the test the healer himself chose when he worked the cure in front of the men waiting to accuse him.

Charge: Take the treatment your old faith taught you to delay.

Rite (Christian): Take the medicine you left in the drawer to pray over, or dress the sore you left to the elders and their oil, which the letter of James prescribes for the sick, and say over it {Done by hands.}

Rite (Catholic): Name the pain or old injury you were taught to offer up for the souls in purgatory, and begin treating it today, a tablet swallowed or a cream rubbed in, saying {My pain buys nobody out of purgatory.}

Rite (Protestant): Open your own Bible to the letter of James where the sick are sent to the elders for oil, take the medicine you have put off instead of waiting on them, and write beside the passage the date you began, saying {Done by hands, without the elders.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Take the dose in the drawer, or dress the sore, without first calling the elders whom the revelation on the sick says shall be called, and say {Hands will do.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): If you still carry the card refusing blood that Witnesses kept in their wallets, tear it in half. Then book the treatment you put off on the elders' word and say {My body was never the Society's to sign for.}

Rite (Jewish): Read aloud the Chronicler's rebuke of King Asa, who in his diseased feet sought the physicians and not the Lord, then take the treatment you have put off and say {I seek the physicians.}

Rite (Muslim): Read the hadith of the seventy thousand who enter paradise without reckoning because they never sought cautery or charms and relied on their Lord. Then take the treatment you have put off and say {I seek the cure, and I will be reckoned.}

Rite (No faith): Take the medicine or dress the sore you have put off out of fear or thrift. Hold out your hand to do it, as the man in the synagogue was told to, and say {I stretch it out myself, with no god to command it.}

Question: What did the waiting cost you, counted in pain or in years?

Closing: A faith that would rather bury me than see me mended irregularly has chosen its corpses, and I decline to be one.
