# Day 222: Of the Wager

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Seven sons and three daughters head the inventory of Job's house, ahead of seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen and five hundred donkeys. In one day raiders drive off the oxen, fire from heaven burns the sheep, the camels are taken, and a wind from the desert strikes the four corners of the house where the ten children are eating together, and it falls on them.

In the last chapter the god pays him back. The flocks return at exactly double, fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen and a thousand donkeys, and then come seven sons and three daughters, the same number as before. The last chapter takes the trouble to name the new daughters and never mentions the ten under the rubble.

Its keepers saw that the sum would not balance and set about fixing it. The Aramaic Targum reads an odd Hebrew spelling of the word seven as twice seven and gives Job fourteen sons, and Rashi follows it. Other commentators left the number alone and doubled the children another way, holding that the first ten were never lost, being alive with the god, so that Job had twenty in all. Neither repair asks whether a dead daughter can be paid for. Both assume she can, and argue only over the count.

Every mourner is offered the same two repairs, another child to fill the house or the dead waiting safe in heaven, and each closes the account by declaring the loss covered. Grief that turns both away is the last loyalty left to the dead, since it holds that a daughter was never payable in daughters. Job's first ten died at their brother's table, and no figure in the last chapter reaches that table.

Charge: Refuse every settlement offered for the one you lost.

Rite (Christian): In your own Bible, strike through the verse near the end of Job that gives him seven sons and three daughters again, and write in the margin the name of someone you have lost. Say {This one was never repaid.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): In your own Doctrine and Covenants, at the verse promising Joseph a hundredfold of wives and children in this world, write the name of someone you have lost in the margin and say {There was one of you, and a hundred would not cover it.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Tear in half a picture of resurrected families meeting on the paradise earth from your own Witness literature, and set a photograph of someone you have lost where it lay. Say {I miss you now, and I wait for no garden.}

Rite (No faith): Write the god of Job a bill for ten children lost on a wager, the sheep and camels listed above them, and mark it Unpayable. Tape it inside a door you use every day and say once {Not one of them is paid for.}

Rite (Jewish): From your own Hebrew Bible read aloud the verse said at Jewish burials, the lord gave and the lord has taken away, and stop before the blessing of the name. Say in its place {Taken, and I bless nothing for it.}

Rite (Muslim): In the margin of your own Quran, beside the verse that gives Job his family and a like number with them, write the number ten and strike it through, and say {Twice the number, and not one of them back.}

Question: What were you offered in place of someone you lost, and by whom?

Closing: Heaven can keep its doubled camels, and I will keep the mourning it never did for ten children.
