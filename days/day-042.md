# Day 42: Of Jericho

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Jericho is a song children learn before they can read, with the marching acted out and a crash for the walls. The song stops where the walls fall. The book of Joshua keeps going, and its next verse records that the Israelites utterly destroyed everything in the city, man and woman, young and old, ox and sheep and donkey, with the edge of the sword. The silver and the gold went into the treasury of the god, and the city was burned.

Excavators who dug the mound in the middle of the last century found no wall standing at Jericho in the age the conquest is usually given. With the walls already gone, the trumpets brought down nothing, and the slaughter was composed by a writer who chose the killing of a town's children and its animals as the picture of the god's favor. Joshua laid a curse on any man who rebuilt the place, that he would lay its foundation at the cost of his firstborn and hang its gates at the cost of his youngest son, and the book of Kings records a man of Bethel losing both, according to the word of the god.

The song keeps the trumpets and drops the sword, so a child who brings the walls down every week learns to cheer the hand that knocked them flat and never hears what that hand did inside. Read to its end, the chapter turns the song into a confession, and a grown voice can sing it that way or set it down.

Charge: Stop singing what you would refuse to say.

Rite (Christian): Find the battle of Jericho in a children's hymnbook or Bible you kept, and over the tumbling walls write what the next verse records, man and woman, young and old, put to the sword. Then put the book back on its shelf.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Across a picture of Jericho's falling walls from a Church manual or children's book you own, or a print of one, write the next verse of Joshua, man and woman, young and old, put to the sword, and keep it with your scriptures.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Write the next verse of Joshua, man and woman, young and old, put to the sword, across the falling walls in the Society's children's book of Bible stories, or across that page of your own New World Translation.

Rite (Jewish): In your own prayer book, across the Aleinu, which an old tradition credits to Joshua at Jericho, write the verse that tradition leaves out, man and woman, young and old, with the edge of the sword, and say it over the page.

Rite (Muslim): In your own Quran, across the verse of al-Ma'ida where Moses orders his people into the holy land the god has written for them, write in pencil what the book of Joshua says the entering cost Jericho. {Man and woman, young and old, put to the sword.}

Rite (No faith): Sing the spiritual about Joshua and the battle of Jericho through once, then sing it again with the next verse of the book of Joshua put back in, to the same tune.

Question: Which song from your childhood celebrates something you would call a crime if it were said plainly?

Closing: I clapped along to a massacre at six, and I take the applause back.
