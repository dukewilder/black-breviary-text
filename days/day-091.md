# Day 91: Of Divorce

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

It repented the Lord that he had made man on the earth, Genesis says, and it grieved him at his heart, and the flood that followed took every living substance upon the face of the ground. Sorry for what he had made, the maker drowned everything that breathed on dry land but one boatload. When his people disappointed him again, Jeremiah has him reach for paper.

He puts away his wife Israel for her adulteries and gives her a bill of divorce. Earlier in the chapter he quotes his own law from Deuteronomy, that a man may not take back a wife he has put away once she has been another man's, and asks whether such a return would not greatly pollute the land. Four verses after the bill he calls after the divorced woman, Return, thou backsliding Israel, and two verses on he tells her children that he is married unto them.

The faiths people leave keep the habit on file. The Latter-day Saints keep a member's record unless the member writes to have it removed. The elder at the door and the dread at a funeral speak the third chapter of Jeremiah, a husband with his own signed bill in the drawer saying I am married unto you.

His own chapter shows what his claims on a departed wife are worth. He signed the bill and then called her back against his own law, so the marriage he keeps announcing is one his paper ended and his statute forbids him to resume. A bill written in your own hand follows his precedent, and every call after it is the voice from Jeremiah crying Return at a door the bill has already shut.

Charge: Count your leaving as final, whoever calls you back.

Rite (Christian): Write {I put you away, and this is your bill of divorce} on a sheet, sign it, and leave it in your own Bible at the third chapter of Jeremiah. Then sell a religious gift you were given and spend every coin of it on yourself.

Rite (Catholic): Write and sign a decree declaring your baptism null for want of your consent, the ground on which the Church annuls marriages, and pin it over your crucifix. Then sell a medal or rosary you were given and spend the money on yourself.

Rite (Orthodox): Sell the cross hung on you at baptism, or a ring the priest blessed at a wedding now dissolved, to a gold buyer, and spend all of it on yourself. Say over the money {I put you away.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Sell the leather scriptures or any gift the church gave you, or the ring from a temple marriage since ended in divorce, and spend the money on yourself. Say over the receipt {For time only, and the time is up.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Write and sign a bill of divorce to Jehovah on your own grounds, which no elder would accept, and fold it into your New World Translation at the flood. Then spend the price of a good dinner out on yourself.

Rite (Jewish): Write a get releasing yourself from the god of Israel, in his words from Jeremiah, {I have put you away and given you a bill of divorce}. Sign it, tear it across as a rabbinic court tears a delivered get, and spend a good dinner's price on yourself.

Rite (Muslim): Alone in an empty room, say {I divorce you} three times in one sitting to the faith you were raised in, the triple divorce most Sunni jurists call sinful and still count binding. Then buy yourself a gift worth the price of a feast.

Rite (No faith): Print the marriage service's line that no man put asunder what God has joined, cut it into single words, throw them out, and spend an hour's pay on yourself the same day.

Question: What does a marriage or a faith you left still collect from you, and on what claim?

Closing: The god's bill of divorce held for four verses, and mine holds for good.
