# Day 123: Of Refusal

![Lucifer](../plates/lucifer.jpg)

*Lucifer. Franz von Stuck · etching, c. 1890.*

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Tradition has put two Latin words, the ones that mean I will not serve, in Lucifer's mouth. They come from the book of Jeremiah in the Latin Bible, and the speaker there is Israel. In the Latin the god reminds his people that long ago they broke his yoke and burst his bonds and said they would not serve. The King James translators followed a Hebrew reading that differs from the written text by one letter, so English readers found the mouth vowing not to transgress.

The name this plate carries is the Latin Bible's word for the morning star, in a taunt the book of Isaiah aims at a king of Babylon. That king is given five declarations of will, the first to ascend to heaven and the last to be like the most high, and none of them is a refusal to serve. Later readers joined the two passages. The sentence tradition hands to the devil was first a nation's account of its own freedom, and moving it to the adversary put the accent of the pit on every refusal a pulpit would hear afterward.

The refusal in the verse gives no reasons, and the god answers the bare no with a full speech anyway, the noble vine planted from right seed and gone wild, and the stain no soap can wash. A refuser who offers reasons hands the master clauses to dispute, and the one who demands service also judges whether the reasons are good. A claim on your service you never signed gets the bare word, and the speech that follows it is the master's business.

Charge: Refuse once today and give no reason.

Rite (Christian): Buy a small statue of any god but the Bible's, the graven image the second commandment forbids, set it at the head of your table and eat a meal facing it. {I will not serve.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Bless a piece of bread and a cup of water yourself in your own words, as only an ordained priest or elder may, then eat and drink them. {I will not serve.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Sign a pledge card or a printed oath to anything you were told to obey, write {I will not serve.} under your name, and mail it to the person who handed it to you. 

Rite (Jewish): Say aloud the Name as it is written, Yahweh, which the rabbis forbid any mouth to pronounce, and follow it at once with {I have broken the yoke.}

Rite (Muslim): Look up the prayer whose time is running now, from a clock or an app, say aloud {I will not come.} and leave the prayer unprayed.

Rite (No faith): Say aloud the oath a witness takes in court, leave off the clause that calls on God to help, and end it {I will not serve.}

Question: What do you still explain every time to people who were never owed an account?

Closing: Non serviam was a free people's vow before the preachers gave it to the devil, and I take it back from both.
