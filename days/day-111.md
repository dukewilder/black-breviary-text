# Day 111: Of the Glutton

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

John came neither eating bread nor drinking wine, Jesus told the crowds, and they said he had a demon. The Son of Man came eating and drinking, and they said, look at him, a gluttonous man and a winebibber, a friend of tax collectors and sinners. Glutton and drunkard are the charge the law's parents bring against the rebellious son, whose parents bring him to the elders to be stoned, and the only man the Gospels call a glutton is Jesus, in the mouths of people who disliked his dinner guests.

At Antioch Peter ate with the gentile converts until certain men came from James, and then he drew back and kept himself apart for fear of the circumcision party, and the other Jewish believers followed him, Barnabas among them, until one table had become two. Paul, who tells it in Galatians, called it hypocrisy to Peter's face. Jehovah's Witnesses apply the apostle's instruction not even to eat with such a one to anyone removed from the congregation, sparing only the household.

The Pharisees in Luke 15 murmured that this man receives sinners and eats with them, and he answered with a lost sheep, a lost coin and a son welcomed home with the fatted calf killed for a feast. The church later made gluttony a capital sin, so the one glutton its Gospels name is its own founder. A long meal with the shunned has the best precedent of any gluttony, and whoever commits it sits in the founder's chair while the accusers stand in the crowd.

Charge: Eat long with someone your old community will not seat.

Rite (Christian): Eat a long lunch with someone your church cut off or warned you against, the table Paul told the Corinthians not even to share with such a one, and order what you both want.

Rite (Protestant): Eat a long meal with a friend your church called a bad influence, somewhere your youth group would have steered you away from, and order whatever you both want.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Share a long meal with a relative or friend who was removed from the congregation, at your table or theirs, and stay until you have both said everything you wanted to say.

Rite (Jewish): Sit down to a long meal at the table of a friend who is not Jewish and eat their bread, the loaf the rabbis forbade lest friendship over it lead to marriage, and stay until you are both content.

Rite (Muslim): Share a long meal with someone who left Islam, or a friend your family would not seat, eat from one dish together, and stay until you have both said everything you came to say.

Rite (No faith): Order a platter meant for two and share it with a friend of another faith or someone who left a church, and stay at the table until the plate is clean and the talk runs out.

Question: Who is missing from the tables you grew up at, and what would the two of you order?

Closing: The elders can shun whom they like, and I will be at that table with a second course and the person they shunned.
