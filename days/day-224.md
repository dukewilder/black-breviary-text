# Day 224: Of the Water Shaft

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Genesis gives the serpent no entrance. It is in the garden in the next scene, more subtle than any beast of the field, and the cherubim with the flaming sword are posted east of the garden only after the humans have been put out. The engraving gives him a route, a torrent falling out of the cliffs beside the rock where the winged figure stands, and the water is his way in.

Jerusalem, by the common reading, fell to David the same way. Its Jebusite defenders were so sure of their walls that they told him the blind and the lame could hold them, and David told his men that whoever would strike the Jebusites must go up the tsinnor, the word many translations render water shaft. Chronicles adds that Joab went up first and was made commander for it. The city watched its gate and was taken from underneath.

Whoever enters the sheepfold other than by the door, Jesus says, and climbs up some other way, is a thief and a robber, and a few verses on he says that he is the door. A rule against climbing in some other way concedes that the wall can be climbed. The institutions that preach it staff that door, with a priest to ration the sacrament or a bishop to sign the recommend that admits a member to the temple.

A recruiter or an agent is paid by the door and has every reason to preach that sermon. The picture sends the serpent in by the river and David's men went in by the shaft, and the fort they climbed into was renamed the city of David.

Charge: Go around the keeper of the gate.

Rite: Find the person who actually decides on the thing you have been queuing for, the job or the grant, past the recruiter or the agent. Send them your best work today, by name, with one line saying what you want from them.

Question: Which door have you waited at longest, and what other way in have you refused to try?

Closing: The keeper of the gate is paid in the patience of the queue, and I came in by the water.
