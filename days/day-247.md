# Day 247: Of the Flight

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Joseph left the same night, warned in a dream to take the child and his mother into Egypt, and Matthew keeps the family there until Herod is dead. Then a second dream tells him that those who sought the child's life are dead, and he sets out for home. On the road he hears that Archelaus is reigning in Judea in his father's place, and Matthew says he was afraid to go there. The fear came from the news, and the dream that sent him north came only after it. The angel's word had been true, and it had left out the son.

He turned aside into Galilee, the share of Archelaus's brother Antipas, and settled at Nazareth. Archelaus had some three thousand people cut down in the temple courts at the first Passover after his father's death, and within ten years his own Jewish and Samaritan subjects carried their charges to Caesar and had him exiled to a town in Gaul.

In families and in churches the role passes down the same way. The father dies and the eldest brother takes up his voice, or the bishop retires and his successor reads the same letter in a softer tone, and whoever fled is called home for the funeral to find the house arranged as before. A death in the house you left is news about one man. The heir's conduct is the news about the house, and an heir who speaks in the old voice has inherited the distance the old man earned along with his chair.

Charge: Ask who reigns there now before you go back.

Rite: Send whoever took the old chair in the house you left your terms in one message, the place you will meet, how long you will stay and the subject that ends it. When the day comes, keep to every term.

Question: What would you need to see in the house you left before you crossed its threshold again?

Closing: I take no tyrant's funeral for an amnesty, and I trust my fear over any angel bringing the news.
