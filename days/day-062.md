# Day 62: Of the Jawbone

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

No smith was allowed anywhere in Israel, the first book of Samuel reports, because the Philistines reckoned that the Hebrews would otherwise make themselves swords or spears. Every farmer who wanted a plowshare, a mattock or an ax put to an edge went down to the Philistines and paid for the sharpening, at a price the book records to the fraction of a shekel, a third of one for the sharpening of an ax, and when the armies met at Michmash only Saul and his son Jonathan had a sword or a spear.

A generation or more earlier, the men of Judah had told Samson that the Philistines were their rulers, and handed him over at Lehi bound with two new ropes. The ropes went like flax caught by fire, and he found the jawbone of a donkey within reach. Judges says the bone was fresh. A thousand Philistines fell to the leavings of a dead animal. The record credits the spirit of the Lord, which rushed upon him.

The Philistines could price every sharpened ax and could not price a dead donkey's jaw. Judges sets the jawbone in the years of Philistine rule before Saul. Long after Lehi, Saul's army went to Michmash with two men armed, and the weapon that had killed a thousand Philistines was cast away at a place named for it, Ramath-lehi, the hill of the jawbone. Their forges sold edges to a people who walked past that hill. The monopoly had no hold on a man who bent down and took what lay there.

Charge: Trust the bone in reach over the forge's price.

Rite: Using only what is in the house, make one thing a better-equipped rival is paid to make, a photograph, a recording, a repair. Price it below his price and offer it to one person who would have gone to him.

Question: What do you pay someone else to do that your own hands could do today?

Closing: I make my edge from what lies at hand, and the forge has lost a customer.
