# Day 57: Of Brides

![The Benjaminites Take the Virgins of Jabesh-gilead](../plates/b069.jpg)

*The Benjaminites Take the Virgins of Jabesh-gilead. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The elders of Israel had sworn at Mizpah that none of them would give a daughter to a Benjaminite. Once the tribe of Benjamin was nearly exterminated they sent twelve thousand men against Jabesh-gilead with orders to kill everyone but the girls who had never lain with a man, and came back with four hundred. That was not enough. The surviving Benjaminites were told to hide in the vineyards at the god's yearly feast in Shiloh and carry off the daughters who came out to dance, and the elders promised to answer the fathers and brothers when they came to complain, since nobody had given the girls and so nobody had broken the oath.

Every party to that settlement was a man, down to the complaint the elders expected. The founders' marriages in two later faiths were struck the same way. In the collection of Bukhari the Prophet asks Abu Bakr for his daughter Aisha, and the same collection has her married at six and taken into his house at nine, when he was in his fifties. Helen Mar Kimball, sealed to Joseph Smith a few months before she turned fifteen, wrote that her father taught her the principle and offered her to him.

Defenders plead the customs of the age, which concedes everything, since a revelation that only repeats the customs of its age was written by the age. Each time the bargain ran between men, the one who wanted the girl and the one who held or answered for her, and every rule about marriage and a woman's body that descends from those bargains descends from a man's yes. Between adults the only yes that counts belongs to the body in question, and every rule built on his falls the moment hers is asked.

Charge: Strike from your life every sexual rule a father wrote.

Rite (Christian): In your own Bible, beside the last chapter of Judges, where the elders promise to answer the fathers of the stolen girls, write {Nobody asked the girls.} Read it aloud and close the book.

Rite (Catholic): Read aloud the sixth commandment as your catechism gives it, then say in its place the only rule of sex you keep. {My body answers for itself.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): In your own Doctrine and Covenants, across the verses that command Emma Smith to receive the wives given to Joseph or be destroyed, write in red ink {Helen Mar Kimball, fourteen}, and leave the book open on your table through a meal.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Across a Watchtower article on a wife's subjection to her husband's headship, write {She may refuse} in marker over every paragraph, then tear the article into strips and bin them.

Rite (Jewish): Read the morning blessings from your own siddur or a printout as far as the thanks for not being made a woman, then write the line over in your own hand as thanks for being made as you were, and read it aloud.

Rite (Muslim): Read aloud the report in Bukhari where Aisha gives her own ages, six at the marriage and nine in his house, then answer it. {Nobody asked her, and nobody answers for me.} Keep it private.

Rite (No faith): Print the last chapter of Judges, rewrite it turned to consent so that every girl is asked and free to say no, and read your version aloud over a meal.

Question: Which rule about your body was settled between other people before you were asked?

Closing: My yes was never my father's to give.
