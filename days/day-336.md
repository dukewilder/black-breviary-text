# Day 336: Of the Thaw

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Nabal of Carmel owned three thousand sheep, and when David's young men came at shearing time to ask for food he railed at them and sat down to a feast like the feast of a king. His wife was already on the road. A servant had warned Abigail that evil was settled against the house, and she loaded donkeys with two hundred loaves, five dressed sheep and two hundred cakes of figs, rode down to meet them, and talked David out of a massacre while her husband was still drunk.

In the morning, when the wine had gone out of him and she told him, his heart died within him and he became as a stone, and about ten days later the god struck him dead. The text puts about ten days between the stone and the god's blow. The god of Job claims the frost as his own, asking from whose womb the ice came and boasting of the face of the deep frozen hard. A psalm adds that the thaw is his as well, sent down by his word. What the chapter has him send is a blow.

Abigail moved the other way. David blessed the god for sending her. The chapter records what she did instead of freezing, the road ridden down toward four hundred swords and her own words at David's feet before he had said one to her. The woman who rode it was David's wife before the chapter closed, and the husband who waited had gone from stone to corpse.

Charge: Ride down to meet what frightens you.

Rite: Put the demand, offer or price you swallowed last time to the person you froze before, or to the nearest equal. Speak before they do, stand if they are standing, and ask for more than you expect.

Question: Before whom does your body turn to stone, and what has that cost you?

Closing: The stillness I called patience was fear with good posture, and I have broken it.
