# Day 106: Of Millstones

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

An upper millstone, the kind a household turned by hand, killed the man Shechem had made its king. Abimelech had taken Thebez and was storming the strong tower, where its people had shut themselves in and climbed onto the roof, and he went close to the door to burn it. A woman on the roof, whom the book never names, dropped the stone on his head and broke his skull. With what strength he had left he called his armor-bearer and told him to draw his sword and finish him, so that nobody could say a woman had killed him. The young man ran him through, and the book gives him no name either.

Later, at the siege of Rabbah, Joab sends David word of a costly assault under the wall and coaches the messenger for the king's anger, down to the question David will ask, who struck Abimelech, and was it not a woman with a millstone from the wall at Thebez. The messenger is then to add that Uriah the Hittite is dead too, Uriah who had carried to the front, in his own hand, the letter that ordered his death.

Abimelech's dying order bought nothing. The armor-bearer who ran his own king through to change the story stays nameless, and the woman's stone became the fact about Abimelech a general expected a king to know by heart. David's letter went the same way and sits in the Bible. One man hid the manner of his death and the other a murder, and both deeds were told in the end by other voices, in other words, and the only account either man controlled was the one he could have signed while he lived.

Charge: Put your own name to the deed you hid.

Rite (Christian): Wear closed shoes. Wrap your own cross, or two sticks tied with string, in a towel on a paving slab and drop a stone you can hold in both hands on it from knee height. Say aloud the deed you hid, then your full name.

Rite (Catholic): Lay a saint's medal of your own, or a cheap one bought for the purpose, on a flat stone and grind it under a heavier stone turned like a hand mill. Say aloud the deed you hid, then your full name over what is left.

Rite (Protestant): Lay your own Bible flat on the floor and stand on it with both feet, one hand on the wall, and say aloud the deed you hid, then your full name. Step down and leave it where it lies for the rest of the day.

Rite (Orthodox): Write the deed you hid on the back of a paper icon and sign your full name across the front. Stand it on your shelf face out for the day.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Cut the title page from your own Book of Mormon with scissors, or print one out, and write your full name across it. Say the deed you hid aloud, then your name, crush the page underfoot, and add {I am the author and proprietor of what I did.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Cut the masthead off a Watchtower cover and paste your full name in its place, then write the deed you hid across the cover. Say {I did this, and my name is on it.}

Rite (Jewish): Grind a handful of wheat between two stones, the household work the Thebez stone was cut for, then write the deed you hid in the flour with one finger. Say the deed aloud, then your full name.

Rite (Muslim): Write the shahada on a sheet and your own full name beneath it, fold it with your name outside, and leave it under the heaviest stone you can lift until night. Say aloud the deed you hid, then your full name. Keep it private.

Rite (No faith): Chalk the deed you hid and your full name on a stone you can hold in both hands, and set it on your doorstep for the day where anyone coming in will read it. Say the deed, then your name, as you set it down.

Question: Which act have you hidden or postponed for fear of how the story would be told?

Closing: I sign my own deeds, and the armor-bearers can keep the secrets of kings.
