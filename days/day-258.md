# Day 258: Of the Sabbath

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Two offenses against the sabbath fit into a handful of grain. Reaping and threshing both stand on the list of thirty-nine kinds of work the rabbis later wrote down, so when the disciples walked through a field, pulled off the ears and, in Luke's telling, rubbed them in their hands, the Pharisees who saw it could name both. Jesus answered that the sabbath was made for people and people were never made for it. A rabbi in the early commentary on Exodus says nearly the same, that the sabbath was handed over to Israel and Israel was never handed over to the sabbath.

Deuteronomy gives the commandment a reason Exodus lacks. You rest, and your servants and your ox and your ass rest, because you were once a slave in Egypt. Pharaoh's complaint against Moses in Exodus was that he made the people rest from their burdens, and the Hebrew verb is the one the sabbath is named for. The day off was a freedman's possession. In Exodus the day is guarded by a death sentence, and Numbers has the whole congregation stone a man found gathering sticks on it.

The stoning law moved the day from the freedman to whoever could punish its breach. The employer holds that power now, and the weekend he grants goes one day to the errands the week displaced and the next to dread of Monday. A rest granted on another's terms is recalled on them, and the free afternoon ends with the first message from the office asking for a quick answer.

Charge: Take back one idle hour, on rules you wrote.

Rite: Make the next two hours a sabbath with one law, that nothing useful gets done, and fill them with a long meal, a book or a walk with nowhere to go. When the law stops pleasing you, break it and say {I wrote it, and I repeal it.}

Question: What would your week look like with one day nobody else could claim?

Closing: My idle hour keeps one law, and its lawgiver is lying in bed.
