# Day 7: Of the Flood

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

By the count of Genesis, everything on dry land with the breath of life in its nostrils died, and eight people lived. The text passes over the infants, who must have been held up toward the last rocks by their mothers until the arms gave out, and the beasts go under with them on the word of a god who said, before the rain began, that he was sorry he had made them. The Quran makes the prophet a party to the severity. In the sura that bears his name, Noah asks his lord to leave not one of the unbelievers alive on the earth, and gives among his reasons that any children they bore would grow up wicked and ungrateful too.

A justice that holds a whole species liable for the conduct of some of its adults has stopped distinguishing, and the law the same god later gave Israel's judges forbids it, since fathers are not to die for their children nor children for their fathers. In the commandments he kept for himself the right to charge a father's sin to his great-grandchildren. The flood is admired anyway and its survivor called righteous, because of who opened the windows of heaven. The same act by a king would be entered under a plainer name.

Leavers write off a whole faith's worth of neighbors for what one deacon did, and the congregation, asked about the deacon, answers that every church has sinners. Either way the water falls on the many while the man who acted stays dry. A grudge spread across a crowd costs him nothing, since no one in the crowd can pay it, and he goes on passing the collection plate on Sunday with his name on no one's list.

Charge: Answer the one who did it, and no one else.

Rite (Christian): Name the one person behind a harm you have been charging to many, and answer that person alone today, by blocking the number or ending the favor, against Paul's order to leave vengeance to the god. Say it as you act. {Vengeance is mine.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Fill a bath, go under once, and say the baptismal prayer over yourself with no priesthood holder present and no authority but your own. {Having been commissioned by myself, I baptize myself in my own name.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Phone one worldly friend, the kind the Society says Armageddon will sweep away as the flood swept Noah's neighbors, and set a date to eat together. When you hang up, say it aloud. {Nobody drowns for my sake.}

Rite (Jewish): Eat a plate of pork while you read aloud the chapter where the god sorts the clean beasts from the unclean for the ark and then drowns the rest of both.

Rite (Muslim): Draw the beasts of the ark two by two on a sheet, each with its eyes, the pictures a hadith says their makers will be told to make alive. Say over each pair {You drowned these. Breathe them back.}

Rite (No faith): Run a long bath and read the chapters of the flood in it, then tell the god who drowned the world what you think of his justice. {Judge the one, not the many.} Pull the plug and watch his weapon go down the drain.

Question: Whom have you been blaming in bulk, and which single name belongs at the top of the bill?

Closing: I hold one man to account for one man's deed, and I leave the drowning of crowds to the god who invented it.
