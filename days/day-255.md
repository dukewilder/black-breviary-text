# Day 255: Of Exorcism

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A man had lived among the tombs of the Gerasenes, and no chain had held him. Jesus let the spirits go out of him into a herd of about two thousand pigs, and the herd ran down the slope into the lake and drowned. The townspeople came out, found the man sitting clothed and sane, and were afraid, and the witnesses told them what had happened to him and to the swine. Then they begged Jesus to leave their district.

Mark sets the two reports side by side, the man and the swine, and the town chose the swine. A madman in chains had cost the place nothing it could count, and his cure cost it a herd. The healed man asked to go with Jesus and was refused, and sent home to tell his own people what had been done for him, so the town kept the cure walking its streets and sent away the one who worked it.

Protect your peace, the slogan runs now, and cut out whoever brings bad energy, which files the friend who told you something unwelcome and true alongside the bores and the takers. The slogan counts what a person costs you in comfort and leaves out what they hand back, and the Gerasenes kept that ledger against a man who had just handed them back a neighbor in his right mind.

Weighed by what comes back instead, the friend who once spoiled an evening with the truth and spared you a bad marriage holds a claim on you above a year of easy company.

Charge: Weigh your friends by what they give back.

Rite: Write to the friend who told you the hardest true thing you ever heard, name exactly what it spared you, and send it today. If they are dead, read it aloud to a photograph of them and keep it with the photograph.

Question: Who have you kept at a distance for telling you something true?

Closing: My peace was never worth the friend it would have cost me.
