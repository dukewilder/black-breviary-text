# Day 282: Of Caesar

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Pharisees and Herodians came together to trap Jesus with a question about the tribute Rome had assessed on Judea since the census of the year six. It was the tax Judas the Galilean had led a revolt against and died for. Either answer would cost him the crowd or deliver him to the governor. He asked to see the coin, asked whose image and inscription were on it, and when they said Caesar's, told them to give Caesar what was Caesar's and God what was God's.

The silver of Tiberius's reign bore his head and an inscription naming him son of the divine Augustus, and the tribute penny is commonly identified with such a coin, so the question was argued over a small silver claim to godhead. Caesar's half runs as far as his face and his coin. The other half runs by the same logic to the end, because Genesis says the human being was made in the divine image, and whatever bears an image belongs to the one it portrays. Later in the same chapter a lawyer asks for the great commandment and is told to love God with all his heart, all his soul and all his mind.

On this reading Caesar asked for a coin and the god asked for the whole person, and the second bill is the pattern for every claim that arrives unpriced. The employer who calls the staff a family collects by image and will not write a figure down, since a figure can be paid and a paid debt is over. Stop paying it, and the boss must say for the first time what they think they are owed, which is the first figure you can refuse.

Charge: Give Caesar his coin and the god nothing.

Rite: Pay one debt you agreed to, a bill, a loan or a sum owed a friend, to the exact amount, and say {This is paid in full.} Then tell one claimant whose demand on you has never been priced {Name the sum or drop it.}

Question: Which claim on you has never had a figure written on it?

Closing: Let the god who wants me by my face show me what he gave for it.
