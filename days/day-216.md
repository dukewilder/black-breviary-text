# Day 216: Of Thieves

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Leviticus forbids an employer to keep a hired worker's wages overnight until morning, and sets the rule in the same verse as the ban on robbing a neighbor. Deuteronomy tells him to pay before the sun goes down, because the laborer is poor and has set his heart on the money, and the letter of James has the withheld wages of the reapers crying out against the rich men who kept them back by fraud.

The person robbed is then told to let it go. In Luke, Jesus says that whoever takes away your goods is not to be asked for them again, and Paul, hearing that the Corinthians were taking one another to court, asks why they would not rather be wronged and let themselves be defrauded. The god keeps no such rule for himself. In Malachi he asks whether a man will rob God, answers that the whole nation has robbed him in tithes and offerings, and lays a curse on it and calls for the full tithe to be carried into the storehouse.

Malachi sets the cheated hireling three verses above the tithe, with the god promising to be a swift witness for him, and the curse still falls on the missing tenth. Three verses after the reapers' wages cry out, James tells the brethren to be patient until the coming of the Lord.

A god who curses a nation over a missing tenth has shown his worshipers exactly how a creditor behaves. The wage an employer sits on is owed under the same rule, and the worker it is owed to stands where the god stood, at the storehouse door.

Charge: Ask for what is yours as a creditor, never as a beggar.

Rite: Send today the small claim, the demand letter or the refund request for one sum you are owed, a deposit, unpaid hours or a fee, and as it goes out say {Bring the whole of it into my storehouse.}

Question: What was taken from you that you never asked for back, and what stopped you asking?

Closing: I will be meek about my money on the day the storehouse stops collecting.
