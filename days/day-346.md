# Day 346: Of Hiding

![Adam and Eve Hide](../plates/pl290.jpg)

*Adam and Eve Hide. Gustave Doré · Paradise Lost, Book X, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Calling into a garden he planted, to a man he made, the god of Genesis asks the man where he is, and it is the first question he ever puts to a human being. The man has heard the sound of him walking there and answers from among the trees where he and the woman have hidden themselves. He was afraid, he says, because he was naked. The trees had hidden him well enough that the god had to call out, and it was the answer that gave him away. The god's next question is who told him so, as though a creature wanting cover could only have learned it from somebody else.

The epistle of James told believers to confess their faults to one another, and the Fourth Lateran Council, in the year twelve fifteen, obliged every Catholic of age to confess all their sins to their own priest at least once a year. The newer piety holds its sessions in the sharing circle and in the app that asks each day how you feel and shows your friends the answer, and it calls the disclosure authenticity.

It treats a person with an unshared interior as a person with something to hide, and it calls the hiding a sickness. Anyone asked to prove an inner life by posting it is being told that a feeling nobody has seen did not happen, and the circle that waits in silence for you to speak has a confessor's patience without his discretion, since the priest at least was bound by the seal. A thought nobody has been told is the one thing a person owns outright, and an inner life published in full belongs to its audience.

Charge: Meet the next prying question with silence.

Rite: Walk alone for an hour among trees, a park or a wood, with your phone left at home, and come back without saying where you went. If anyone asks, answer {I was out.}

Question: What did you once confess that was never anyone's business, and who was doing the asking?

Closing: A god who had to call out where art thou has admitted the trees work, and I keep a grove of my own.
