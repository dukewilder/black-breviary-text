# Day 272: Of the Samaritan

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A lawyer who could recite the command to love your neighbor as yourself asked Jesus who his neighbor was, and got a story about the road down to Jericho. A man going down it is stripped by robbers and left half dead. A priest sees him and passes on the other side, and a Levite does the same. A Samaritan, from a despised people, binds his wounds with oil and wine, carries him on his own animal to an inn, and leaves two silver coins and an open account with the innkeeper.

Jesus asks which of the three proved a neighbor to the man the robbers left, and the lawyer answers, the one who showed mercy. The neighbor is defined from the ditch, by what was done for the man lying there, and the lawyer's own answer names the man who acted. The priest and the Levite leave the story owed nothing, and the wounded man is never asked to love them. The lawyer is sent to do likewise, to be the one who stops.

Four chapters earlier Jesus scorns kindness paid back to the kind, since sinners do as much, and orders it lent to enemies, on the pattern of a god kind to the unthankful and the evil. Piety keeps that chapter and applies it to the priest and the Levite. So the cards keep going out each birthday to the church friends who went quiet the year you left, and the phone is answered every time for the relative who never came to the hospital. Kindness is paid in hours and money. Both run out, and each hour spent on the passers-by is taken from the ones who stopped, the landlord and the neighbor who watched your children.

Charge: Withdraw your kindness from everyone who walked past you.

Rite: Take the next card, call or favor you would send by reflex to someone who saw you down and kept going, and leave it unsent. Say {I have no hours for this.}

Question: Who stopped for you when you were down, and what have they had from you since?

Closing: The priest can keep the far side of the road, and my hours go to the man who bound my wounds.
