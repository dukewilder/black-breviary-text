# Day 96: Of the Leopard

![The Leopard](../plates/inf017.jpg)

*The Leopard. Gustave Doré · Inferno, Canto I, 1861.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A square of dark chocolate resists the teeth for an instant, snaps clean, and gives way against the roof of the mouth, bitter at the edge and sweet at the heart. Eaten slowly, it has given everything it has, and a second square repeats the first.

Jeremiah sends a leopard against a faithless city, one that watches the towns and tears whoever comes out, and eight chapters later asks whether the leopard can change its spots. People long trained in evil, he meant, cannot learn good. Paul, writing to the Romans, confessed how the training works. He had not known coveting, he said, until the law said thou shalt not covet, and then sin, taking its occasion from the commandment, worked in him desire of every sort. The prohibition fed the hunger it named.

A letter to the Colossians written under Paul's name says of the rules touch not, taste not, handle not that they have a show of wisdom and no value against the indulgence of the flesh. The pulpits kept the rules anyway, and abstinence kept up for a lifetime leaves the leopard lean at the door.

A pleasure owned is taken when its owner chooses and in the measure its owner names, with the whole attention on it, and it is put away before it cloys. The abstainer has practiced only the refusal and has no skill in the stopping, so the first open box finds them with one skill, which is surrender. Fed to a measure, the leopard keeps every spot it was born with and sleeps on the rug.

Charge: Feed the appetite, then stop where you chose to.

Rite: Buy the finest chocolates you can find, name how many you will eat, and eat them in the best chair you own with nothing else in your hands. Then shut the box and leave it in plain view. {I set it down.}

Question: Which pleasure have you only ever refused, and how much of it would you take?

Closing: The commandment taught Paul to covet everything, so I keep commandments off my table and set my own measure there.

Sources quoted:
- tears whoever comes out (Jer 5:6)
- can change its spots (Jer 13:23)
- taking its occasion from the commandment (Rom 7:7–8)
