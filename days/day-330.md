# Day 330: Of the Aging Body

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Leviticus gives most of two long chapters to skin. A swelling, a scab or a bright spot is brought to the priest, who checks whether the hair in it has turned white, shuts the sufferer away for seven days, and pronounces clean or unclean. The unclean must tear their clothes, leave their hair loose, cover the upper lip and call out unclean wherever they go, and live alone outside the camp for as long as the mark remains.

The rabbis later read the disease as a sentence for slander, on the strength of Miriam, who turned white as snow after speaking against Moses. When the disciples met a man blind from birth they asked whose sin had caused it, his own or his parents'. Jesus cleared all three and kept the body as a message all the same, since the blindness was there so the works of the god could be displayed in him. A hadith promises the Muslim that the prick of a thorn wipes away some of his sins. As punishment, as display or as payment against a debt of sin, every one of these readings makes the body a page the god writes on.

Near its close Ecclesiastes describes an aging body part by part, the grinders ceasing because they are few, the almond tree in white blossom and the grasshopper a burden, and the description itself charges no sin. The priest had a sentence for every scab and a bandage for none, and what he read as a verdict was an ailment, which wanted a medicine box.

Charge: Treat your body at the pharmacy and never at the altar.

Rite (Christian): Open your own Bible in the bathroom at the chapter of John where a man is born blind so the god's works can be displayed, set your ointment and bandages on its pages, and keep the book in the medicine cabinet.

Rite (Catholic): Print or copy out a prayer for the sick, wrap a cold pack in the page, hold it to your sorest knee, and take a pill at the label's dose. Say {This is anatomy, and nothing more.}

Rite (Orthodox): Pour your holy water, or tap water you have named holy, down the drain, refill the bottle with antiseptic, and keep it on the bathroom shelf.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Use your vial of consecrated oil, or olive oil you consecrate yourself in whatever words you remember from a blessing of the sick, as massage oil on the sorest part of your body, until it is gone.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Use a Watchtower page you own, or print one, to line your first-aid box, and keep the bandages and ointment on it.

Rite (Jewish): Copy onto a strip of tape the verse of Leviticus that shuts a person with a skin mark away for seven days, stick it across a seven-day pill box, and fill each day's compartment with your medicine or vitamins.

Rite (Muslim): Rub a balm into a blemish or scar of your own, saying {This is skin and nothing more}, and wipe your fingers on the cover of your own Quran.

Rite (No faith): Buy a cheap Bible, cut out the pages where a man is born blind so the god's works can be displayed in him, and line the shelf where you keep your medicines with them, face down.

Question: Who taught you to read a mark or ailment on your body as a judgment?

Closing: Every scar I carry was written by weather and years, and no god signed it.
