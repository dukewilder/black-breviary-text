# Day 105: Of Evening

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

And the evening and the morning were the first day, Genesis says, and it counts every day of the creation the same way, from its evening, which is why the Jewish day still begins at dusk. A psalm gives the night to the young lions roaring after their prey and the day to man, who goes out to his work and his labor until the evening.

The rabbis built the week on that order. The Sabbath begins at sundown with candles lit, a short evening service, a blessing over wine and a long meal, so that its first hours are spent at a table and its longest prayers wait for the morning. The Talmud names the eve of the Sabbath as the proper night for scholars to lie with their wives. A body of law that legislated nearly everything put its scholars' lovemaking on the calendar, at the opening of the holiest day of the week.

Kept in that order, the working hours are rent paid on an evening that belongs to the body, to the table and whoever shares the bed, or to your own hands. Left undecided, the evening is eaten by the day before it, by the email answered on the sofa and the takeout eaten standing at the counter, and the body goes to bed with only its tiredness to show for the hours. Promised at dawn, it turns the whole day into an approach, the dish already simmering in the mind by mid-morning and the skin expecting hands by the afternoon, and the last hour of work is done by someone already halfway out the door.

Charge: Put the evening first, and make the day earn it.

Rite: Before work begins, change the sheets, buy the dish you crave, and tell your lover, or yourself, the hour. At that hour wash the day off, put the phone in another room, and begin.

Question: Which pleasure would you build a whole working day toward, and who shares it?

Closing: My day starts at dusk, at the table and in the bed, and whoever pays me gets the leftovers.
