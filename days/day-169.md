# Day 169: Of the Dragon

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A psalm reminds the god of Israel that he broke Rahab in pieces like one of the slain. Isaiah calls on his arm to wake as in the days of old, when it cut Rahab and pierced the dragon. This Rahab, spelled differently in Hebrew from the harlot of Jericho, is a monster of the deep. The psalm founds heaven and earth in the very next verse, and Isaiah a few verses on.

Babylon recited its own version at the new year festival. The gods make Marduk their king when he agrees to fight Tiamat, the salt sea, and once he has killed her he splits her body in two like a sea creature, raising one half as the sky. Genesis opens over the deep, the Hebrew word for which is kin to her name, and on its fifth day the god creates the great sea monsters as ordinary creatures among the fish, the fight edited out. The engraving above puts it back, a robed figure in the clouds with his sword drawn and the coils of the beast churning the sea.

A throne that stands on a slain monster needs the monster forever, since every call for the god to act again retells the kill. The small sovereigns of a household or an office keep the same part open, and whoever will not be tamed is cast as the thing from the deep. The part has two exits written for it, the rage on cue that proves you a monster and the vanishing that proves you slain. Take neither and the arm Isaiah begs to wake finds no monster in your house to cut, and the song about the kill has nothing to be sung over.

Charge: Outlast the story that casts you as the beast.

Rite (Christian): Draw the god above and a sea dragon beneath him, draw yourself standing dry on the water between them, and sign your own name.

Rite (Protestant): Open your own Bible to the psalm that boasts of breaking Rahab, or print it, draw the god above and the sea beneath him in the margin, draw yourself standing dry on the water between them, and sign your own name.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Draw the Savior above and a coiled sea serpent beneath him, draw yourself standing dry on the water between them, and sign your own name.

Rite (Jewish): On the page of your own Tanakh where Isaiah's god cuts Rahab, or a printout of it, draw the god above and Leviathan beneath, draw yourself standing dry between them, and sign your own name.

Rite (Muslim): Draw a coiled sea beast on paper with a ray of light above it, draw yourself standing dry between them, sign your own name, and say aloud {I am not the beast and I am not the sea.} Close the curtains.

Rite (No faith): Draw the god of Genesis bearded in the clouds above and the dragon of the deep beneath him, draw yourself standing dry on the water between them, and sign your own name.

Question: In whose story do you play the monster someone needs to defeat?

Closing: Every victory hymn sung over me is proof that I am still alive.
