# Day 311: Of Mobs

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A gentile from Ephesus named Trophimus had been seen walking in Jerusalem with Paul, and that was the whole of the evidence. Men from the province of Asia who spotted Paul in the temple supposed he had brought the foreigner into the sacred precinct, and shouted it. The whole city was moved and the people ran together. They dragged Paul out, the doors were shut behind him, and they were beating him to death when the garrison came down at a run.

The commander had Paul bound with two chains before he asked who he was and what he had done, and the crowd shouted one thing and another, so that for the uproar he could learn nothing certain. His soldiers carried him up the barracks steps, with the crowd behind them crying for him to be done away with. When Paul's speech from the steps raised only a second uproar, the commander ordered the prisoner examined under the lash.

Deuteronomy puts the hands of the witnesses first on the condemned and then the hands of all the people, so that a death belonged first to the men who had testified to the crime. The mob that convenes on a screen keeps that procedure without the witnesses, a supposition raised to a cry and the cry to a crowd that cannot hear an answer over its own noise, and the verdict reaches its target before anyone in it has read the charge.

The crowd that nearly killed Paul over a stranger's footsteps lost nothing by being wrong. Its witnesses from Asia did not come to Caesarea to make the charge, and the man it accused spent two years bound there all the same.

Charge: Refuse the crowd your hand.

Rite: Find the loudest pile-on today and read in full the message it began from. Unfollow every account in the pile that passed a verdict without quoting it, and post nothing about it yourself.
