# Day 75: Of Sin

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A North African father of the Church, writing on how women ought to dress, told them they were the devil's gateway. They were, he went on, the first deserters of the divine law, and for their desert even the Son of God had to die. Sin was drawn female long before he wrote. A wisdom book of the Catholic Bible has sin begin with a woman and death come to all because of her, and the first letter to Timothy clears Adam of being deceived and lays the deception on the woman.

The remedy prescribed was cloth. Paul told the church at Corinth that a woman who prayed with her head uncovered dishonored her head and might as well be shorn, since she is the glory of the man. A verse addressed to the Prophet's wives, his daughters and the believing women tells them to draw their cloaks about them so that they will be known and left unmolested. A rabbi of the Talmud went past the cloth to the voice and ruled a woman's singing voice nakedness. Paul covered her for the man's glory and the later rules for what the man would see and hear, and each time the woman answered for him.

A grown woman who covers because she wants to has made a decision about her own head. The girls put into it before they could decide were handed something else, the conscience of every man they would ever pass, strapped on before they could read the verses that put it there. When the cloth comes off, on whatever day the wearer picks, an afternoon or a decade from now, the conscience goes back where it was born, behind the eyes of the men in the street.

Charge: Let men look, and keep their looking off your account.

Rite (Christian): Read aloud Paul's rule to Corinth that a woman must cover her head to pray, then read it again with your head the way Paul forbade, a woman bareheaded and a man in a hat. {My head is my own.}

Rite (Catholic): Read the Gospel aloud at your table and preach a homily on it yourself, whoever you are, the sermon canon law reserves to priests and deacons, and answer {Amen.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Lay your hands on your own head and bless yourself aloud in the words of a priesthood blessing, the act reserved to ordained men. If you are a man, say the sacrament prayer over bread and water alone at your table instead.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Stand in your own room and give a five-minute talk aloud on a scripture, in your own interpretation and from no outline, the discourse Witness women do not give to the congregation and Witness men give only from outlines the organization supplies.

Rite (Jewish): Listen for an hour to a woman singing, the voice the Talmud calls nakedness, and sing along if you know the words. If you are a woman, sing aloud yourself for an hour.

Rite (Muslim): Take off your hijab and go out uncovered for one errand, by your own choice, somewhere no believer will know you. If you never wore one, wear for the same errand whatever your tradition calls immodest on you.

Rite (No faith): Find the verse in First Timothy that says the woman was deceived and the man was not, read it aloud, strike it through on a printout, and say aloud the name of a woman who taught you something.

Question: Which modesty rule do you keep only for someone else's conscience?

Closing: If a woman is the devil's gateway, I hold the door open.
