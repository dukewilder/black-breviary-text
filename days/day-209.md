# Day 209: Of Going Alone

![The Demon Escort](../plates/inf150.jpg)

*The Demon Escort. Gustave Doré · Inferno, Canto XXI, 1861.*

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Elijah left his servant at Beersheba and went on by himself, forty days and forty nights, to Horeb, where the god asked him twice what he was doing there, and twice he complained that he alone was left. The second time the question came after the wind and the earthquake and the fire, in a still small voice, and what followed his complaint was a list of errands. Go back, anoint two kings, anoint Elisha to take your place, and know that seven thousand in Israel have never bowed to Baal.

The handbook for Latter-day Saint missionaries tells them never to be alone, to stay within sight and hearing of a companion everywhere except the bathroom and a few interviews, and to make no private hour by staying up later or rising earlier than the other. One stated reason is protection from false accusation, which makes the companion a witness on duty around the clock. Abu Dawud preserves a hadith that calls a lone rider a devil and two riders two devils, and only three a company.

Whoever was raised under such rules learns that an hour with no one in it needs explaining, and that outlasts the belief, so that a meal for one feels like an exposure and a film seen alone like a lapse. A pleasure taken alone has one judge. The appetite sets the order and the pace, the dish chosen without a glance across the table, the evening spent at the speed of one person's wanting. Elijah answered the question in the cave with complaints and was sent off with work. The answer he never gave is that he had come for his own sake, and whoever gives it keeps the evening a god would have filled with errands.

Charge: Take one pleasure unaccompanied.

Rite: Book a table for one at the best restaurant you can afford, the kind you were told to save for company, order exactly what you want, and keep your phone in your pocket until the bill comes.

Question: Which pleasure have you postponed until someone could watch you take it?

Closing: My long dinners for one go unrecorded, and the god who kept a census of the faithful will find me missing from it.
