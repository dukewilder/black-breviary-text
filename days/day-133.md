# Day 133: Of Fruit

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Genesis supplies a menu before it issues a single prohibition. The god gives the first pair every plant that yields seed and every tree with fruit in it, sets the man in a garden of trees that were a pleasure to see and to eat from, and tells him he may eat freely of every one save one, which alone comes with a threat of death.

She saw that the tree was good to eat and lovely to look at and desirable for the wisdom it would give, and she took of the fruit and ate. Appetite comes first in that list. The narrator reports what she saw and never says she saw wrongly, and the word sin has not yet appeared anywhere in Genesis, where it waits for Cain.

The sentence fell on the table. The god cursed the ground for the man's sake and made it bring up thorns, so that he would sweat for his bread until he returned to the dust. One meal taken freely was punished by turning every later meal into a wage, and the garden's menu gave way to a field that had to be worked.

The believer's table still serves that sentence. Gluttony stands among the seven deadly sins, and the holiest day of the Jewish year is spent without food. Both keep the meal out in the field of thorns, where food is earned and owed for. The first reason scripture records for eating anything is that the thing was good to eat, and a mouth that eats for that reason alone is back at the garden's table, with every tree on the menu.

Charge: Eat something today for the first reason Genesis gives.

Rite: Buy one fruit, the dearest ripe one the best grocer near you sells, a kind you have never eaten, and eat all of it with your bare hands, with cream or honey beside it, until the juice runs to your wrists.

Question: Which food did you learn to eat with an apology, and what does it taste like without one?

Closing: I say no grace over food I bought and cooked, and the god who cursed the ground for my sake gets no share of the plate.
