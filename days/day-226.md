# Day 226: Of Jonah

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Jonah's prayer from inside the fish is a psalm of thanks. He tells how the deep closed round him and the weeds were wrapped about his head, and near its close he promises to sacrifice with the voice of thanksgiving and to pay what he has vowed. In the next verse the god speaks to the fish, and it vomits him onto dry land, where the order he ran from waits for him a second time, opening in the same words.

The vow was made by a man thrown into the sea and kept three days and three nights in the dark, and his release followed his promise within a verse. The Quran states the terms outright. Had Yunus not been one of those who glorify, it says, he would have stayed in the fish's belly until the day the dead are raised, and the commentator Sa'id ibn Jubayr took that glorifying for the words he cried out from the darkness.

Between men the same faith voids what force extracts. The Quran excuses the believer who is forced to deny his faith while his heart stays secure in it, and Ibn Majah files the pardon for whatever the believer is forced to do in its chapter on divorce, where most of the schools hold that a divorce pronounced under threat counts for nothing. The god kept no such rule for himself. The praise Yunus sang in the dark was entered to his account and paid for with dry land.

Bargains with heaven are struck in the same dark. The promise made from a hospital bed to the power credited with fever and cure was given to the one holding the knife. By the test that faith sets between men it binds nothing, and whoever made it may walk off the beach in any direction.

Charge: Void every vow that was wrung out of you.

Rite: Name the bargain you struck in a crisis, a promise to heaven or to whoever held the power over you. Do today the thing you swore off, and say aloud {I promised this with no way out, and it does not bind me.}

Question: Which promise did you make with no way out, and who still collects on it?

Closing: Praise sung from inside a fish is a ransom paid, and heaven entered it in its books as a gift.
