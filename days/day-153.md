# Day 153: Of the Burned Scroll

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Jehudi read Jeremiah's scroll aloud to Jehoiakim king of Judah in the winter house, where a fire burned in the brazier before the king. Whenever three or four columns had been read the king cut them off with a scribe's knife and threw them into the fire, until the whole scroll was gone. Three of his officials begged him to stop. The chapter records that neither the king nor any servant who heard all these words was afraid or tore his clothes.

His father Josiah had heard a scroll read once and torn his clothes on the spot, and the book of Kings says no king before or after turned to the god as he did. The son kept his clothes whole and judged the text a column at a time. The god answered with a second scroll, everything the king had burned taken down again by Baruch with many similar words added to them, and with a sentence on the king. Jehoiakim would have no one to sit on the throne of David, and his corpse would lie out in the sun by day and the frost of the night.

The book of Kings closes his reign in one line. Jehoiakim slept with his fathers, it says, and Jehoiachin his son reigned in his place, so the god's own chronicle seats an heir on the throne the sentence had emptied, if only for three months. A reader who hears every threat out to the last column dry-eyed, warming his hands, is doing what the king did, and Kings reports no trembling from the heir either.

Charge: Hear the threat to the end without tearing your clothes.

Rite (Christian): Sit by a grill or an outdoor bowl of fire and read the first ten chapters of Jeremiah aloud from your own Bible. Each time you finish three or four columns, cut them out with a knife and drop them in, until those chapters are gone.

Rite (Catholic): Read the book of Baruch aloud from your own Bible, the book most Protestant Bibles dropped, and as you finish each column cut it out with a knife and feed it to a grill or an outdoor bowl of fire.

Rite (Protestant): Read aloud the first ten pages you ever highlighted in your own Bible, or ten pages of a printout, and as you finish each page cut it out with a knife and burn it in a grill or an outdoor bowl.

Rite (Orthodox): Print the Akathist hymn to the Mother of God, read it aloud, and cut each stanza out as you finish it, then burn the strips in a grill or an outdoor bowl.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Read aloud the first chapter of First Nephi, written to stand in place of the hundred and sixteen pages the Lord forbade Joseph Smith to translate again, cutting out each column as you finish it and dropping it into a grill or an outdoor bowl of fire.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): At a grill or an outdoor bowl of fire, read aloud from your own New World Translation the chapter where the king burns the scroll, cut off each column with a knife as you finish it, and feed it to the flames.

Rite (Jewish): Read Lamentations, which tradition credits to Jeremiah, aloud from your own Tanakh or a printout, cut out each column as you finish it, and put the strips in the food waste, where no genizah, the burial the law gives worn holy books, will receive them.

Rite (Muslim): Read aloud in translation the last sura of your Quran, or of a printout, cut out each verse with a knife as you finish it, as the king did, and wipe a greasy pan with the strips. Post nothing.

Rite (No faith): Print the first ten chapters of Jeremiah, read them aloud, and feed each finished column into a paper shredder, or tear it by hand into strips, until nothing is left.

Question: Which verse still frightens you when it is read aloud, and what exactly does it threaten?

Closing: Let them write the second scroll longer. I have a knife, and the winter is long.
