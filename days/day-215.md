# Day 215: Of Caiaphas

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Three stakes pin a naked man to the floor of the pit while hooded figures shuffle past behind him, and the plate names him Caiaphas. As high priest he told the council it was better for one man to die for the people than for the whole nation to perish, and the council's fear, given two verses earlier, was that the Romans would come and take away both their place and their nation. John calls the sentence a prophecy.

The Romans came anyway. Within forty years they burned the Temple and took Jerusalem, and the place and the nation went with the one man long dead on their behalf. On the Day of Atonement the high priest had laid both hands on a live goat, confessed over it the iniquities of the people and sent it into the wilderness, where, the Mishnah records, its handler pushed it backward off a cliff, and before it was halfway down it was broken in pieces.

Leviticus makes the goat a statute forever, once a year, so the account it closed stood open again a year later. Hebrews calls the repetition a remembrance of sins made every year, since the blood of bulls and goats could never take sins away, and offers one death to end it. The Catholic Church that preaches that death still binds its faithful to confess their grave sins at least once a year, on a yearly round like the goat's.

Caiaphas lies across the hypocrites' road so that each one passing treads on him and he feels the weight of every one. A wrong paid for by the one who did it closes the day it is paid, and no priest, goat or corpse stands between him and the account.

Charge: Refuse every offer to suffer in your place.

Rite (Christian): Spit on the figure of a crucifix you own, on the body the hymns say bore your sins, and hang it back up unwiped, saying {Carry your own.}

Rite (Catholic): Print the Lamb of God said at the breaking of the bread, pin it to the floor with three tacks, two at the top and one at the foot, and walk across it in your shoes, saying {I answer for my own.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Read aloud the verse where the Lord says he bled at every pore so the repentant would not suffer, then write beneath it in your own hand {My wrongs are mine to pay.} and keep the page.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Call or visit someone you wronged, name the wrong yourself with no excuse, and make it good as far as it can be made. Before you start, say to yourself {Nobody was my ransom, so I pay this one.}

Rite (Jewish): On an ordinary day eat a meal of pork and shellfish off a printed copy of the Day of Atonement confession of sins, and say {What I did is mine, and no goat carries it.}

Rite (Muslim): Read aloud the verses where the god ransoms Abraham's son with a great sacrifice, then stand on your own prayer mat in your street shoes and eat meat that was never slaughtered in his name, saying {No ram dies for me.}

Rite (No faith): Write on a sheet It is better that one man die for the people, sign it Caiaphas, and burn it in a grill or an outdoor bowl, saying {Nobody dies for me.}

Question: Which of your own wrongs is still waiting for someone else to carry it?

Closing: Any god who needs a corpse before he will forgive has told me what his forgiveness is worth.
