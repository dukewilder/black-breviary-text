# Day 109: Of Temptation

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Genesis, in the King James, states in so many words that God did tempt Abraham, ordering him to offer up his son, and the letter of James says outright that God tempts no one. The Lord's Prayer asks him not to lead us into temptation, which assumes he does. James and Genesis use one Greek root, peirazo, for testing and tempting alike, so the quarrel is over a word and the churches left the fear standing.

In the print above, the saint sits at the right edge under a lean-to of sticks and cloth, his head propped on his hand, and the rest of the sheet belongs to the other side, a giant fish with a domed house on its back, a sea full of boats and swimmers, men scrambling up ladders at the left and a gallows on the far hill. The saint got a stick shelter at the margin of his own legend.

Most of what you were trained to resist harms nobody. The laws in question mostly stand outside the ten commandments, the fast, the modest hem, the lowered gaze of sura 24, and each can be broken in an afternoon. The forbidden thing is usually small, a sleeve, an hour of music or a second glance, a warning recorded in Abu Dawud and Tirmidhi, and a church council in six ninety-two thought a mask and a dance worth forbidding. The appetite was yours before any tempter arrived, and a test you pass by refusing a sleeve proves only that you can be told.

Charge: Yield to one temptation you were forbidden for nothing.

Rite (Christian): Buy the food your church's fasts kept from you, the meat or the sweet, and eat it on the spot, slowly, to the last crumb. Then say the Lord's Prayer line turned round. {Lead me into temptation.}

Rite (Orthodox): Put on a mask, or tie a scarf over your face, and dance alone to loud music until you are out of breath, the masks and dances a church council forbade in six ninety-two.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Play loudly, from start to finish, a song with the vulgar or offensive lyrics the Church's older youth pamphlet told you to avoid, and sing along with every word.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Read the opening chapters of a novel about wizards or vampires, the magic in entertainment the Watchtower warns Witnesses away from, in one sitting, and enjoy every page of it.

Rite (Jewish): Hold the hand of a consenting adult friend who is not your spouse or close kin, or hug one, the touch the rabbis fenced off, for as long as you like. If no one is near, eat shellfish or bacon slowly.

Rite (Muslim): Take the second look the hadith counts against you, at a film, a painting or a friend who has looked at you first, and hold it as long as you enjoy it, without lowering your gaze.

Rite (No faith): Print a picture of Saint Anthony's temptation, cut the saint out and throw him away, and pin up the rest, every creature of it, where you eat. Eat there, beside it, the meat or sweet the old church would have refused you.

Rite (Protestant): Watch, in one sitting, a film your church or youth group warned against, or the one you suspect they would have, and enjoy it to the credits.

Question: Which harmless law of appetite do you still keep out of old reflex?

Closing: The saint got the lean-to, and I take the sea and everything swimming in it.
