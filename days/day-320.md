# Day 320: Of Saints

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Lately orphaned and eighteen or twenty years old, Antony of Egypt heard a gospel read in church. In it Jesus tells a rich man to sell all he has and give to the poor, and Antony went out and did it. Athanasius, the bishop who wrote his life, records that he gave the villagers the family land, some two hundred acres of good soil, sold the rest, and handed his young sister to a house of virgins to be brought up, so that he could give himself to the discipline unencumbered.

He spent most of the next eighty-odd years in a tomb, a deserted fort and then a mountain in the desert, fighting what he took to be demons. Decades on, coming down from the mountain, he saw his sister grown old in her virginity and the leader of other virgins, and Athanasius records that he rejoiced. The churches that venerate him call him the father of monks.

Athanasius gives the sister no name and no word. She was a little girl when her brother placed her, and the life measures her only by how well she kept the arrangement he made for her. The Quran's sura of the cave has a servant of God, whom the tradition names al-Khidr and honors, kill a boy for fear that he would one day burden his believing parents with defiance and disbelief, and the boy goes unnamed as well.

Each of these halos was paid for by someone else, a sister handed over at an age when she had no say and a boy killed for a sin he had not yet committed. The tales that honor the saint never record what either of them thought of the bargain, and the reverence they ask for goes whole to the one who made the bargain.

Charge: Take the halo off before you admire anyone.

Rite (Christian): Take a holy card, print or page showing a saint or hero of the faith you were raised to admire, write across it in marker the worst thing that figure did, read aloud what you wrote, and hang it upside down where you eat.

Rite (Catholic): Print the name of the saint you were named for, or any saint you were shown, and argue aloud against that saint as the devil's advocate did. Then write Not proven across the name and stand the page on its head.

Rite (Orthodox): Take the icon of your name saint from your icon corner, or a printout of it, lay it face down on the floor, or on the shelf where it stood, and read aloud over it the worst thing the saint's life records.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Hang a printed picture of President Spencer Kimball upside down and read aloud beneath it the harshest page of his Miracle of Forgiveness, or of any prophet's book you own.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Hang a printed photograph of Judge Rutherford upside down and read aloud to it the story of Beth Sarim, the San Diego mansion held in trust for the faithful men of old on their return from the dead, where Rutherford himself wintered and died.

Rite (Jewish): Open your own Tanakh at the story in Numbers where the priest Phinehas spears a man and a woman together in their tent and is rewarded with a priesthood forever. Write their names, Zimri and Cozbi, in large letters across it, and shelve the book upside down.

Rite (Muslim): Read aloud, in your own Quran, the cave sura's account of the servant who killed the boy, and answer it aloud {He had done nothing yet.} Then write the same words on a slip of paper and leave it in the book at that page.

Rite (No faith): Print a picture of a saint you were shown as a child, or of Antony himself if you were shown none, write the case against the saint across it in marker, read the case aloud to it, and put it face down under your doormat.

Question: What did the holy figure you most wanted to resemble as a child actually do?

Closing: My heroes are the ones who stayed home with their sisters.
