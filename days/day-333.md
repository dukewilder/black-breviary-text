# Day 333: Of Volume

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

At Jericho, Joshua forbade the people to shout or let a word out of their mouths until the day he gave the order. For six days the army circled the city in silence with seven priests blowing rams' horns in its midst, the horns sounding and the men mute, and on the seventh day the order came, the people shouted, and the wall fell flat. The noise belonged to the priests until the general released it.

The Talmud kept the silence for the standing prayer and dropped the release. A man who lets his voice be heard in the standing prayer is of little faith, and one who raises it is counted among the false prophets, who shouted to their gods, the kind the prophets of Baal on Carmel would illustrate. The Quran has Luqman teach his son to lower his voice in general speech, since the harshest of all sounds is the braying of the donkey.

The synagogue kept loudness for its offices, the shofar in an appointed mouth, and the mosque the call to prayer given to Bilal because his voice carried farther, and the Talmud filed the raised voice in the standing prayer with the doubter and the false prophet, excusing only a man praying alone who cannot hold his mind on the words under his breath.  A congregation gets it on cue, an amen in its place and a response at the leader's signal. Raised to full strength where nobody gave the order, the same throat fills a room to the walls.

Charge: Raise your voice without waiting for the cue.

Rite: Play the grandest music you love as loud as the room will bear, somewhere nobody will be disturbed, and sing over it at the full size of your voice. End on a shout held for as long as your breath lasts.

Question: Where did you learn to make yourself small in sound, and who gained by it?

Closing: I have kept quiet in enough prayer rooms to be owed a lifetime of noise.
