# Day 273: Of the Inn

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Jesus sends Peter and John to prepare the Passover. He tells them to follow a man carrying a water jar, enter the house he enters and ask the goodman of the house where the guest room is, and they are shown a large upper room, furnished, where he eats his last Passover reclining with the apostles. Luke's word for that guest room, kataluma, is the word he uses at the birth, on a reading some scholars hold, for the place with no room, though the sense of the word there is disputed between a guest room and a lodging. The inn where the Samaritan, who has bound the stranger's wounds and set him on his own beast, on the morrow, gives two denarii to the host and promises to repay whatever more is spent has another word, pandocheion, for a house that takes in anyone who pays.

The gospel that opens with no room for him nears its end with a room furnished for him, and the man who said he had nowhere to lay his head sent Peter and John ahead to claim it. He had told his followers to sell what they had and give alms. When the hour came he sat down in the furnished room and Luke records no word about alms.

Those raised on holy poverty keep the manger and never reach the upper room. They pay a guest's bill without blinking and haggle over their own, and keep the narrow bed for years. None of the four gospels records a complaint about the furniture.

Charge: Refuse yourself nothing you would gladly buy for a guest.

Rite: Check the price once, then book the best room, table or spa hour within your means, and use everything on offer, the bath, the robe, the meal, without looking at a price again.

Question: Which discomfort have you kept so long that you take it for character?

Closing: I take the furnished upper room and leave the stable to the hymns.
