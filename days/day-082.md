# Day 82: Of Relics

![The Paradise of Fools](../plates/pl076.jpg)

*The Paradise of Fools. Gustave Doré · Paradise Lost, Book III, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Two merchants of Venice carried Saint Mark out of Alexandria in the ninth century in a basket under a layer of pork, trusting the Muslim officials of the port to recoil from searching it. Venice celebrated the theft and built a basilica over the corpse. The evangelist, packed in with the meat, did nothing to the men who took him, and the city that stole him put his lion on its flag.

Thomas Becket lay behind the high altar at Canterbury from twelve twenty, in a shrine plated with gold and set with jewels, and pilgrims carried home water tinged with his blood. A window beside the shrine still tells how a Yorkshire knight, Jordan son of Eisulf, revived his dead boy with that water and promised the martyr four pieces of silver, and the next panels show another son dying because the silver came late. Every kneeling pilgrim walked past a picture of what the saint did to slow payers. In fifteen thirty-eight the king's commissioners broke up the shrine and carted off its jewels, and the saint did nothing to them either.

In these three stories the relic's anger falls only on the man who believed he owed it. Thieves who stole one saint and a king who stripped another went unharmed, and the knight who believed lost a son for being slow. A rosary kept in a drawer long after belief has left, because throwing it away still feels dangerous, is held there by the debt the window was glazed to teach, and that feeling is the object's only power. A household that throws its last blessed bead in the trash joins the merchants of Venice and the king's men, among whom the record counts no dead sons.

Charge: Throw out the object you are afraid to throw out.

Rite (Christian): Take the cross necklace, bracelet or devotional you still keep out of unease, and put it in the trash.

Rite (Catholic): Put your blessed palm in the ordinary trash with the potato peelings, the sacramental the Church says must be burned or returned, or with no palm any blessed medal or holy card you keep, and take the bag out.

Rite (Orthodox): Put the blessed candle you carried home from the Paschal service, or any other blessed object you keep, out with the kitchen trash.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Throw away your copy of your patriarchal blessing, the one you have been afraid to lose, or any card, certificate or keepsake from the church you keep out of unease, and keep no copy anywhere.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Throw the keepsake of your baptism, the photograph from the assembly pool or the card from that day, into the ordinary trash with the kitchen scraps.

Rite (Jewish): Cut the fringes from the small four-cornered garment you own and put them in the trash, or if you never owned one, throw out any ritual object you kept from unease, a kippah or a mezuzah case.

Rite (Muslim): Pour the Zamzam water you were given down the sink and use the bottle for something ordinary. If you have none, put the prayer beads or printed prayer you keep as a charm in the trash.

Rite (No faith): Find the lucky object you keep and would be uneasy to lose, a charm, a coin, a ring, and throw it in the trash today.

Question: What harm do you expect from parting with the sacred object you still own?

Closing: What stays in my house stays because I like it, and every blessed thing I owned failed that test long ago.
