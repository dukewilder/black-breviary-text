# Day 317: Of Merchandise

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Cinnamon is on the list of cargoes that Babylon's merchants mourn in Revelation. When the city falls, the merchants of the earth stand far off and weep because nobody buys their cargoes any more. The book itemizes those cargoes with a dealer's care, the gold and silver and pearls, fine linen and purple and silk and scarlet, the cinnamon and spice and incense, the wine and oil and fine flour, the cattle and horses and chariots, and last of all, after the chariots, the bodies and souls of men.

The fall leaves a ruin of smoke and silence, a city become the haunt of every foul spirit and a cage for every unclean and hateful bird, and the verdict counts her luxury among her crimes.

The angel who hurls the millstone into the sea gives his reasons, and the first is that her merchants were the great men of the earth. Greatness in trade heads the angel's list, ahead of the sorcery and the blood of the prophets. The sentence falls on the whole city at once, and with the market in bodies it silences the harper and the piper, the craftsman of every craft, the miller at his stone, the light of the lamp and the bridegroom's voice. The seer wrote the souls of men into one inventory with the cinnamon and the fine flour, and the burning that ends the slave market ends the bakery with it. A verdict that cannot tell the miller from the slaver has lost the right to sentence either, and the cinnamon dealer, standing far off for fear of her torment, is owed exactly what he was owed before she fell, his price.

Charge: Buy the luxury and owe heaven no apology for it.

Rite: Make one dish from the merchants' own list, fine flour and good oil and true cinnamon bark, the best of each you can get, bought from its maker where you can, and serve it on the finest plate you own.

Question: Who taught you to feel guilty for wanting the cargo you have never bought yourself?

Closing: Money paid for excellent work is the cleanest praise one adult gives another, and I owe no apology for the pleasure it buys.
