# Day 236: Of the Almighty

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Almighty is the King James word for Shaddai, an old Hebrew name for the god in Genesis and Job whose original meaning is uncertain, and Latin rendered it omnipotent, and the Latin Creed opens with belief in one god, the father almighty, maker of heaven and earth. The Quran repeats dozens of times that the god has power over all things. Such a god answers for everything, as governor of all things.

The first command the god gives the man in the garden forbids one tree among all the rest on pain of death, and every other tree is eaten under that warning. The gospel sells the same arrangement as ease. Look at the birds of the air, Jesus says, your heavenly father feeds them, and on that ground he tells his hearers to take no thought for the morrow. The ease he offered was drawn on the governor's account, and whoever closes the account loses the reason the sermon gave for taking no thought.

A person who has watched the governor leave can take the governor's chair as sovereign of a single life, with title to its pleasures and its acts and no jurisdiction over the tides. The god of Genesis held all things by decree. One of his decrees drowned every creature of the dry land outside a single boat. A sovereign of one life has no flood to answer for, and can leave the economy and the weather to run without him, as they ran all along under the last holder of the chair.

Charge: Resign from governing what was never yours.

Rite (Christian): Write ALMIGHTY on a card, strike it through, and sign your own name under it. Carry the card until night and say {I answer for my own days.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Pour a cup of coffee, the drink the Word of Wisdom forbids, and over it recite the first Article of Faith with yourself in it. {I believe in myself, the eternal father of my own days.}

Rite (No faith): Lie down in your own bed with the lights out and say the Creed's first line with yourself in it, {I believe in myself, almighty, maker of my own days.} Then sleep without checking a single thing.

Rite (Jewish): Tonight, in place of the bedtime Shema, say aloud {I keep my own house tonight, and I need no one to guard it.} and turn out the light.

Rite (Muslim): Say {He has power over all things.} then stand for prayer, at whatever hour you open this, without the washing the law requires, and end it by saying aloud {I rule my own life.} in place of the closing greeting.

Question: Which part of your own life are you still leaving to providence?

Closing: My sovereignty ends at my own skin, and I would not trade it for the Almighty's.
