# Day 128: Of Cedars

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Hiram, king of Tyre, had the cedars cut on Lebanon and floated down the coast in rafts, and took his payment in wheat and oil, twenty thousand measures of wheat a year. Solomon raised a levy of thirty thousand men from Israel and sent them up the mountain in shifts of ten thousand a month, and kept besides seventy thousand who carried loads and eighty thousand who hewed the great stones.

All of it was for a god who had told David, through Nathan, that he had never once asked for a house of cedar and had lived in a tent since Egypt. At the dedication Solomon himself conceded that heaven and the heaven of heavens could not contain the god, and how much less this house. In the oracle to David the god promised that David's son would build him a house, and when the son had built it in cedar the god hallowed it, and let the Babylonians burn it nearly four centuries later.

He took seven years over the temple and thirteen over his own palace, and one hall of the palace, the House of the Forest of Lebanon, ran a hundred cubits on its rows of cedar pillars against the temple's sixty. The king who said heaven could not hold the god kept the longer job and the longer hall for himself. Congregations still raise cedar for a god who told David he had never asked for it, through building funds and roof appeals and the tithe that pays for marble, and some of the givers go home to rooms they have not finished.

Charge: Spend the temple's money on the rooms you live in.

Rite: Spend today the sum a month's offering once cost you, or the price of an ordinary gift if you never gave, on one thing made well in wood, wool, stone or glass for the room where you live most, and use it daily.

Question: Which room of yours has gone without while the god's house got cedar?

Closing: Cedar belongs where somebody sleeps, so the good wood comes home with me and the god can keep his tent.
