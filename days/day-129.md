# Day 129: Of Wisdom

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A rabbinic tradition holds that Solomon wrote three books at three ages, the Song of Songs in youth, Proverbs in his middle years and Ecclesiastes when he was old. The young man compared a lover's breasts to two fawns. The man of middle years called wisdom a tree of life to all who lay hold on her and promised that the rod would drive folly out of a child. The old man said that in much wisdom is much grief and advised eating bread with joy, since the grave has no work in it. Each book was wisdom in its decade, and the last takes back the tree of life.

The first book of Kings supplies the old man's circumstances. Seven hundred wives and three hundred concubines turned his heart after Ashtoreth of the Sidonians, and he built high places on the hill facing Jerusalem for Chemosh of Moab and for Molech of the Ammonites, so that the wisest king the god ever made spent his last years raising shrines for his wives. His counsel moved with his life, and the canon that kept all three books kept the drift.

A man who married at twenty and never left his county will tell you that roots are everything, and a woman who never had to earn her living will tell you that money does not matter, and each is giving an honest report of a life that is not yours. The cult of the elder assumes the old have walked your road. They walked one road to its far end, and a map of another country is worth reading and owed no obedience. Parents still take the rod to children on the word of Proverbs, whose author, by the same tradition, ended his life calling all of it vanity.

Charge: Read each inherited rule as its maker's life.

Rite: Choose the rule you inherited from an elder who never stood where you stand, never spend on yourself, work before pleasure, dessert last, and break it today with a pleasure you can finish before the day is out. {This decade is mine, and so is its rule.}

Question: Whose rule are you still keeping about a life they never had to live?

Closing: I honor my elders by letting their rules die with the lives that made them.
