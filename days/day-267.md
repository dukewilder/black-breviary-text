# Day 267: Of Doubt

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Only Matthew lets Peter out of the boat. The disciples are rowing against a head wind in the fourth watch of the night when they see a figure walking on the sea, and Peter says that if it is the Lord he should bid Peter come to him on the water. The figure says come. Peter climbs over the side and walks, then sees the wind, is afraid, begins to sink and shouts to be saved. A hand catches him, and with it comes a question, why he doubted.

Peter's request was an experiment, a condition stated with the one way to check it, and the experiment returned a result. The promise held while the walker saw nothing to fear and failed when he saw the wind, and the failure was entered against Peter. Little faith, the verdict says, and the water and the promise are acquitted. Deuteronomy had already forbidden putting the god to the test, and the Catholic Catechism still counts voluntary doubt among the sins against the first commandment.

Scripture wrote the verdict into a rule. Let a man ask in faith, nothing wavering, says the letter of James, and let the waverer not think he will receive anything, and Matthew says that in his own country he did not many mighty works because of their unbelief. A promise built that way is safe from its own failures, since each one is booked to the believer who tested it. The men who stayed in the boat worshiped him and drew no rebuke that night, having risked nothing that could be checked. Of the men in that boat, Peter alone learned what the promise would bear, and he learned it sinking.

Charge: Put one article of your old faith to the test today.

Rite (Christian): Read aloud the promise that faith can move a mountain, tell your heaviest piece of furniture to move with all the belief you can raise, wait one full minute, and write the result in ink across that page of your Bible.

Rite (Catholic): Say aloud any prayer you were taught earns an indulgence, doubting on purpose that anything is remitted, the voluntary doubt your Catechism names a sin, and write on a slip what, if anything, changed.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Read aloud the Word of Wisdom's promise of health in the navel and marrow to the bones, drink a glass of the strong drink it forbids, or whatever you drink, and write beside the promise what you noticed in the next hour.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Read one chapter of a book by a former Witness, the kind the Society calls apostate, and write on a slip one claim of the Society's that the chapter lets you check today.

Rite (Jewish): Take up Malachi's dare, in which the god says prove me now. Put a tenth of today's money in a jar, write the date and the verse on its lid, and in a month write underneath whether the windows of heaven opened.

Rite (Muslim): Alone and unseen, take up the Quran's own challenge to produce its like. Write three lines in the rhythm of its verses on any subject you please, read them aloud beside the verse that dares you, and note underneath what happened.

Rite (No faith): Fill a basin or a bath with water, say Peter's request, {If you are there, tell me to come to you on the water.} set a foot in it, and see what holds you up. Laugh at the god who blamed a fisherman for noticing the wind.

Sober alternative (Latter-day Saint): Read aloud the Word of Wisdom's promise of health in the navel and marrow to the bones, eat a large meal of meat, which it says to use sparingly and only in times of cold or famine, and write beside the promise what you noticed in the next hour.

Question: Which failure of a promise were you taught to blame on yourself?

Closing: Doubt is the one prayer that ever got an answer in my house.
