# Day 61: Of Judges

![Minos](../plates/inf044.jpg)

*Minos. Gustave Doré · Inferno, Canto V, 1861.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Minos, a king of Crete in the Greek telling, sat as judge among the dead, a man trying men. The court the Christian is promised has a stranger bench. The Gospel of John says the Father judges no one and has committed all judgment to the Son, and the First Epistle of John names the same Son as the advocate who pleads for sinners with the Father, so that the judge and the counsel for the defense are one person.

The Quran admits no intercession with its god except by his leave, so that the only advocate allowed in the room is one the bench has licensed, and it hands each person the record spread open with the order to read it, since on that day he is sufficient as his own accountant. The Mishnah keeps the ledger open and the hand writing, with the collectors going their rounds every day and taking payment with the debtor's consent or without it. Another saying in Avot gives a man one advocate for each commandment he keeps, so that the voice raised in his defense is the court's own law.

A religious childhood installs that court in the head, where it sits long after the catechism is shut, ruling in a parent's voice or an elder's, with the defense still appointed by the bench. Every acquittal it grants arrives as mercy, and the accused goes home thanking the judge for sparing him a sentence the judge wrote. The office it kept from him was counsel of his own choosing, and he can take it without anyone's leave by refusing the bench's books along with its mercy.

Charge: Dismiss the judge you never appointed.

Rite (Christian): Say aloud the line of the Creed that has him coming again to judge the living and the dead, then answer it as your own counsel. {I speak for myself, and the case is closed.}

Rite (Catholic): Say the prayer the rosary adds after each decade, {O my Jesus, forgive us our sins, save us from the fires of hell,} and answer the judge who lit them. {Save me from nothing. I sit in judgment here.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Say aloud the title the Church gives a bishop, a common judge in Israel, and then take the bench yourself. {I am the only common judge in my house.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Say aloud what Witnesses are taught about the end, {Judgment Day will last a thousand years,} and then pass judgment on yourself in a single sentence. {I have judged myself, and I am acquitted.}

Rite (Jewish): Read aloud the Yom Kippur prayer of who shall live and who shall die, and wherever it has the god writing and sealing, answer the line. {I hold the pen.}

Rite (Muslim): Write one page of your own record for this week, the deeds you are glad of, read it aloud, and sign it. {I have read my own record, and I approve it.}

Rite (No faith): Take the chair at the head of your table, strike the tabletop three times with a spoon for a gavel, and rule {This court has no judge, and the case against me is dismissed.} Then stand and walk out as one acquitted.

Question: Whose voice does the judge in your head actually use, and what was its last ruling against you?

Closing: I choose who speaks for me, and I have chosen myself.
