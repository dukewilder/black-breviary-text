# Day 200: Of Radiance

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

When Moses came down the mountain with the second tablets, Exodus says, the skin of his face shone, and the people were afraid to come near him. He gave them the commands with his face bare and then put a veil over it, and he took the veil off only when he went in to speak with the god. Paul, writing centuries later, decided the veil had been worn so that Israel would not see the glory fading.

Exodus names no cause for the veil and sets it after one fact, the fear of the people. Paul supplied a cause of his own. The shining itself he never denied. The same Paul told the Philippians to count others better than themselves and the Romans to think of themselves no more highly than they ought, and the churches made a virtue of the covering, so that a gift shown openly reads as pride and a gift kept covered reads as grace.

The people below the mountain were afraid of a face, and the fear was theirs. The veil eased it at the face's expense, and every dimming since has made the same trade. A voice that fills a room and a mind that sees the answer first unsettle the people beside them, and a cloth over either buys the room its ease.

Exodus never says the light faded, only that the people saw it shining each time Moses came out from the god, and the fading is Paul's addition to the text. A gift kept under cloth for a lifetime does fade at last, from want of use, and the cloth will have done the fading Paul blamed on the light.

Charge: Take off the veil and let them be afraid.

Rite: Put on the thing you look best in, and today, in front of one person whose opinion matters to you, use the skill you usually play down. When you are praised for it, answer {Yes, I am good at it.} and add nothing after.

Question: Which of your gifts do you cover in company, and whose comfort does the cover buy?

Closing: Lucifer was named for the light he carried, and I carry mine uncovered.
