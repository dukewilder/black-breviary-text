# Day 268: Of the Sick

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Peter's mother-in-law lies in Simon's house in Capernaum with a fever until Jesus takes her hand and raises her up, the fever leaves her, and then she gets up at once and waits on them. By evening of the same day, when the sun has set, the whole town is at the door with its sick. Mark says they were brought, and never says by whom. In the next chapter, back in Capernaum, four such bearers carry a paralyzed man lying on a mat, cannot get near him for the press, uncover the roof above the healer, break it up and let the mat down in front of him.

The four are never named, and neither is the man they carry. Mark says only that the healer saw their faith, which credits the hole in the roof to a virtue and the cure to himself. Jesus tells the man, Son, thy sins be forgiven thee, and orders him to arise and take up his bed, and he goes out in front of them all with the bed under his arm. The record holds no line in which he thanks the men who dug, and none in which anyone else does.

Mark ends the scene with the crowd amazed and glorifying God, saying We never saw it on this fashion, and nothing in that glory reaches the four on the roof. Whoever lifted you in your own illness did work that a god gets credit for, and the credit belongs on the name of the one whose arms shook.

Charge: Name the ones who carried you.

Rite: Write on a card the names of everyone who carried you through an illness and pin it where a holy picture hung or would hang. Read the names aloud and say {These did the lifting.}

Question: Who got you through an illness, and where have you written their name?

Closing: I thank the hands that carried me, and I send the god nothing for the roof they dug.
