# Day 3: Of Falling

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The Greek root of apostasy means to stand away, a word that keeps the leaver upright, and the King James translators, meeting it in a prophecy of the last days, rendered it a falling away. The rest of the churches' vocabulary for leaving runs downhill with it. A Christian who sacrificed to the gods under the emperor Decius was counted among the lapsed, the ones who slipped, as though the act had been an accident of the feet, and Cyprian of Carthage wrote a treatise on them and priced their return in penance. The letter to the Hebrews declares it impossible to bring anyone who falls away back to repentance. Fallen from grace is Paul's phrase, aimed at Galatians who were adding circumcision and the law to their faith, and the pulpit has since turned it on people who walk out.

Each of these words moves the body by a force outside its control, the way a stone moves when the hand opens, and each carries a verdict with it, that the one who left was dropped or tripped and should be ashamed of the bruise. A slip asks for a hand up, and the hand belongs to the priest. Backsliding, the King James word for Israel in the prophets, breaks the pattern, because in Hosea it describes a heifer, a backsliding heifer, where the Hebrew calls her stubborn.

Keep the heifer and refuse the rest. Hosea meant her as an insult and the King James translators made a backslider of her, and still she is the one leaver in that whole vocabulary drawn from life, a heifer with all four feet planted against the goad.

Charge: Refuse every word that says you were dropped.

Rite (Christian): Recite the Apostles' Creed as far as {he descended into hell}, then stop and name your own going in a plain past tense. {He descended. I walked out.}

Rite (Catholic): Say aloud the words your Church keeps for one who leaves, apostate or lapsed or fallen away, then say what you did in plain past tense. {Lapsed, they call it. I chose the door.}

Rite (Protestant): Say aloud the word your church kept for people like you, {backslider}, the King James word for a heifer that would not be driven, then say what you actually did, in two words. {I left.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Stand as at a fast and testimony meeting and bear your testimony in the old words with one word changed. {I know this church is not true, and I left it standing up.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Say aloud whichever of the elders' words fits you, inactive or disassociated or removed from the congregation, the new name for disfellowshipped, and add your own judgment of it. {It is the best thing I have done.}

Rite (Jewish): Say in English the phrase the Orthodox keep for those who leave, {gone off the path}, and then give the verb it hides. {I walked off it.}

Rite (Muslim): Say aloud the word the jurists keep for you, {murtadd}, which means one who turns back, and then answer it. {I turned, and I am alive.}

Rite (No faith): Say aloud the churches' word for people raised outside them, {lost}, and then tell the god of Abraham where you have been standing the whole time. {Here, on level ground, with no need of you.}

Rite (Orthodox): Say aloud the Greek word your church uses for one who leaves, {apostate}, which means one who stands away, then give the plain verb. {I stood away, on my own legs.}

Question: What word did they give your leaving, and what did it ask you to feel?

Closing: Lapsed is the church's word for a door it could not keep shut, and I went through it upright.
