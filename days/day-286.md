# Day 286: Of Judas

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

It is objected that anyone who leaves the faith of a family is a Judas. He breaks a vow, leaves his place at the family table bare and goes over to the other side for whatever it pays, and the Church carved what becomes of such a man on the tops of its columns, Judas hanging by the neck between two devils.

Judas went to the chief priests of his own accord and asked what they would give him to hand Jesus over, and they set the price at thirty pieces of silver, and from then on he watched for his chance. He chose the sign himself, a kiss, and gave it at Gethsemane, and the man he kissed was dead the next afternoon. Only when he saw the sentence passed did he bring the silver back, throw it down in the temple and hang himself.

That is a grown man seeking out a buyer, taking money and marking a victim for arrest. The one who leaves a family's faith has no buyer. He goes over to an empty side, where no priest waits with a purse, and everyone he leaves sleeps in his own bed that night. His chair at a long table goes to someone else, and his name drops off the Christmas cards.

Matthew has the priests gather up the thrown silver and refuse it for the treasury, since it was the price of blood, then buy a potter's field with it to bury strangers in. The men who paid for the kiss kept their books clean on a point of law. A family keeps the same books when it enters the cost of a son's leaving under his name, and the word traitor goes to the one person at that table who paid.

Charge: Refuse the traitor's role others have cast you in.

Rite: Lay thirty coins of any kind on a table. Name aloud each thing you lost by going, sweeping one coin off with {I paid that} for each, however few. Then spend all thirty, with whatever you add, on something for yourself.

Question: Which of your losses has the family recorded as your crime?

Closing: Judas took thirty pieces for a man's life, and all I took when I left was my Sundays.
