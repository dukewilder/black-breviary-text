# Day 279: Of Lazarus

![The Raising of Lazarus](../plates/b197.jpg)

*The Raising of Lazarus. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

When word comes that Lazarus of Bethany is sick, Jesus stays where he is for two more days. John says he loved the man and his sisters, and has him tell the disciples that the sickness is for the glory of God and that he is glad, for their sakes, to have been away, so that they may believe. By the time he arrives Lazarus has lain four days in the tomb.

Martha meets him on the road and opens with one sentence, that if he had been there her brother would not have died. Mary comes out of the house, falls at his feet and says the same words. Two sisters, separately, bring the identical charge, and John records it twice and answers it with a miracle. At the open grave Jesus thanks his father aloud and explains that he says it for the crowd standing by, so that they may believe. Lazarus walks out and never speaks a word in the gospel, and the chief priests then plan to kill him as well, because on his account many were going over to Jesus.

Piety calls the two days God's timing and teaches the person at the bedside to wait without complaint on a schedule kept elsewhere. The delay was for the disciples' belief and the prayer at the grave for the crowd's, and Lazarus and his sisters paid for both with four days. Martha and Mary kept no such patience, and Jesus, answering them with a resurrection, never once denies that his presence would have saved the man. To wait on the proper time is to wait on someone else's display, and its cost falls on whoever lies in the rock.

Charge: Set the date yourself and look for no sign.

Rite: Take the thing you have put off until heaven or circumstance gives the word, and make it irreversible today, the deposit paid, the ticket bought, the letter sent. Before you do it, say aloud {You were not here, so I went ahead.}

Question: What have you been told to wait for, and who gains from the wait?

Closing: A god who let his friend lie four days in a tomb to impress a crowd keeps no calendar of mine.
