# Day 301: Of Tongues

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Parthians and Medes, Cretans and Arabs, and pilgrims from eleven other peoples and places heard the disciples that day, Acts claims, each in their own language. Tongues like flames had settled on each of the disciples in the house where they were gathered, and they began speaking languages they had never learned.

The Pentecostal revival of the gift began in Topeka at the turn of the twentieth century, when a student named Agnes Ozman was reported to have spoken Chinese, and within a few years Pentecostal missionaries were sailing abroad expecting to preach in languages granted on arrival. Her teacher had taught that the tongues were human languages that would spare a missionary years of study. The languages did not come. What the Pentecostal churches practice now is a flow of syllables that most linguists who have studied it find has the sounds of the speaker's own language and no grammar at all.

The churches kept the gift and changed its terms. Tongues became, for most, a private prayer language, a speech no hearer is expected to translate and so no hearer can check. Paul had already warned the assembly at Corinth that an outsider who walked in on a whole church speaking in tongues would say they were mad, and wrote that he would sooner speak five words with his understanding in church than ten thousand in a tongue.

Five words a listener understands commit the speaker, and ten thousand he cannot follow commit nobody. The demand you owe the colleague who takes your credit, or the price you have never named to the client who underpays, fits in about that many, and once they have been said to his face, you can be held to every one of them.

Charge: Make your demand in words you can be held to.

Rite: Write your demand or your price for one person in five words, or as few as will carry it, and say it to them today, in person or by phone, with no preamble and no apology. Skip it where saying it would put you at risk.

Question: What is the short sentence you owe the one who has been taking from you?

Closing: I name my price in the language of the man who pays it, in few enough words that he can say them back to me.
