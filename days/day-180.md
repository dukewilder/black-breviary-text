# Day 180: Of Pursuit

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Timotheus camped beyond a stream with his army and told his captains what would decide the battle. If Judas crossed the water first, they would not be able to stand against him. If he was afraid and camped on the far side, they would cross over themselves and beat him. Judas posted officers along the bank with orders to let no man stay behind in the camp, and went over first, and the whole army after him, and the enemy threw away their weapons and ran.

Timotheus told his captains what he feared, and the book gives Judas's scouts a report of a very great host and no word of that plan, yet Judas did what the enemy feared. The order he gave left the army no vote on whether it was ready to wade. Staying had been made impossible, and the only ground left to stand on was the far side.

Judas set his officers at the stream with orders that no one camp, because the danger to his crossing lay behind him. The near bank holds the tents and the reasonable voice that counsels one more night, and a man who waits for his nerve to carry him past them waits for a thing that comfort exists to dissolve. Clear the camp and the nerve is unnecessary. The men who waded that day were no braver than the men who would have stayed behind, and they were the same men. Timotheus had staked his battle on Judas being afraid to cross, and he lost it to a few officers standing at the stream.

Charge: Cross the water before you feel ready.

Rite: Name the contest you have been camped beside. Cancel the one thing that lets you put it off another week, the extension or the second opinion, and make your offer, application or demand today, before the other side moves.

Question: What keeps the near bank comfortable for you?

Closing: My enemies are counting on my fear of the far bank, and I am already across.
