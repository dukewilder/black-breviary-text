# Day 124: Of Combat

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Abner proposed a game. His army, holding out for the house of Saul, and Joab's, fighting for David, had sat down on either side of the pool at Gibeon, and Abner suggested that the young men get up and play before them. Twelve went out from each side, each caught his opposite by the head and put a sword in his side, and all twenty-four fell down together. The place was named for it, the field of sharp edges, and the game won neither house a yard of ground and opened a long war.

The man who proposed the game was the first to want it stopped. At sundown Abner called down to Joab, shall the sword devour forever, do you not know it will be bitter in the end, and Joab blew the trumpet and the pursuit stopped. Nothing had been settled. Nineteen of David's men lay dead besides Joab's brother Asahel, whom Abner had killed with the butt of his spear, and three hundred and sixty of Abner's. The account came due at Hebron, after Abner had come over to David, when Joab drew him aside in the gate as if to speak privately and stabbed him for his brother's blood.

The war for Saul's throne had a prize, and it closed when David was anointed over all Israel. Joab's quarrel with Abner, kept for a dead brother, closed only in the gate. Most rivalries in a life are fought on Joab's terms, the office feud and the dinner-table quarrel, and they stop when somebody tires and start again when somebody remembers. A rival who wants your client is beaten by the better bid on the day the decision is made, and a feud with no prize keeps its dead on the books until somebody lies under the fifth rib.

Charge: Name the rival and the prize before you draw.

Rite: Write your rival's name and the prize on one card and cut it in two. Burn the name in the sink, put the prize in your wallet, and today send the bid, file the application or call the client you both want.

Question: What exactly do you mean to take from your rival?

Closing: I draw only for a prize I can name, and the family feud can go on with one fewer player.
