# Day 51: Of the Few

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Gideon had thirty-two thousand men, and the voice said they were too many, in case Israel should boast that its own hand had saved it. Whoever was afraid was sent home, and twenty-two thousand went. The ten thousand left were taken down to the water, where Gideon was told to set apart those who lapped it like dogs, bringing their hands to their mouths, from those who knelt to drink. Three hundred lapped. With three hundred, said the voice, Israel would be saved, and the rest went back to their tents.

The army was thinned for a reason the text states outright, so that nobody but the god could take the credit. Gideon heard the reason and overrode it. The cry he chose for his three hundred, when they broke their pitchers and blew their trumpets around the camp of Midian in the dark, was for the sword of the god and of Gideon, his own name shouted beside the name of the god who had cut his army to keep it out.

If you were raised to give all glory to heaven, you were raised on the same rule. The psalm prays that the glory go to the god's name and twice refuses it to the singers, and the letter to the Ephesians says salvation comes by grace and not of works, lest any man should boast. Every success went upward and every failure stayed with you.

Judges credits the rout to the god, who set every man's sword against his fellow, and lets the shout stand with no rebuke. Heaven's name and Gideon's went up in one breath, and the book charges neither to the other.

Charge: Keep the glory of your own work.

Rite: Take one piece of your own work that was credited to a boss, a team or heaven. Put your name on it where the people who judge such work will see it, or tell them in your own words that it was yours.

Question: What did you build that you let heaven or the people above you take the credit for?

Closing: The work is mine, and so is the glory the psalm wants sent upstairs.
