# Day 87: Of Holy Water

![Saint James and the Magician Hermogenes](../plates/the-magician.jpg)

*Saint James and the Magician Hermogenes. Pieter Bruegel the Elder, 1565.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The Roman Ritual makes holy water in three movements. The priest exorcises a pinch of salt, commanding every unclean spirit out of it, then exorcises the water and prays that it cast out demons and banish disease, and last pours the salt into the water in the form of a cross. Salt and water and a spoken command against fever were the stock in trade of the village cunning woman too, and the difference between her bowl and the priest's lay in who had been licensed to stir it.

The Church told the story of that license itself, in the legend of the apostle James. A magician called Hermogenes sends demons to fetch the saint in chains, an angel binds them in chains of fire, and they are sent back to fetch the magician instead. Freed and afraid of their revenge, Hermogenes asks for something of the apostle's to carry, is given his staff, throws his books into the sea, and ends the story a penitent working wonders. The legend that celebrates the end of magic leaves its magician holding a charm against demons, his license transferred to the apostle.

The same centuries tried cunning women for charms and spoken cures, and in fourteen eighty-four a pope authorized inquisitors against witches in Germany. A healer and a parish priest could say the same command over the same salted water, and only one of them answered to a court for it. The Universal Life Church now ordains anyone online, of any belief and at no charge, and advertises the standing to perform legal weddings. A clergyman's title can be had for nothing by anyone who asks, and the words over the water keep the only force they ever had, the breath of the mouth that says them.

Charge: Say the blessing yourself, and owe the bishop nothing.

Rite (Christian): Fill a jug with tap water, hold your hand over it and say {I bless this water in my own name and need no one's leave}, then pour yourself a glass and drink it.

Rite (Catholic): Stir a pinch of salt into a bowl of water, hold your hand over it and say {Every unclean thing out of this salt and water, by my word and no bishop's}, then sprinkle your bed with it.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Lay both hands on a pitcher of water, the gesture your Church keeps for ordained men, and say {By no priesthood but my own, I bless this water}, then drink from it.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Fill a glass with water, raise it and say {Blessed be this water in my own name, and in no other}, then drink it down.

Rite (Jewish): Hold up a cup of water and say {Blessed am I, who make this water holy by my own word}, then pour it over your hands as the law does before bread.

Rite (Muslim): Say over a glass of water a short blessing of your own making that ends {by my name and by no other}, blow on the water as the ruqyah reciters do, and drink it.

Rite (No faith): Look up the free ordination a church offers anyone online and decline it, then hold your hand over a jug of tap water, say {I bless this by my own authority}, and drink a glass of it.

Question: What would you bless first if your mouth needed no license?

Closing: The font at the church door was filled from the same pipes as my kitchen sink, and blessed by a man no better than I am.
