# Day 343: Of the Garden Path

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Isaiah wants every valley raised and the crooked made straight, and the same book names a twisting thing it wants dead. In that day, it promises, the god will take his great sword to Leviathan the crooked serpent, the twisting one, and slay the dragon that lives in the sea. The book that loves a straight road also loves a dead serpent, and it paves a highway of holiness where even fools who walk it will not go astray.

Moses and Proverbs lay the same road. Moses forbids Israel to turn aside to the right hand or to the left, and Proverbs tells the son to ponder the path of his feet to turn neither way and to remove his foot from evil. The psalm uses the serpent's own root, aqalqallot, for the walkers who turn aside. Those who turn aside to their crooked ways, it says, the god will lead away with the workers of iniquity.

The straight road is the road with one destination, the one named at the start, and the crooked road goes where the eye goes, along the water and under the tree whose shape stopped you, and it comes in late with the afternoon spent on what the walker chose. Four books lay one map, the valleys filled, the feet kept to the road and the twisting serpent dead, and the book's own word for the walkers who turn aside is the serpent's word. A walker raised on that map goes from door to door for a lifetime and arrives having seen the pavement.

Charge: Take the long way, and let your eye steer.

Rite: Go today by the longest beautiful route you know to somewhere you must be, on foot if you can. Stop at the first thing that holds your eye, and stay until you have had your fill of it.

Question: What have you walked past for years because the straight way was quicker?

Closing: Isaiah paved his highway for fools who could not be trusted to wander, and I wander.
