# Day 36: Of Tablets

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The rabbis kept the pieces. In their reading the fragments of the first tablets, which Moses smashed below the mountain when he saw the calf and the dancing, were gathered up and laid in the ark beside the whole tablets that replaced them, so that Israel carried the broken law through the desert alongside the mended one. The book of Exodus says those first stones were the god's own work, the writing on them written by his finger. Moses threw them down anyway. The text records no rebuke.

One teacher in the Talmud read the god's later mention of the tablets Moses broke as a compliment, as though the voice in the cloud had thanked him for breaking them. The replacement was a cheaper production. Moses cut the stones himself, and the laws the god dictated on that second climb, just before the writing, run to feasts, firstborn sons and a kid boiled in its mother's milk.

The dread you feel at breaking an inherited rule was put there to guard it, and the record gives it no ground. The first edition of the law, the god's own handiwork, went to pieces under the hand of its own prophet, and heaven sent him back up the mountain with stone he had cut himself. Whoever breaks one of the copies for pleasure finds the dread sharpening the pleasure it was put there to prevent. The rules you keep after that are kept by your own choice, and the rest can travel as the first stones traveled, in a box on the shoulders of the devout.

Charge: Treat every commandment you grew up under as a draft.

Rite (Christian): Write six hundred sixty-six on your right palm in ink, the mark Revelation says no one may buy or sell without and the faithful must refuse, and buy yourself something you want with that hand.

Rite (Catholic): Pour the holy water you keep at the door down the sink, or bless a cup of tap water yourself and pour that out. Fill the font or a bottle with your best scent, and touch it to your throat before an hour of pleasure.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Dress for an hour among strangers in the clothes your upbringing called immodest, bare shoulders or bare knees, the cut you were taught to measure against the garment line, and enjoy the looks.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Raise a glass with someone you like and drink a toast to a rule you broke, the toasting custom The Watchtower traces to pagan offerings to the gods. {To the rule, in pieces.} Wine, or whatever you drink.

Rite (Jewish): Take the mezuzah from your front doorpost, or roll up a printed Shema if you never hung one, and fix it to the bathroom door, which the law leaves bare as no fit place for the Name. Run a long hot bath behind it.

Rite (Muslim): Say aloud a pleasure you will take within the hour and the time you will take it, plainly, without the inshallah the sura of the Cave commands for every plan, and take it at that time.

Rite (No faith): Write six hundred sixty-six across your forehead in eyeliner or ink, the other place Revelation sets the mark, and wear it at home through an hour of whatever pleasure you like best.

Sober alternative (Jehovah's Witness): Raise a glass of sparkling water or juice with someone you like and drink a toast to a rule you broke, the toasting custom The Watchtower traces to pagan offerings to the gods. {To the rule, in pieces.}

Question: Which inherited rule did you break for pleasure, and how did the old dread taste?

Closing: I owe the tablets the reverence Moses showed them at the foot of the mountain.
