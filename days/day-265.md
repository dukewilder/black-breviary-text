# Day 265: Of Multitudes

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Sound no trumpet when you give alms, Jesus tells the crowds and his disciples on the hillside. Give so secretly that your left hand does not know what your right is doing, since those who give for show have already had their reward. The father who sees in secret will repay the hidden giver, and in the Greek text the King James translators worked from, he repays openly.

The temple where he taught had thirteen offering chests shaped like rams' horns, and Mark has Jesus sit opposite the treasury, watch the crowd drop its money in, and call his disciples over to rank the gifts by what each giver had left. The preacher of secret giving sat and graded the gifts of strangers before his disciples.

On the King James text the sermon concedes that the givers with trumpets collected their applause in full, and it promises the secret giver a better audience later, in the open. The trumpet was forbidden so that one spectator could hold the whole gallery, a father who sees in secret and settles in public.

Being looked at is a pleasure. The wish to be admired for a thing done well is an appetite with every right to be fed. A carpenter who builds a good table wants one particular person to run a hand along it, and the hand he chose is the whole reward he was after. A giver who hides the gift for the father's sake is still giving for an audience, one he has never seen, and he dies without once watching a face change at what he gave.

Charge: Sound your own trumpet, and choose who hears it.

Rite: Ring a bell, or tap a spoon against a glass, and tell one person whose admiration you want the best thing you have done lately, in person and in full. Take the applause.

Question: Whose admiration have you gone without because you never let them see the work?

Closing: I want to be admired, and I take my applause from people who were in the room.
