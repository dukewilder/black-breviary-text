# Day 356: Of the Thousand Years

![Satan Bound for a Thousand Years](../plates/bound.jpg)

*Satan Bound for a Thousand Years. Jean Duvet, c. 1555.*

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

In Revelation an angel comes down with the pit's key and a great chain in his hand and seizes the dragon, that old serpent. He binds him for a thousand years, throws him into the abyss, and shuts and seals it over him. After the thousand years, the chapter adds, he must be let loose for a little while.

Christians have waited on timetables like it for nineteen centuries, and some of them acted on it. The Anabaptists who seized the German city of Munster in fifteen thirty-four proclaimed it the New Jerusalem and held it until the siege broke them a year later. In sixteen sixty-one some fifty Fifth Monarchy Men rose in London for the reign of King Jesus, held out four days, and saw their leader hanged, drawn and quartered within a fortnight. Followers of the Baptist preacher William Miller fixed the return of Christ for a day in eighteen forty-four, and the churches that grew from them kept the expectation when the day passed.

A household can live on the same calendar. Someone promises to stop drinking once the job settles, and every dinner and every plan is arranged around a change one person controls and has never once dated. Revelation at least gave its prisoner a term, a thousand years and then a little season, and the Millerites had a day to be disappointed on. A promised change with no date owes nothing on any day, and the patience spent on it is a loan made without terms, recoverable on demand.

Charge: Hold no seat for a promise that names no day.

Rite: Take back the key you lent, the access you granted or the seat you saved for whoever has made you wait longest, and give the place to someone who turned up. Where that person would answer with force, say nothing to them.

Question: What have you kept giving the one whose promised change you have waited on longest?

Closing: Whoever wants my patience can name the day, and I will be elsewhere on the one after.
