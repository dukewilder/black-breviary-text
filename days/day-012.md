# Day 12: Of Sodom

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Righteous is the word the second letter of Peter uses for Lot, a man worn down by lawlessness. When the men of the city surrounded his house and demanded the two strangers he was sheltering, he went out, shut the door behind him, and offered them his two virgin daughters instead, to do with as they pleased. The Quran has Lot tell the mob that his daughters are purer for them, and no verse reproaches him.

At dawn the angels took him by the hand, with his wife and daughters, and one gave the order not to look back. His wife looked back from behind him and became a pillar of salt. She is the one person in the story punished for something she did with her eyes, and what she saw was a burning city that still held the sons-in-law who had taken the warning for a joke and stayed.

Her keepers wanted her seen. The Wisdom of Solomon calls the pillar a monument to an unbelieving soul, the first letter of Clement says she remains to this day as a warning to the double-minded, and Josephus claims to have seen her himself. Jesus told his followers to remember her. A salt figure by the road exists to stop the next traveler turning his head.

Genesis gives one reason for the whole order, lest they be consumed, and a glance could neither spread the fire nor put it out, and the one thing the glance endangered was the judgment of whoever turned and counted the sons-in-law in the smoke. The god spared the man at the door and salted the woman who looked, and Peter and Clement kept that ranking. Lot's wife saw the city entire before she died, and her righteous husband, walking ahead, saw only the road to Zoar.

Charge: Turn round and judge the city you left.

Rite (Christian): Open your own Bible at the verse where Jesus says to remember Lot's wife, pour a heap of salt on the page, and grind it in with your thumb until the paper tears. {I remember her, and I look back.}

Rite (Catholic): Lay a holy card of a saint face up on the table, bury the face in salt, and as you leave the room turn round and look at it. {I looked back, and I am still standing.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Open your hymnbook, or a printout of it, at Press Forward, Saints, pour salt over the page until the title is gone, then name aloud the one teaching from your years in the Church you never let yourself question, and question it. {I looked back.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): The Watchtower has used Lot's wife to warn Witnesses against missing the world. Pour salt over a copy you own, or a printed page of one, name aloud one worldly thing you miss, {I miss it, and I am going back for it,} and do it today.

Rite (Jewish): Open your own Chumash at the chapter where Lot's wife looks back, pour salt over the page, and lick a pinch of it from the paper. {She looked, and so do I.}

Rite (Muslim): Copy in your own hand the verse where Lot tells the crowd his daughters are purer for them, write beneath it {A prophet who says this to a mob is no guide of mine}, and bury the page in salt.

Rite (No faith): Print the passage of Genesis where Lot's wife looks back, bury it in salt on a plate by the door, and every time you leave the room today, stop and look back at it.

Question: Which part of the faith you left have you never let yourself examine up close?

Closing: Heaven called the man who offered his daughters to a mob righteous, and I would rather stand by the road in salt.
