# Day 307: Of Unclean Things

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A great sheet comes down out of the sky by its four corners to a man praying on a rooftop in Joppa at noon. Inside it are four-footed animals of every sort, creeping things, wild beasts, birds of the air, and a voice tells Peter to get up, kill and eat. He refuses because nothing common or unclean has ever passed his lips. The voice answers that what the god has cleansed he must not call common, and the scene is played three times.

Peter took the vision to be about people. The Spirit then sent him to the house of a centurion named Cornelius, where he explained to a gentile household that his law had forbidden him to come. When he got back to Jerusalem the charge against him was that he had gone in to uncircumcised men and eaten with them. At the council that later settled what gentile converts owed the law, Peter asked why they would put on the disciples' necks a yoke their fathers could not bear, and the converts were released from nearly all of the old diet.

Leviticus had named the camel, the pig, the hare and the rock badger, and had classed the last two as chewing the cud, which neither does. A list that got its own animals wrong, and was waived for gentiles after a rooftop vision, still sorted tables for centuries, deciding who ate with whom, and trained the hand to pause before the mouth at every meal. A former Latter-day Saint can feel it over a cup of coffee and a former Muslim over a slice of ham, years after the belief has gone.

A pause that shortens each time it is overruled was never a law. Peter needed three repetitions from heaven to override his, and yours needs one forkful.

Charge: Eat the unclean thing and enjoy the hesitation.

Rite (Christian): Cook a rabbit or a hare from the butcher, the animal Leviticus credits with a cud it never chews and still calls unclean, and before the first bite say aloud {I call this clean myself.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Drink a full cup of strong coffee, the hot drink the Word of Wisdom is understood to forbid, and hold the first sip in your mouth until the flinch has passed.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Buy black pudding or blood sausage from a butcher or a British or Polish grocer and eat it to the last slice, one of the few foods the apostles' letter still kept off the gentile table and the Society taught Witnesses to refuse.

Rite (Jewish): Spread a sheet on your own table and eat catfish off it, a fish without scales, the abomination Leviticus names, then bless the meal in your own name, {Blessed am I, who chose this fish.}

Rite (Muslim): Spread a sheet over your own table and say over a plate of chicken {In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.} The Quran forbids meat dedicated to other than Allah. Eat it slowly.

Rite (No faith): Eat bacon or a ham roll and, before the first bite, say aloud {This is unclean, and it is mine.}

Question: Which food still makes your hand hesitate, and whose voice is in the hesitation?

Closing: Heaven let the sheet down three times and Peter never ate from it, and I eat at the first bite.
