# Day 345: Of the Second Bite

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The woman eats, and for the length of a clause she is the only one in the garden who has tasted the fruit. Then she gives some to her husband who is with her, he eats, and the eyes of both are opened, and they sew fig leaves into aprons. One has eaten and one has not, they share a bed and a bank account, and each looks across the table and waits.

Paul tells a believer married to an unbeliever to stay if the unbeliever is willing, and his ground is that the unbeliever is made holy, which the children are said to prove. The same rule runs both ways, for the brother with an unbelieving wife and the woman with an unbelieving husband, if the unbeliever is pleased to dwell with them. The unbelieving husband is made holy by the wife, he says, and the unbelieving wife by the husband, or else their children would be unclean, and as it is they are holy.

The child has no vote in it. A child is counted holy by the church on a parent's account before he can read the verse that does the counting, and the unbelieving parent is counted by the same rule. The one who has eaten owes that count nothing, and the child owes it nothing either. If the unbeliever departs, Paul says, let him go and the brother or sister is not under bondage in such cases, so the same chapter that counts the child holy also lets the household break.

Charge: Let no verse count your children holy for you.

Rite: Write to one church asking that your family's names come off its roll, cradle roll or mailing list, send it today, and say {Nobody counts us but us.}

Question: Which kindness in your house would you trust more if the church were out of the room?

Closing: Paul calls me holy by way of the one I married, and I decline the holiness and keep the marriage.
