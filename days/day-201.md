# Day 201: Of Descent

![The Descent on Geryon](../plates/inf126.jpg)

*The Descent on Geryon. Gustave Doré · Inferno, Canto XVII, 1861.*

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

When Pharaoh let the people go, Exodus says, the god did not lead them by the road through the land of the Philistines, although it was near. The people might change their minds when they saw war, he said, and go back to Egypt. He took them the long way round, through the wilderness toward the Red Sea.

The long way did not cure the longing. In Numbers the people weep for the fish they ate in Egypt for nothing, and for the cucumbers, the leeks and the garlic, and complain that they see nothing now but manna. Moses heard them weeping in the door of every tent. What pulled at them was the remembered food of the house of bondage, and no wonder from heaven tasted of it, and the god struck down the ones who lusted for meat at the place named for their craving.

A leaving meets the same craving. The holiday table and the Sunday company are the old faith's cucumbers, and a deserter who breaks every law in one month and puts no new pleasure in their place has only the wilderness and the memory of the table.

Each law broken should buy a pleasure that is the deserter's own and be given a few weeks to settle in before the next one falls. The god chose the long road to keep his people from turning back and fed them the same bread six mornings in seven. A deserter who pays himself at every stage has a pleasure of his own in hand when the craving comes.

Charge: Leave by steps you can keep taking.

Rite (Christian): Sleep through the hour of Sunday service, then go out for a long brunch and order the dish you like best.

Rite (Protestant): Play a hand of poker for money with friends, the cards and the gambling your church preached against together, and play it to win.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Eat the two meals a fast Sunday would take, cook the best late breakfast you can and eat it slowly, and keep the fast offering for yourself.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Buy one lottery ticket, the gambling the Watchtower tells Witnesses to shun, and spend the evening over a good dinner deciding what you would do with the jackpot.

Rite (Jewish): After a good dinner, switch on the lamp and the television and watch a film you love to the end.

Rite (Muslim): Play the music you love most through a long, good meal, the instruments a hadith warns the faithful against, and take your time over both.

Rite (No faith): Say grace over a meal, in your own name and to nobody, and begin eating before anyone else does. {Bless me, and the food I paid for.}

Question: Which comfort of the old faith still pulls hardest, and what pleasure could stand in its place?

Closing: Heaven fed its runaways manna and struck down the ones who craved meat, and I feed myself better than heaven did.
