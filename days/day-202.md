# Day 202: Of Courting

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Paul forbade the Corinthians to be unequally yoked with unbelievers, and the churches have read the yoke as a marriage ever since. A Latter-day Saint is raised to marry in the temple, a Witness to marry only in the Lord, and the Quran forbids a believing woman to an unbeliever. Paul wrote a second half for the believer already lying beside an outsider. She is to stay with him, since the unbelieving husband is sanctified by the wife, and who knows whether she will save him. Peter tells wives that a husband deaf to the word is to be won without a word, by their conduct.

Between them the two halves make the bed a post of the mission. Inside the faith it keeps the believer in, and across the line it is where the outsider is won, by a service attended together and a kindness that asks for nothing yet. One sect, the Children of God, followed the method to its end, and from nineteen seventy-four to nineteen eighty-seven its women went to bed with strangers to win them for Christ. The churches called it a cult and kept the verses. A believer who courts an apostate brings both halves along.

Leaving ends both offices. The apostate may now choose by what the old rules ranked below doctrine, a hand that stays a moment too long on the wrist, a laugh across the table that arrives in the chest before the mind has been consulted. A lover chosen by the pulse wants the body in front of them. A lover who chose you in order to win you wants the person you would become after the winning, and has already counted you among the sanctified, as Paul counted the husband.

Charge: Choose by appetite, and refuse to be anyone's mission.

Rite: Dress and scent yourself to be wanted and go out tonight wherever you would like to be looked at, or take the lover you already have out the same way. Say aloud before you leave {I go out to be wanted as I am.}

Question: What do you want in a lover now that no elder signs off on the match?

Closing: Let the god who meant to win me through a lover's good conduct come and court me himself.
