# Day 155: Of Dung

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Deuteronomy issues the army of Israel a paddle among its weapons. A soldier who needs to relieve himself is to go outside the camp and bury what comes from him, because the god walks in the middle of the camp and must see nothing indecent there and turn away. The same god dictates Ezekiel's bread down to its weight, six grains and pulses baked in the people's sight over human dung, as a sign of the defiled bread Israel would eat in exile. The prophet protests that he has never eaten anything unclean, and heaven relents so far as to allow him cow dung.

Through Malachi the god promises the priests who displease him that he will spread the dung of their feasts across their faces. Paul, ranking everything he once valued beside the knowledge of Christ, chose a word for it that politer translations still soften to rubbish. The King James and the Douay both kept dung.

Those raised on these books were told that foul speech defiles the mouth. Ephesians bans filthiness and coarse jesting among the saints, and Colossians orders filthy talk put out of the mouth altogether. The same canon hands the word to its god and his apostle to shame with, in a prophet's bread, on the faces of priests, in a verdict on a former life. The believer was trained to flinch at a word his own Bible throws. Said aloud over the open page, the word goes back to naming what every body makes each morning, the bodies of the priests in Malachi included.

Charge: Give the pulpit back its own dung.

Rite (Christian): Mix wheat, barley, beans, lentils, millet and emmer, or whatever of them you can find, as the god dictated to Ezekiel, shape a flat cake, bake it on a grill fired with pages torn from your own Bible, and eat it hot.

Rite (Catholic): Take your own missal or Bible, or a printout of the Douay-Rheims passage where Paul counts everything but dung, to the toilet and read it aloud there. At the last word, answer as at Mass. {Thanks be to God.}

Rite (Protestant): In your own Bible, write the word dung in marker over the whole page of Philippians where Paul counts his gains as rubbish, and read the page aloud. If yours already says dung, underline it twice and read it louder.

Rite (Orthodox): In a grill or an outdoor bowl, burn a piece of dried cow dung from a garden shop, the fuel the god allowed Ezekiel for his bread, in place of incense, and stand in the fumes with your icons if you keep any.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): With a stick dipped in garden manure, write dung across the title page of your own Book of Mormon, or a printout, for the god who promised dung on his priests' faces and gave every worthy man in your ward the priesthood. Leave it open to dry.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): In your own New World Translation, find where Jehovah tells Ezekiel to bake his bread over human excrement, write the plain word dung over it in marker, and read the verse aloud with the plain word in it.

Rite (Jewish): Open your own Tanakh at Malachi, where the god promises to spread the dung of the feasts on the priests' faces, smear a handful of garden manure across the page as he promised, and leave the book open to dry.

Rite (Muslim): Look up the direction of Mecca on a map or compass app, then relieve yourself facing it, which the hadith forbids, and say aloud {Let him see.} Tell no one.

Rite (No faith): Print the verses of Deuteronomy in which the god walks through the camp and must see nothing indecent, cut them into squares, and hang them by your toilet. Take one in with you, read it aloud, and bin it. {The god has seen enough.}

Question: Who taught you the disgust you still carry for the plain facts of your own body?

Closing: Heaven's favorite insult is mine now, and I say it at dinner.
