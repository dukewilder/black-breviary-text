# Day 313: Of Patmos

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Patmos is a dry island of some thirteen square miles off the coast of Asia, and a man named John says he was there for the word of God and the testimony of Jesus. Church tradition sends him there old, an exile under the emperor Domitian, writing across the water to the seven churches he could no longer visit, and on that rock he drew up a guest list for the end of the world.

Revelation sets two suppers on either side of the white rider's war. An angel tells John to write that those called to the marriage supper of the Lamb are blessed, and a few verses later another angel stands in the sun and calls every bird in the sky to the supper of the great god, to eat the flesh of kings and captains and mighty men, of horses and their riders. The exile furnished his feast so that the host chooses every guest and the uninvited are the meat.

Leaving a faith costs a whole table, the people who brought food when someone died and kept a chair for you at every feast, and the hunger that follows is a poor judge of guests. It takes whatever is set in front of it, the potluck among people who still pray for your return, the evenings given to bores and borrowers because any voice beats the quiet. That hunger keeps the Lamb's seating after the Lamb is gone, a chair beside whoever else was called and no say in who. A table laid for the one face you most want across from you feeds it better than a hall full of whoever was free.

Charge: Seat no one at your table out of hunger.

Rite: Choose the one person whose company you want most, invite them today, and feed them at your expense at the best table you can afford. Serve them first, saying {You are the one I wanted here.}

Question: Whose company have you been settling for, and whom would you rather have across from you?

Closing: Heaven's host picks every guest and feeds the rest to the birds, and I pick mine with my eyes open.
