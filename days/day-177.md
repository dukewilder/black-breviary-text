# Day 177: Of Vindication

![The Justification of Susanna](../plates/b147.jpg)

*The Justification of Susanna. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A young man named Daniel stopped the procession taking Susanna to her death and shouted that he was clear of her blood. Fools, he called the people, to condemn a daughter of Israel without examination and without knowing the truth, and he sent the court back to its seat. He had the two elders separated. To the first, after a rebuke, he said, if you saw her, under which tree. Under a mastic tree, the elder said. Daniel had the second brought in and asked the same thing, and he said under an evergreen oak. The whole case came apart in two answers, and the people turned on the pair and gave them the death they had meant for her.

Two men who had watched the same garden day after day could not agree on its trees. They had agreed on the lover, the shut doors and the maids sent off, the parts that carry a sentence, and neither had fixed the scenery, which nobody in the assembly thought to ask about.

A story a family or a congregation keeps about a member who left can be built the same way, in company, each teller nodding along with the rest, and nobody has asked it for a tree. Ask each teller alone where it happened, and write down the answers before any of them can compare notes. The first two that disagree are the first crack in the verdict, and a story that holds only while its tellers stand together has nothing under it but their word.

Charge: Question your accusers one at a time.

Rite: Write on a card what your old family or congregation says about you. Ask each person who repeats it, alone, where they saw it happen, and write each name and answer under it. Bury the card under the nearest tree, blanks and all.

Question: Which story about you has only ever been told in company?

Closing: My accusers agree on the story and part ways on the tree.
