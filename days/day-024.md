# Day 24: Of Cattle

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The fifth plague falls on the horses and the asses and the camels, the herds and the flocks, and Exodus reports that all the livestock of Egypt died while not one beast belonging to Israel was touched. Then the text forgets its own report. The hail two plagues later is aimed at the cattle still out in the field, and the last night kills the firstborn of the cattle along with the firstborn of men, so that the same herds die three times over in a single argument between a god and a king.

The god promises to make a division between the cattle of Israel and the cattle of Egypt. The same god wrote a sabbath into his law so that the ox and the ass might rest, and he knew what a working beast was owed, and he spent the herds of Egypt anyway, in a quarrel no cow had any part in.

At the end of Jonah he argues for Nineveh because it holds more than a hundred and twenty thousand who do not know their right hand from their left, and also much cattle. Nineveh's beasts were spared to make a point to Jonah, and Egypt's were killed to make one to Pharaoh.

Exodus counts the herds as property the god could spend and Jonah counts them as lives he could spare, and an animal that comes to your door at its hour for food has never read either book. The bowl by your door is the only place in this story where the beast is paid.

Charge: Give an animal your regard for its own sake.

Rite (Christian): Bless a few strips of plain cooked meat, no bones or seasoning, then give them to a dog, yours or a friend's with its owner's leave, against the saying that what is holy is not for dogs. Sit on the floor while it eats.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Spend an hour with a dog or a cat, yours or a friend's with its owner's leave, idling on the floor beside it, the idle time the Doctrine and Covenants forbids, with your whole attention on it.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Spend an hour of the time you were raised to give to field service on a dog or a cat, yours or a friend's with its owner's leave, sitting with it and giving it your whole attention.

Rite (Jewish): Take a dog, yours or a friend's with its owner's leave, off its leash to run in a yard or an off-leash area, against the Mishnah's rule that a dog be kept tied with chains, and play with it until it tires.

Rite (Muslim): Spend an hour with a dog its owner lets you meet, in the house if you are invited, and feed it from your hand before you part, against the hadith in Bukhari's collection that has angels refuse a house with a dog.

Rite (No faith): Print the passage about the fifth plague and put it under the food bowl of a dog or cat, or a dish of seed for birds, so a beast gets some use from the god who killed Egypt's herds.

Question: What have you given in return to the creature that has trusted you longest?

Closing: A god who spent Egypt's herds to win an argument ranks below the dog asleep at my door.
