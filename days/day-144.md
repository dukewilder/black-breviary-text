# Day 144: Of the God of the Land

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The Assyrians deported much of the northern kingdom and resettled its towns with people from Babylon and four other cities. Because the newcomers did not fear the god of the place, the second book of Kings reports, he sent lions among them, which killed some of them. The Assyrian king, told that the nations he had planted did not know the manner of the god of the land, sent back one of the deported priests, who settled at Bethel and taught them how they ought to fear the Lord.

The chapter never says the lions stopped. It records the complaint, the diagnosis and the priest, then turns to the gods each nation went on making for the high places, the Sepharvites burning their children to theirs, all of them fearing the god of the land and serving their own, the text says, to this day. The remedy went into the record and the result did not.

The god's own law had already explained the lions. In Exodus he promises to drive out the Canaanites little by little, lest the emptied land fill with wild beasts, and Sargon's annals boast of twenty-seven thousand carried off from Samaria in one campaign. Hill country stripped of its farmers breeds predators, and the same country plowed and herded again pushes them back to the thickets, with a priest at Bethel or without one.

A former believer keeps the same books. The bedtime prayer said just in case, the cross signed as the plane drops and the medal on the mirror are each a remedy with no result on file. Behind each stands a real danger with a real cause, and while the gesture makes the cause feel handled, the cause stays where it was, the bald tire on the car and the dead battery in the smoke alarm.

Charge: Retire one charm and fix the cause it stood in for.

Rite (Christian): Take the Bible from the glovebox or the nightstand, where it sits as a guard, and put it in the recycling. Then put a first-aid kit in the place it kept.

Rite (Catholic): Take the saint's medal from your mirror or key ring, and hang in its place the receipt for the repair you have put off, a tire, a brake check or a fire alarm battery, paid today.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Tear from your own Doctrine and Covenants, or a printout of it, the promise that the destroying angel will pass by those who keep the Word of Wisdom, throw it away, and fix a carbon monoxide alarm outside the bedrooms.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Write on a slip the prayer for Jehovah's protection you say before a long drive, burn it in the sink, and before you next drive check the tire pressure, the spare and the brake lights.

Rite (Jewish): Open your mouth to the Satan, as the Talmud forbids, and say aloud the misfortune you most dread, with no spitting and no ward after it. Then take the real precaution against it that you have put off.

Rite (Muslim): Say aloud your plans for today as flat certainties, with no inshallah after any of them, though the Quran says to add it. Then make the first one certain the practical way, by booking it or paying for it now.

Rite (No faith): Tape the luck-piece you keep, or any coin, to your fire alarm, then press the test button until it sounds. Say {This one works.} If it stays silent, fit a new battery before you sleep.

Question: What danger do you still answer with a gesture instead of a repair?

Closing: A god who teaches manners with lions gets my deadbolt and none of my prayers.
