# Day 249: Of the Wilderness

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Mark describes what John the Baptist wore before quoting a word he said. A garment of camel hair with a leather belt at the loins recalled the costume the second book of Kings gives to Elijah, the prophet promised to come back before the great and dreadful day, so anyone who knew the scriptures could read the man's claim off his body. His food made a second claim. Locusts and wild honey could be had for the gathering, and locusts are among the few insects the law of Moses permits, so he ate clean off the open ground. He had dressed and fed himself clean out of the economy of Jerusalem, and Matthew has him call the Pharisees and Sadducees who came down to the river a brood of vipers to their faces.

The white shirt for the sacrament table and the hem measured against the knee were chosen by a committee to say something on your behalf, which was that you belonged and would embarrass nobody. Plenty of people leave and go on dressing for that committee for years, in a careful drab.

A wardrobe is a sentence written on the body each day, and John's said that its owner had left the city and its tailors behind. What others wrote on you can go to the shears. The red coat or the silk shirt you were told was too loud is the garment that says something you wrote yourself, and a room that looks twice is reading it. John wore camel hair to the Jordan, and the crowds went out to look.

Charge: Fire the committee that dresses you.

Rite: Put on today the garment you wanted and someone you no longer answer to forbade, buying it if you must, and wear it out of the house. Cut up or sell what you wore to please them.

Question: Which clothes in your closet were chosen by people you no longer answer to?

Closing: I wear red past the door of the church that taught me drab.
