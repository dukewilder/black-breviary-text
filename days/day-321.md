# Day 321: Of Death

![Death: My Irony Surpasses All Others](../plates/death.jpg)

*Death: My Irony Surpasses All Others. Odilon Redon · To Gustave Flaubert, 1889.*

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The oldest verified human being on record was a Frenchwoman who died in nineteen ninety-seven at a hundred and twenty-two. She smoked from the age of twenty-one until she was a hundred and seventeen, took a small glass of port after meals, and sometimes ate a kilo of chocolate in a week. Ecclesiastes, which tradition credits to Solomon in his old age, had long before said what the three religions of the book later built doctrines to deny. The same fate meets the human being and the beast, and all return to dust. The dead know nothing and have no further reward, and their love and their hatred and their envy perished with them.

Its counsel is to eat your bread with joy and drink your wine with a glad heart, since there is no work and no knowledge in the grave. The longevity regimes skip the Preacher. They treat death as a defect awaiting a patch, and their devotees keep a rule stricter than a monk's, the supplements counted out by the dozen and the blood drawn and charted on a schedule, all of it spent on a reprieve that nobody has yet been granted, the woman with the chocolate included.

Health is worth having for the life it allows, and the regime spends that life buying more of it, the bread refused and the wine poured away for a decade nobody can promise. The dead of every regime lie as still as the dead of every feast. The Preacher gave his order to eat with joy to people who would die early or late, and he meant the bread for the years when the eater still has teeth.

Charge: Eat today what the regime is saving for later.

Rite: Say aloud your exact age and then {I will die}. Then slowly eat warm bread, fresh from the oven or the bakery and thick with salted butter and honey, the loaf you have been refusing.

Question: What are the pleasures you postponed worth, set against the longer life they bought?

Closing: The regime charts its blood every quarter, and I mean to die with honey on my fingers.
