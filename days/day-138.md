# Day 138: Of the Chariot

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Elijah tried three times to leave his disciple behind, at Gilgal and at Bethel and at Jericho, and three times Elisha refused. The sons of the prophets kept reminding him that his master would be taken that day, and he told them to hold their peace. At the Jordan Elijah rolled up his mantle and struck the water, and it parted, and they crossed on dry ground. A chariot of fire with horses of fire came between them, and Elijah went up by a whirlwind. The mantle fell. Elisha, who had asked for a double portion of his master's spirit, picked it up.

Fifty men of the prophets' guild searched the hills for three days for the body and found nothing. Elijah had lived on ravens' bread at a brook and a widow's last handful of meal, slept under a broom tree and in a cave, and the one luxury the text allows him is the manner of his leaving. Jewish custom still keeps a chair for him at every circumcision, set for a guest who left no grave.

Many exits in a life are made apologizing, out of parties through a string of excuses and out of jobs through months of resentment, and what the room keeps of the person is the trailing end. Elijah went in a single stroke, with the sons of the prophets watching from across the river, and the mantle he dropped went to the one disciple who had refused to be left behind. Leaving is the part of a life that other people carry away, and the man of the ravens' bread is remembered for his horses.

Charge: Give your exits the money you would spend on an entrance.

Rite: Leave one shop, cafe or room today by the grandest ride you can pay for, a hired car or a taxi called to the door, and say aloud on the step, as you go, the line you wrote for today's question.

Question: What line would you say at the door if horses of fire were waiting outside?

Closing: I owe a room my company while I stay in it and nothing on the way out.
