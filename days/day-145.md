# Day 145: Of the Herdsman

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Amaziah, priest of the royal shrine at Bethel, sent word to King Jeroboam that Amos had said the king would die by the sword. He told Amos to go home to Judah, eat his bread there and prophesy there, since Bethel was the king's sanctuary and a royal house. Amos answered that he was no prophet and no prophet's son. He was a herdsman and a dresser of sycamore figs, and the god had taken him from following the flock.

Amaziah's advice assumes that prophecy is a living, to be practiced where the customers are one's own. Amos refused the title along with the trade. Amaziah drew his bread from the king's chapel, and the one voice in it that drew none was the voice that foretold a sword against the king's house. What he brought in place of a lineage was his work. The sycamore fig had to be gashed with a blade while it grew, an art the Egyptians used to bring on ripening, so a dresser walked the rows wounding each fruit at its moment. His book is built from that ground, a basket of summer fruit whose Hebrew name sounds like the word for the end, and a remnant of Samaria likened to what a shepherd pulls from a lion's mouth, two legs or a piece of an ear.

The nurse who watched a protocol fail and the member who counted the offering both know what the credentialed are paid to overlook, and a title would only have proved they belonged to the house. Amaziah's weapons were the king's house and a charge of conspiracy, and a man who wants nothing from the shrine can answer them with his trade and pronounce sentence on the priest in the king's own sanctuary.

Charge: Correct the credentialed from what you know firsthand.

Rite: Write to someone who holds a title over your field, a manager, a pastor, an official or an author, the correction you learned on the job, in three sentences. Sign it with your trade and your years, such as a mechanic of twelve years, and send it today.

Question: What does your own work know that the titled in your field are paid to overlook?

Closing: A priest who eats the king's bread calls the truth a conspiracy, and I believe the man with sap on his hands.
