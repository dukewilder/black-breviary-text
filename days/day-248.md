# Day 248: Of Prodigies

![Jesus Questions the Doctors](../plates/b166.jpg)

*Jesus Questions the Doctors. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

After the infant in the temple and before the man at the river, Luke allows Jesus one scene, at twelve. His parents are a day out of Jerusalem on the road home from the Passover before they notice he is missing. They search three days. They find him in the temple sitting among the teachers, listening to them and asking them questions, while everyone who hears him marvels at his answers. When his mother says that she and his father have been looking for him in anguish, he replies that they ought to have known he would be in his Father's house.

Then he goes home with them, is obedient, and drops out of the record for about eighteen years. A second-century gospel went back and filled the years before the visit with a boy who strikes a playmate dead for bumping him and blinds the neighbors who complain. Sent to school, he lectures his teacher, telling Zacchaeus that a man ignorant of the nature of alpha has no business teaching anyone beta, and Zacchaeus gives up the boy in shame.

Luke's boy listens to the teachers and asks them questions. The legend retells the temple scene and adds that he was shutting the mouths of the elders and teachers, and its schoolroom shows what that meant, a child who knows everything already and teaches his teachers by humiliating them. Piety preferred the prodigy who never needs to ask, and it goes on preferring him in every room where an expert speaks with more assurance than knowledge and is answered with nods. Luke closes the scene with his boy increasing in wisdom. The legend's boy could strike a teacher down and had nowhere left to grow, since he had already announced that he knew the alpha.

Charge: Ask aloud, in the room, what you do not know.

Rite: Ask the most assured speaker within reach today, a colleague or a salesman, one question about a claim of theirs that you do not understand. If the answer slides away, ask again, and wait for the second answer.

Question: Whose assurance have you been nodding at for years, and what would you ask it?

Closing: I left the god who was born finished, and I took the boy's questions with me.
