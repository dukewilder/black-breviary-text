# Day 65: Of Staying

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Orpah kissed her mother-in-law and went home, and the book of Ruth never names her again. Naomi, widowed in Moab and then bereaved of both sons, had set out for Judah with her two Moabite daughters-in-law, and on the road she told them, more than once, to go back to their mothers' houses and find husbands there, since she had no more sons to give them, and both wept. Orpah took the advice. Ruth refused it in the speech weddings still borrow, your people my people and your god my god, and the narrative follows her to Bethlehem, to a rich kinsman, and through her son to a great-grandson named David.

What Orpah went back to was her mother's house, her own people and her own gods, and what she gave up was a dead husband's mother who had released her and asked nothing back. The book gives her no speech of her own. Her one line is spoken in chorus with Ruth, that they would go on with Naomi to her people, before Naomi talked them out of it.

The rabbis could not leave a silent woman alone. The Talmud made her the mother of Goliath and his three giant brothers, paid out, it says, for the four tears she shed at parting, and another passage has David's nephew Abishai kill her. The book gave her no reason, and the four giants of her line fell to Ruth's great-grandson David, while the one speech the Talmud gives her asks a young man to bring her spindle.

Charge: Explain nothing to the relatives who already wrote your part.

Rite: Hug the relative who let you go with no terms, tell them in a dozen words what you left or changed, and leave the story there. Answer nothing the others send about it.

Question: Which relative let you go without a sermon, and what do they deserve to hear from you?

Closing: I left with a kiss, and every line my family adds to the story is its own.
