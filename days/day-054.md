# Day 54: Of the Virtuous Pagans

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

In fourteen forty-two the Council of Florence copied a sixth-century bishop's sentence into its decrees nearly word for word. Fulgentius of Ruspe had told the faithful to hold most firmly and doubt in no way that pagans and Jews, heretics and schismatics, whoever ended this life outside the Catholic Church, would go into the everlasting fire prepared for the devil and his angels. By the decree's own logic the great dead of antiquity stood with the rest, the lawgivers and the astronomers, dead outside the Church and most of them before it existed.

By a report in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet told a man who asked after his dead father that the father was in the Fire, and called him back to add that his own father was there too, and another report has him ask leave to pray for his mother's forgiveness and be refused. Against his colleague Rabbi Eliezer, Rabbi Joshua held that the righteous of the nations have a share in the world to come, so that the rabbis disputed the line within a single generation. A pope allowed hope to the invincibly ignorant in eighteen sixty-three, and a council in nineteen sixty-four opened salvation to those ignorant of the gospel through no fault of their own.

The line fell where birth and date put it. Every faith that drew it has quarreled over where it falls. A sentence its own judges keep revising was written by them and handed down as the god's. Under it stood the grandmother who prayed in another tongue and the teacher who believed nothing and taught you to think, condemned by the same accident that spared you. Whatever they were owed, they collected in this life, from the people who knew them, and the only verdict on them that anyone living can still pass is yours.

Charge: Honor one good unbeliever above every council that damned them.

Rite (Christian): Name a great pagan the Church consigned to the fire, a lawgiver or a physician of the old world, and say grace over your next meal to them by name in place of the god. {Thanks to you, and none to the god who damned you.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Pour a cup of water over your own head and say the name of a good person who died outside the faith, a baptism for the dead done by no priesthood holder in no temple. {I stand for you, and no prophet shuts you out.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Spend an hour with a person the elders would have you shun, or one the congregation would call worldly, if they welcome it, and tell them to their face that they are among the best people you know.

Rite (Jewish): Eat a dish cooked start to finish by non-Jewish hands, food the rabbis forbade for that reason, in honor of a righteous gentile you would seat above any sage.

Rite (Muslim): Name a good person who died outside Islam and ask mercy for them aloud, the request the Prophet was refused leave to make for his own mother. {God's mercy on you, whatever the book says.}

Rite (No faith): Put a photograph of the best unbeliever you ever knew, or their name on a card, where you will see it, and say aloud what they were worth, every verdict of every council on them overruled.

Question: Whom did your faith condemn that you would trust over any council?

Closing: I would stand outside with the astronomers before I would stand inside with the men who sentenced them.
