# Day 349: Of Sin and Death

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

James draws death's family tree in a single verse, desire conceiving and bearing sin, and sin, grown to its full size, bearing death, so that death, in this telling, descends from appetite, a hand reaching, a mouth open.

Paul made the genealogy doctrine. Death came into the world through one man's sin, he told the Romans, and the wages of sin are death, and from that letter the Church built its teaching that death entered through sin, an intruder admitted by a crime. Genesis makes the man from dust before any bite, and when it passes sentence it says he is dust and will return to dust, which describes his material. Genesis does threaten death on the day of the bite, but when the sentence falls the god posts a guard on the tree of life, lest the man eat of it and live for ever, which treats the man as mortal until he is fed from that tree, so the verse takes from him the remedy and leaves the dust.

A watch counts the day's steps and an app weighs every meal against a target, and the discipline promises years added at the far end for appetites refused now. It is the old genealogy with the theology filed off, death still descended from appetite and still to be held off by refusal. Meanwhile butter browns at the edge of a pan, and a thick cut of meat takes its salt and gives up its fat, and the whole kitchen fills with a smell no tracker scores. Whatever years refusal at the table adds to a life, the refuser pays for them with a thinner supper every night on the way to the same grave.

Charge: Eat one meal today consulting nothing but your mouth.

Rite: Cook the dish you have been rationing against a longer life, the butter and cream and marbled meat, and eat every bite slowly at a laid table, with the watch off your wrist and the tracking app closed for the day.

Question: Which dish have you been refusing yourself, and what did the refusal promise?

Closing: The undertaker keeps the only count of my days that matters, and I refuse to do his bookkeeping at dinner.
