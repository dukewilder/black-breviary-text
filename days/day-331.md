# Day 331: Of Rivals

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Leah called her first son Reuben, because the god had seen her misery and now her husband would love her. She called the second Simeon, because the god had heard that she was unloved, and the third Levi, because this time at last her husband would be joined to her. Her sister Rachel, the loved wife and the barren one, gave her maid to Jacob and named the maid's second son Naphtali, saying she had wrestled great wrestlings with her sister and had won.

Leah bought a night with him from Rachel with the mandrakes her firstborn had found in the field. For years the two women measured their lives against each other in the currency their world prized most, which was sons, while the man they competed for went on preferring Rachel and her children. Rachel died in childbirth on the road and named the boy son of my sorrow.

The sons were counted as points, and Jacob's open preference for Rachel's firstborn handed his older sons the scoreboard. Out of envy they sold him to a caravan for twenty pieces of silver. In Egypt Joseph, the son the sisters had been counting against each other, ended the score at last by feeding the brothers who had sold him, telling them they had thought evil against him and that he would nourish them. Rachel was buried by the road to Bethlehem and Leah in the family cave at Machpelah, and the only man who put the account down was the one it had cost most.

Charge: Keep score openly, and salute only the fair win.

Rite: Tell the sibling, cousin, colleague or friend who beat you fairly at something, in one sentence, that they won it. In the next sentence name the contest you mean to take from them.

Question: What would you have to win to call settled the score you still keep with someone you grew up beside?

Closing: My children will inherit my house and none of my feuds.
