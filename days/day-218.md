# Day 218: Of Transformation

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Balaam, hired to curse Israel and made to bless it, tells the king who hired him that the god is no man, that he should lie, nor a son of man, that he should change his mind. The schools made it doctrine. Any change in the god would mean he had lacked something or lost it, so he was declared immutable, and Malachi has him say it in his own voice, I am the lord, I change not.

Exodus records him changing his mind. Down on the plain the people had cast a calf of gold, and the god told Moses to let him alone so that his wrath could burn hot against them and consume them, and he would make a great nation of Moses instead. Moses argued. He asked what the Egyptians would say, he reminded the god of the oath sworn to Abraham, and the lord repented of the evil he had thought to do to his people.

First Samuel puts both claims in one chapter. The god tells Samuel it repents him that he made Saul king, and when Saul, who had kept the Amalekite king alive with the fattest of his sheep, begs pardon for it, Samuel tells him the lord has torn the kingdom from him and that the Strength of Israel will not lie or repent, for he is not a man, that he should repent. Six verses later the chapter closes on the lord repenting that he had made Saul king.

The changeless god is brought out against Balak and against Saul, the men who wanted a sentence changed, and put away whenever the god changes one himself. Whoever changes course is doing what the god did on Sinai and again over Saul, and needs the congregation's leave no more than the god needed Saul's.

Charge: Change your mind without asking leave.

Rite (Christian): Change your hair today against the teaching you grew up with, cropped short if you were told long hair is a woman's glory, dyed a loud color if modesty was preached, and finish within the hour. Look in a mirror and say {Mine.}

Rite (Jewish): Take a razor to the corners of your head, the temples Leviticus forbids a man to round, and say {These are mine.} If you are a woman, go bareheaded with your hair loose for an hour, the covering the law asked of you laid down.

Rite (Muslim): Shave off the beard a hadith tells men to let grow, or if you are a woman go out with your hair uncovered for an hour, and say to the mirror {Mine.}

Rite (No faith): Cut off a hank of your own hair at the roots with the sharpest scissors you own, drop it in the trash, and say {This was mine to cut.}

Rite (Catholic): Cross out your baptismal and confirmation names on a printed copy of your certificate, or on a page headed with both, write over them the name you would have chosen, and say {Mine.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Put on a second pair of earrings, clip-ons if need be, or a first pair if you are a man, the body piercing the First Presidency and the Twelve discourage, and wear them for the evening, saying once to the mirror {Mine.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Braid your hair, or pin a braid in, and wind gold wire through it, the adornment Paul's first letter to Timothy tells women to leave off, and wear it out for the evening, saying once at the door {Mine.}

Question: What have you kept the same about yourself only because changing it would have to be explained?

Closing: A god who repented of his own king has no business holding me to the faith I was raised in.
