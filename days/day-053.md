# Day 53: Of Lament

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The chapter that gives Jephthah's daughter to her father's vow closes on other girls. Four days in every year, the book of Judges says, the daughters of Israel went out to lament her, and with that custom the chapter ends. She has no name in the book. Her father receives six years as judge of Israel and a grave in Gilead, and she receives what the other girls gave her.

Her one request, once she knew what he had sworn, was for time, two months to go up and down on the mountains with her companions, and she spent it with the girls and away from his house. After her the daughters of Israel made it a custom to give four days a year to someone who could repay nothing, and for as long as the custom lasted the vow that took her body could not take her memory.

Her father swore her away before she came out of the door. The debts a faith teaches run upward, to the god who gave you life and to the parents who answer to him, and this chapter shows one of them collected. The one debt in the chapter that no father enforced ran sideways, from women to a girl who could repay nothing, and nobody owed it until it was given. Whoever gave you days when your upbringing refused you holds that debt now, the friend who drove six hours, the cousin who lied to the elders for you. That debt outranks every claim the household made on you, since the household laid its claims on her and the girls laid this one on themselves.

Charge: Pay the friends who kept you before anyone who claimed you.

Rite: Call the one person who stood by you when your family would not, and give them a whole day of yours on a date they pick. {You came when they would not, and this day is yours.}

Question: Who gave you their time when you had nowhere else to go, and what did it cost them?

Closing: My debts run to the people who came, and the ones who claimed me can wait in line.
