# Day 110: Of Councils

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Constantine summoned the bishops to Nicaea in the year three hundred and twenty-five, three hundred and eighteen of them by the traditional count. He brought them there on the imperial post to settle whether the son was of one substance with the father. The emperor who paid their passage went unbaptized until his deathbed. The creed was written down, and by the old account every bishop signed it but two, who went into exile with Arius.

The first Christians settled their worst quarrel the same way. At Jerusalem the apostles and elders heard Peter, then Barnabas and Paul, on whether gentile converts had to be circumcised, and James gave his judgment. The letter they sent to the gentile brethren in Antioch and Syria and Cilicia announced the ruling as what seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us. The bodies in question belonged to gentile men, and no gentile speaks anywhere in the chapter.

Both assemblies ruled on lives that had no voice in the room. Nicaea fixed what every Christian must believe, for churches from Spain to Persia. Jerusalem settled what every gentile convert must keep at table and in bed, spared his flesh by a vote he never cast, and put heaven's name on the verdict ahead of its own. A decision circled for a month is usually a council with one chair and too many members, a parent or a partner claiming a vote, when the only one with standing to convene it is the person whose life it governs. The arguments deserve a hearing once, at their strongest, as Jerusalem heard Paul. After that the ruling stands under one name, in the first place on the page, where Jerusalem put the Holy Ghost.

Charge: Convene your own council, and rule for the chair.

Rite: Name the one person who claims a vote in a decision that is yours alone. Write your ruling on a sheet and sign it, then tell them the ruling in one sentence and do not answer a reply. Act on it today.

Question: What ruling will you give the one who claims a vote in a decision that is yours alone?

Closing: The emperor paid the bishops' fares and got his creed. I pay my own way and get my life.
