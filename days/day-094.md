# Day 94: Of Crowds

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

One hundred and thirty-three million, three hundred and six thousand, six hundred and sixty-eight angels fell with the adversary, by a reckoning attributed to a Franciscan friar at Salamanca in the fifteenth century. Revelation gives a proportion instead. The dragon's tail draws down a third of the stars of heaven, and his angels are cast out with him.

Whatever else the fallen lost, they fell in company. People who leave a strict congregation usually fall alone, sometimes announced from the front of the room in a single sentence to people they have known since childhood, and within a season they can lose every friend they had and the dozen people who would have brought food after a death. Many spend years taking that loss for the fixed price of leaving, because the people who set it said so from the front of the same room.

The fallen paid a different price. Their company was made by one refusal, made once, and it sent them no bill afterward. A congregation keeps its members by billing them every week, in Sunday hours on a hard pew and in shame said aloud to an elder or a priest, while a bond formed by refusing the same thing is paid off on the day it forms. Whatever its members keep up after that, the long dinners and the private jokes, they keep because it pleases them, and they drop it the week it stops. Three people who left the same church and meet on their own terms keep a table no elder can announce them off.

Charge: Find the few who refused what you refused.

Rite: Invite one or two people who left what you left to the best dinner you can afford, and pay for the whole table. When the plates come, say {We fell together.}

Question: Whose company did leaving cost you, and whose has it brought you since?

Closing: A third of heaven went down behind one refusal, and I would rather eat with my third than kneel with the rest.

Sources quoted:
- attributed to a Franciscan friar at Salamanca (Attributed to Alonso de Espina, Fortalitium Fidei)
- a third of the stars of heaven (Rev 12:4)
- cast out with him (Rev 12:9)
