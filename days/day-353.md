# Day 353: Of Rules

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The revelation the Latter-day Saints call the Word of Wisdom announces in its second verse that it is sent by way of greeting, not by commandment or constraint. It names wine, strong drink, tobacco and hot drinks as unfit for the body. Within ninety years keeping it had become a condition of entry to the temple, the hot drinks had been read as coffee and tea, and a bishop asked about all of it before he signed a recommend.

The office that asks those questions is older than the advice. At Sinai the people heard the thunder and the trumpet, stood far off, and begged Moses to speak to them in the god's place, promising to hear and do whatever he brought back, so long as the god did not speak to them and kill them. Moses had carried messages up the mountain before, and their fear made the errand an office, the god telling him to stand by and receive every commandment for them. The god had spoken the ten words to the whole assembly himself and added no more, and everything after came down through Moses, until the rabbis counted six hundred and thirteen commandments.

A go-between has little to sell but the difference between advice and law. Advice is taken or left in private and leaves him idle. A law can be asked about at a door, and a failure of it can be confessed to him, repaired through him and certified by his signature, so a counsel that passes through his hands can come out the other side as a condition of entry. A rule set for your own reasons is kept or dropped at your own table, where nobody holds a pen over a recommend.

Charge: Take what you like and answer no doorkeeper for it.

Rite: Brew the best coffee or tea you can buy, the hot drinks a bishop asks about, and drink it slowly at your own table. If anyone asks whether you keep a rule you never chose, say {I keep my own counsel.}

Question: What did you once do freely that someone now asks about before letting you in?

Closing: Advice sent by greeting turned into a gate pass, and I was never asking to be let in.
