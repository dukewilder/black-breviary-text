# Day 118: Of the Fleece

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Gideon had his commission from an angel at the winepress and a sign already given, fire from a rock that consumed his offering, and he asked for another. He laid a fleece of wool on the threshing floor and asked that in the morning it be wet with dew while the ground stayed dry, and it was, and he wrung a bowlful of water out of it. Then he asked again the other way round, begging the god not to be angry with him, the fleece dry and the ground wet. That too was granted.

He had now asked for three signs, the last with an apology already attached, and before the battle the god volunteered a fourth, sending him down by night to overhear a Midianite tell his dream of a barley loaf that tumbled into the camp and flattened a tent. Every sign came back yes. The victory went to three hundred jars smashed at once and three hundred torches flaring in the dark, and the man who had needed proof upon proof before he would lift a sword came home holding a record of a god who answered whenever he was asked.

He made the Midianites' golden earrings into an ephod, the priestly vest used elsewhere to put questions to the god, and set it up in his own town, where, the book says, all Israel went whoring after it, and it became a snare to Gideon and to his house. The man who asked for proof three times made himself a standing way to ask, out of spoil he never needed, and the house that once wanted a sign for every step now kept an oracle in the yard.

Charge: Refuse the sign and take the next step.

Rite (Christian): Open your own Bible at random for a sign, the divination a council of Gaul condemned in the fifth century, read aloud the verse it gives you, then tear the page out and line the kitchen trash can with it.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Pick one omen you have been waiting on, a dream, a verse that fell open, a coincidence, and decline it aloud as a reason. Then do the thing it was supposed to permit.

Rite (Jewish): Open your own Chumash at random for an answer, as a diviner would, read the verse aloud, then set the book on the floor as a doorstop for the rest of the day.

Rite (Muslim): Cast an omen as the arrow-casters of old did, the divination the Quran forbids. Flip a coin on the choice before you, write the verdict on paper and tear it up, then choose by the facts you have. Do it where no one sees.

Rite (Catholic): Say aloud {Send me a rose as a sign}, as the rose novena asks, then buy yourself a bunch of roses and set them on your table, the sign made by your own hand.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Take five pages from the journal or notes where you recorded answers you prayed for, the burnings in the bosom and the stupors of thought, or write one such answer from memory, and tear them into shreds.

Rite (No faith): Delete the horoscope or fortune app from your phone, or throw out the tarot deck you consult, and make one choice today you would have run past it.

Question: What sign are you waiting on before you do what you already know?

Closing: I will take my next step without a sign, and the door I open will be one I chose.
