# Day 227: Of Preachers

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Five words in Hebrew, forty days more and Nineveh is overthrown, are all the preaching the book gives Jonah. The people believed the god, and the king rose from his throne, sat down in ashes and decreed a fast for man and beast, herd and flock, all of them in sackcloth and none to eat or drink water. The god saw it and relented, and Nineveh stood.

Jonah took the reprieve as an injury. He asked to die, then went out east of the city and built himself a booth to watch it. One midrash says he had fled in the first place because an earlier warning of his had been averted by repentance and he had been called a false prophet for it. Deuteronomy sets the test he dreaded. A word spoken in the god's name that does not come to pass is one the god never gave, and the people are told not to be afraid of the prophet who spoke it.

A prophet of ruin needs the ruin, because his name rides on it. The commentator who sells the collapse of the currency and the civil war a year or two off sits in the same booth, and his listeners pay him in subscriptions and in the evenings they spend afraid on his schedule.

The cattle in that sackcloth had no wickedness to turn from and could not hear the five words, and their troughs stood empty for a sentence passed on their owners. Every evening given to a prophet of ruin is a day of that fast, kept by a listener with no sin on the account, in front of an empty trough.

Charge: Take back the hours you lent to dread.

Rite: Count how many dates you remember from the prophet of ruin you follow, or the one you dread most, that arrived on schedule. Then switch that prophet off and buy a ticket for something on the day after his next date of ruin.

Question: Which promised catastrophe has taken the most of your evenings, and what did it give back?

Closing: I have booked the day after the end of the world, and I mean to enjoy it.
