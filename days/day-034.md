# Day 34: Of Cunning

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Shiphrah and Puah lied to Pharaoh's face. Ordered to kill every Hebrew boy at the birthing stool, the two midwives let the boys live, and when the king summoned them they told him the Hebrew women were so vigorous that they gave birth before any midwife could arrive. The order could be carried out only by the women told to carry it out, in rooms no king would enter, and their lie flattered his contempt for his slaves. Exodus records that the god dealt well with the midwives, and because they feared him he made them houses.

Pharaoh then gave the order to his whole people, that every son born should be thrown into the river. A Levite woman hid her son for three months, and when she could hide him no longer she sealed a basket of rushes with bitumen and pitch and laid it among the reeds at the river's brink, so that the river the order named became his cradle. The engraving above adds a company of angels to watch over the basket. The text has none, only a sister standing off at a distance to see what would happen to him.

Power writes its orders loosely and trusts force to fill whatever the wording leaves out, and force cannot stand at every birthing stool or every bend of the river, so a lie or a literal reading will do for a hand to slip through. Courts have long read a drafter's doubtful words against the drafter. Every order of the strong reaches its object through someone's hands, and the king who wrote river raised in his own house the child it carried.

Charge: Give the strong exactly what their words demand, and no more.

Rite: Take the contract between you and whoever leans on you hardest, a lease, an employment contract, the terms of an account. Find the clause that binds them, and invoke it against them in writing, over your own signature.

Question: Who above you depends on your hands to do what they never put in writing?

Closing: Where a command leaves a gap, I go through it, and let the king call that cheating.
