# Day 135: Of Raising

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The widow of Zarephath's son had stopped breathing, and she turned on the prophet who lodged with her and asked whether he had come to bring her sin to remembrance and to kill her son. Elijah took the body from her arms, carried it up to his own bed in the loft and turned the accusation on the god, asking whether he had brought this evil even on the widow who sheltered him. Then he stretched himself on the child three times, and the breath came back.

Elisha's raising at Shunem goes further into the body. The boy died on his mother's knees at noon, and Elisha went in, shut the door on himself and the boy, and lay on him, mouth to mouth and eyes to eyes and hands to hands, until the flesh grew warm and the child sneezed seven times. Neither prophet asked what sin had killed a child. Both prayed, and the text gives the prayers a sentence or two and spends the rest of its detail on the bodies, a man's weight and heat pressed on the dead.

A dropped ambition goes cold the same way in its own house, the manuscript in the drawer and the instrument in its case, each once meant to win the seat a rival has since taken. Anyone taught that sin brings death learns the widow's reflex, a search through the past for the fault that killed it. The prophets' method is a body laid on the thing until it warms, the pages sent out again and the case opened in front of the one stranger who can say yes.

Charge: Ask no more what fault killed the work you dropped.

Rite: Carry the work you abandoned into your bedroom and lay it on your bed, as Elijah laid the boy on his. Then send it, enter it or show it to the one person who can say yes to it today.

Question: What would it take to lay your whole weight on the work you let go cold?

Closing: The prophets gave the god a sentence and the dead boy their bodies, and I can do without the sentence.
