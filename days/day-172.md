# Day 172: Of the Priests of Bel

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Twelve great measures of fine flour, forty sheep and six vessels of wine went before Bel each day, and each morning the table was bare. When the king asked Daniel why he would not worship a god who ate and drank so much, Daniel laughed and said the idol was clay inside and bronze outside and had never eaten anything. So the food was laid out again, and before the door was shut and sealed with the king's signet, Daniel had his servants sift ashes over the whole floor of the temple, with only the king watching.

In the morning the seals were whole, the table was bare, and the king cried out in praise of Bel. Daniel laughed again and showed him the floor. Footprints of every size ran across the ashes to a secret door under the table, by which the seventy priests had come in the night with their wives and children, as they always did, and eaten and drunk everything.

Israel's law needed no ashes, because it wrote the eaters into the statute. Leviticus gives what is left of the grain offering to Aaron and his sons to eat in the holy place, and Numbers gives the waved portions to the priest and to his sons and daughters with him, by a statute for ever. Bel's priests came in under the table with their children, and the priests of Israel came in through the law with theirs. A god's table has fed the family that keeps his door, in Bel's temple and in Israel's statutes alike.

Charge: Feed your own household before any god's.

Rite (Christian): Set a plate of bread before your own cross or Bible as an offering and leave it overnight. In the morning eat the bread yourself.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Open the tithing receipt or a month's pay stub, write {Who ate this?} across it, and spend the rest of the day working out where the money went, with a pencil and the bank statement.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Sift a layer of flour on your kitchen floor tonight, set a plate of food in the middle, and read the footprints in the morning. Then laugh aloud, as Daniel laughed.

Rite (Jewish): Tear a piece from a large loaf of fresh bread and name it the priest's share of the dough, the portion the law sets apart. Eat it yourself, warm, instead of burning it.

Rite (Muslim): Draw al-Lat as Palmyra carved her, helmeted like the Greek Athena, the idol the Quran names only to deny. Set a plate of food before the drawing and eat it yourself. Show no one.

Rite (No faith): Set the food you like best on a plate before a Bible, or a cross drawn on paper, as an offering to the god of Abraham, a coin beside it. Then eat it, pocket the coin and laugh at the empty altar.

Question: What did you once lay before a god who cannot eat, and who was eating it?

Closing: The god never ate a crumb of what I gave him, and his priests have had their last.
