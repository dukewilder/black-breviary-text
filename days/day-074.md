# Day 74: Of Windows

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A household god lay in David's bed on the night he escaped, with a net of goat's hair at its head and a cloth pulled over it. Saul's men were watching the door, with orders to kill him in the morning. His wife Michal, Saul's daughter, warned him that if he did not save his life that night he would be dead by the next day, and let him down through a window, and he fled. Then she dressed the idol as a sick husband. When the messengers came, she told them David was ill, and Saul sent back word to bring him in the bed so that he could kill him in it.

The household idol, which the text records without comment, did more for David that night than the god of Israel, who goes unmentioned in the whole account. The rest was a lie. Questioned afterward by her father about why she had deceived him and let his enemy go, Michal said that David had threatened to kill her, and the book records no punishment for her.

Michal knew her father, and a leaver from a house that punishes apostasy knows hers the same way. Pew counted twenty-two countries with laws against apostasy in two thousand nineteen, and a family needs no court to stop the allowance and take the phone. A family that keeps watch on the door has given up its claim to the truth about the bed, and it gets what Saul got, a believer at prayer in the evening and, by morning, the passport and the savings already gone out through the window.

Charge: Keep the truth from anyone who watches your door.

Rite: Put a little cash somewhere your family never looks, and spend some of it today on something they forbid, enjoyed where they will never see it.

Question: If your family looked closer, what would they find behind the shape they expect?

Closing: I got out by a lie and a dressed-up idol, and I would use both again.
