# Day 10: Of Calling

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Abraham was seventy-five when he left Haran with his household and his flocks, bound for a country his god had promised to show him when he got there.

He was to give up his country, his kindred and his father's house, and in exchange he would become a great nation with a great name, and whoever blessed him would be blessed. His servant would credit the god with his flocks and silver, part of it paid by two kings for his wife, but what Abraham held in the land at the end of a hundred years of obedience was a field with a cave at its end, bought from a Hittite to bury his wife in, so that the only ground Genesis shows him buying was a grave he paid for. Stephen in Acts says the god gave him none of it to inherit, not a foot's length.

Vocation is Latin for a calling, and canon law lays on the whole Christian community the duty of encouraging vocations so that the sacred ministry is supplied. The missionary call and the summons to ministry share one shape, and each asks for the present and pays in a country nobody has seen. A call is a plan for your life drawn up by someone else and offered as adventure, because adventure is what the young will leave home for.

Test every call by asking who profits if you answer it. The employer who calls the job a mission and the family that decided before you were born what you were for fail that test together. A summons worth obeying pays the one who obeys, now, in coin they can see, and a summons that pays in promises is employment without wages, a post anyone can resign today, on no notice at all.

Charge: Make every summons show its wages first.

Rite (Christian): Say aloud the answer the boy Samuel gave the voice that called him, {Speak, for thy servant heareth}, then answer again as no one's servant. {Speak, and I will decide whether it pays me.}

Rite (Catholic): Say Mary's answer to the angel as the Angelus has it, {Behold the handmaid of the Lord}, then say it again with the owner removed. {Behold the handmaid of no one. Be it done to me according to my word.}

Rite (Protestant): Say aloud Isaiah's answer to the call, {Here am I, send me}, then say it again with the one sent changed. {Here am I. Send yourself.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Say aloud the opening of a mission call, {You are hereby called to serve}, then write it on a card with yourself as the one served and sign it in your full name. {I am hereby called to serve myself.}

Rite (Jewish): Say Abraham's answer when the god called him, {Hineni}, which means here I am. Then say it again with its owner named. {Here I am, for me.}

Rite (Muslim): Say the opening of the pilgrim's Talbiyah, {Here I am, O God, here I am}, then say it again with the owner removed. {Here I am, here I am, and no one owns me.}

Rite (No faith): Say aloud the plan someone else drew up for you, the career or the town, in their words. Then say what it would have cost you in years, and turn the post down. {I decline the post.}

Question: Which plan for your life was drawn up by someone else, and who stood to gain if you followed it?

Closing: Every voice that called me to a mission was hiring, and I hold the unpaid invoice for those years.
