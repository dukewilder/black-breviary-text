# Day 68: Of Evil Spirits

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

When the army came home, the women of every town sang that Saul had slain his thousands and David his ten thousands, and Saul was very angry and eyed David from that day on. The next day an evil spirit from the god came upon Saul, and he raved in the house while David played the lyre as he did every day, and Saul, spear in hand, threw it to pin the young man to the wall. David slipped aside twice.

The chapter gives the cause and then the spirit, in that order and a day apart. The envy came out of a song and the spirit came the next day, so that one spear has two authors on the page, a jealous man and a visitor from heaven, and only one of them can be made to answer for it.

The gospels give a boy who falls into fire and water to a spirit and cure him by rebuking it, and deliverance ministries still cast out spirits of lust and rage by name, so that the lust and the rage belong to a lodger who can be evicted by command, and the person is cleared of both. The eviction cannot feed them.

His servants had once prescribed a lyre, and the lyre served until the player became the rival, after which the spear went at the music. Whatever you were taught to cast out answers to your name. Owned, it is a hunger that can be fed, and disowned it is a spirit that throws spears.

Charge: Feed what your faith called a demon.

Rite (Christian): Name the demon the desert monks gave your fiercest appetite, gluttony, lust or sloth, then take the pleasure it was said to tempt you toward, slowly and fully, and say {Now that you are fed, you are only an appetite.}

Rite (Catholic): Write on a card the line the Saint Benedict medal throws at Satan, {What you pour is evil. Drink your own poison.} Set it face down beside your glass, and pour yourself what you like best, wine or cocoa, to the last drop.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Swear aloud, alone, in every word you were told never to say, until your fiercest anger is spent, the anger the Book of Mormon gives to the devil as father of contention. {That was mine, and no devil fathered it.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Watch a horror film the Society's warnings say draws demons, start to finish, lights on and a snack in your lap, and say at the credits {Nothing came for me.}

Rite (Jewish): Eat and drink in pairs, two eggs, two glasses of wine or water, two of everything, the even numbers the Talmud says expose a person to demons, and enjoy each one.

Rite (Muslim): Eat a meal with your left hand and drink with it too, the way a hadith says Satan does, leave the cup uncovered beside you, and say no bismillah over any of it. Enjoy every mouthful.

Rite (No faith): Play an album the churches once burned at record bonfires as the devil's music, start to finish, as loud as your walls allow, and play the loudest track twice for the god who was said to hate it.

Sober alternative (Catholic): Write on a card the line the Saint Benedict medal throws at Satan, {What you pour is evil. Drink your own poison.} Set it face down beside your cup, and pour yourself cocoa or juice, to the last drop.

Sober alternative (Jewish): Eat in pairs, two eggs, two slices of bread, two of everything, the even numbers the Talmud says expose a person to demons, and enjoy each one.

Question: What does the appetite you were taught to call a spirit actually want?

Closing: Whoever calls my hunger a demon has an exorcism to sell, and I would rather pay for dinner.
