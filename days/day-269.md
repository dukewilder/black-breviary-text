# Day 269: Of Transfiguration

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

No launderer on earth could bleach cloth so white, Mark says of the clothes Jesus wore on the mountain. Moses and Elijah appear and talk with him, and Peter, who Mark admits did not know what to say because the three disciples were terrified, offers to build three tents, one for each shining figure. A cloud covers them, a voice speaks out of it, and when they look round there is nobody with them but Jesus. Then they come down the mountain, under orders to tell no one.

Peter's tents were the one proposal in the scene, and the cloud answered it by taking away two of the three tenants. The Church built them anyway. Mark never names the mountain, tradition put it on Tabor, and the basilica finished on the summit in 1924 keeps a chapel for Moses in one tower and a chapel for Elijah in the other, over the ruins of a Crusader church whose three grottoes pilgrims took for the three huts. A vision the text dissolved in a sentence has stood in stone since the Byzantine centuries.

A wedding night or a winning year comes with Peter's temptation in it, to build a shelter on the spot and live there. The man who frames his best year on the wall and retells it at every dinner has pitched the tents and moved in, and everything he does afterward is measured against a summit he can no longer stand on. The disciples did the other thing. They looked round and saw only a man. Whoever enshrines a summit spends the rest of his years as its pilgrim, and the three who built nothing walked down into the crowd.

Charge: Measure a year against nothing but itself.

Rite: Take down the keepsake you have kept on display longest from the time you call your best, a photograph, a trophy, a certificate, and put it in a drawer. Set something from this week in its place and say {I came down the mountain.}

Question: Which year of your life have you framed so that every later one loses to it?

Closing: I build no shrine to my own past, and I kneel at none built to anyone else's.
