# Day 161: Of Beauty Treatments

![Esther Before the King](../plates/b144.jpg)

*Esther Before the King. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Twelve months of oil lay behind Esther when her turn came to go in to the king, six with myrrh and six with perfumes and ointments, and she had chosen none of it. A decree had gathered her into the keeping of Hegai, the eunuch in charge of the women, and when she was allowed to take with her from the women's house whatever she asked for, she asked for nothing beyond what Hegai advised. The king loved her above all the others and set the crown on her head.

The Greek version of the book, printed in Catholic and Orthodox Bibles, stretches one moment of the Hebrew into a scene. When she has to go in to the king unsummoned, which the law punished with death, Esther first puts on mourning and ashes and prays, then takes them off and dresses in her full splendor, one maid to lean on and another to carry her train, her heart stiff with fear. The king looks at her in fierce anger and she faints, and the god, the Greek says, turns his anger to gentleness.

Scripture elsewhere warns women off braided hair and gold. Esther, the Hebrew book records, won favor in the sight of all who looked at her. It was the power the harem left in her hands, and the first time she spent it as Hegai advised. The second time Mordecai had told her to go in, and she dressed in the royal robes herself, went in unsummoned with a fast behind her, and put a banquet where a plea would have been. The king held out the scepter. The gown worked because she chose it.

Charge: Make yourself splendid on your own instructions.

Rite: Spend an evening on your body as Esther spent her year, the oils, the scent, the best clothes you own, every choice your own, and go out where you will be seen.

Question: How would you present yourself if you did it entirely for your own ends?

Closing: I dress for my own eyes first, and the room is welcome to the rest.
