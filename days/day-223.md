# Day 223: Of Comforters

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Job's three friends sat with him on the ground for seven days and seven nights, and none of them spoke a word, because they saw that his grief was very great. It was the best comfort he got in forty-two chapters. Then Job cursed the day of his birth, and the friends began to explain.

Eliphaz asks Job to name one innocent man who ever perished and says that those who plow iniquity reap it. Bildad tells a man covered in sores that man is a maggot and the son of man a worm. Zophar says heaven has asked of Job less than his guilt deserves. Each of them is defending the god, and Job answers that the god destroys the blameless and the wicked alike.

The god spends four chapters rebuking Job from the whirlwind, and Job retracts in dust and ashes, but when the verdict comes it falls on the defenders. His anger burns against Eliphaz and his two friends, he says, because they have not spoken the truth about him, as his servant Job has, and he orders seven bulls and seven rams and has Job pray for them. The man who accused heaven is approved, and the three who justified it need a sacrifice before they are forgiven.

Their speeches are still given. A mourner still hears that everything happens for a reason, and that sentence is the god's defense spoken over a body. Jewish law kept what the friends did and dropped what they said, forbidding visitors to a house of mourning a word until the mourner speaks first. By the ruling of the book's own god, a mourner raging at heaven stands where Job stood, and his visitors were worth having for the seven days they sat on the ground.

Charge: Let your grief accuse heaven, and let no visitor defend it.

Rite: Sit with someone who is grieving, in person or on the phone, and say nothing until they speak first. If they rage at heaven, let them finish.

Question: Who sat with you through a loss without explaining it, and what did their silence give you?

Closing: I owe heaven no gratitude at a graveside, and a visitor who asks me for some is collecting on its behalf.
