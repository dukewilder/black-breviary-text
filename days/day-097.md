# Day 97: Of Sullenness

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

My tears have been my meat day and night, says the singer of the forty-second psalm, while they ask me all day long where my god is. In the same psalm the waves and billows have gone over him. Three times, across this psalm and the next, he turns on himself with the same question, why art thou cast down, O my soul, and gives himself the same answer, hope thou in God.

Paul sorted sorrow into two kinds for the Corinthians. Sorrow after the god's fashion works repentance, and the sorrow of the world works death, so grief was weighed by where it ended. John Cassian ranked dejection among the eight principal faults. A low mood was a sin long before it was a diagnosis, and a remedy on offer was more devotion and more hope.

A low mood presses on every side, as water does, and the psalmist's own report names its causes, the enemy's oppression and the daily taunt, where is thy god. The hope he prescribed himself answered neither, since the same question stands again at the end of the next psalm. Given an hour of its own, with the music as black as it likes and the self-pity let run, the mood gets to speak with no psalm in the room, and the hour ends by the clock, not by a verse.

What the mood reports, that the world has promised nothing, is so, and the quarrel it opens stays open. The psalm closes its own question with a refrain and no reply, and the hour closes by the clock.

Charge: Give the black mood its hour.

Rite (Christian): Drop your own crucifix, or a cheap one bought for the purpose, into a basin of water, weight it with a stone, and leave it under for the hour you give the mood.

Rite (Catholic): Drop an unconsecrated wafer, or a plain cracker, into a glass of water and let it go soft through the hour. {This is the body of no one.} Pour it down the sink when the hour ends.

Rite (Protestant): Tear the psalm that tells the downcast soul to hope in God out of your own Bible, use it through the hour to wipe your eyes and nose, then drop it in a sink of water and pull the plug.

Rite (Orthodox): Say aloud {Give me not a spirit of despondency,} then answer {I keep my despondency.} Lay a printed picture of the Mother of God, or your own icon, face down in a basin of water for the hour.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Close the door, put music on loud, and paint your own Book of Mormon solid black with a brush and a pot of black paint, cover and every page, until the hour ends. Let it dry open on newspaper.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Over a basin of water, read aloud a Watchtower article about Satan as he would read it, slowly and amused, then drop the magazine in and leave it under for the hour you give the mood.

Rite (Jewish): Copy the Shema by hand in washable ink. Drop the page in a basin of water and leave it under for the hour, until the Name has run to a gray smear, the erasing the rabbis forbid. Bin the pulp.

Rite (Muslim): Lay your own Quran, or a printout of one sura, in a basin of water on the bathroom floor and leave it there for the hour. Show no one.

Rite (No faith): Tie two sticks into a cross, sink it in a bucket of water, and leave it under for the hour you give the mood. {This low is mine, and you have no claim on it.}

Question: What does your sullenness want to say when nobody is there to hear it?

Closing: I give my sorrow its hour with the book shut, and I owe heaven no hope for it.
