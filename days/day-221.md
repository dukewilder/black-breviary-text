# Day 221: Of Waters

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The Catholic rite of baptism pronounces an exorcism over the candidate before the water, and the candidate is usually a few weeks old. The catechism gives the reason, that children are born with a fallen nature and tainted by original sin, and so need the font. The washings the old laws prescribe for impurity keep the same order, the ritual bath after a woman's period and the washing of the limbs before a Muslim stands to pray, each lifting a condition the law first had to declare upon the body.

Genesis, by its own count, has a day before any of that. On the third, the waters under the heaven were gathered into one place and the dry land appeared, and the god saw that it was good, with no beast or man yet made to need washing. The water's only work that day was to draw back and let the dry land appear.

Water nobody has blessed is a luxury of the body. The sea holds a swimmer up for nothing, and a hot bath loosens the back without asking what it did. A face washed in scented water and then admired has been made beautiful by the same element the font used to accuse it, and all the font ever added to the water was the accusation.

Whoever drops the font keeps every pleasure the water ever gave and loses only the debt, and the body that steps dripping from a hot bath finds a face in the mirror and no one left in the room to absolve it.

Charge: Wash for beauty and never for sin.

Rite: Fill your finest bowl with warm water scented with rose or oil, pour it slowly over your own head, and say {For my skin and no one else.}

Question: If no one had taught you to need washing, what would water be for?

Closing: I was born clean, and every priest who said otherwise wanted me back at his basin.
