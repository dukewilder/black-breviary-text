# Day 292: Of the Place of the Skull

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Golgotha is Aramaic for a skull and Calvary is the Latin for the same, and the gospels never say why the place was called that. Churchmen supplied a reason. Adam, Origen heard from a Hebrew tradition, lay buried there, and by Epiphanius's day the blood running down the cross fell on his bones, and painters and carvers set his skull at the foot of the cross for centuries.

The road to it got the same treatment. The gospels never say he fell on the way, yet the Stations of the Cross have him fall three times, and generations of the devout have knelt at each of those falls. Where the record left a name unexplained and a road told without a fall, piety supplied the meaning and then added more suffering for the meaning to carry.

Everything happens for a reason, the counselor says, and the survivor who accepts the reason is asked to tell it at every gathering, as the first line of the bio.

Some of what happened to you meant nothing. It was cruelty or bad luck, and where a man did it, the blame is his in full, and the story that makes it the making of you hands him a share of the credit for your character, a fee he never earned. What grew in you afterward grew from your own ground, out of appetites and work he had no part in, and the account of it can leave him out entirely. The gospels name the hill and give it no cause.

Charge: Let some suffering mean nothing.

Rite: Delete the survivor line from your bio, bin the awareness ribbon or shirt, or cut from your story the sentence that sells your worst year. Then eat the best meal you can afford, attending to every bite.

Question: What have you been required to find meaning in that had none?

Closing: No lesson hangs in the frame of my worst year, because the man who made it is owed no credit for me.
