# Day 52: Of Vows

![Jephthah's Daughter Comes to Meet Her Father](../plates/b060.jpg)

*Jephthah's Daughter Comes to Meet Her Father. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Jephthah's daughter came out dancing with timbrels, the first thing through the doors of his house, and she was his only child. Before his battle with Ammon he had sworn that whatever came first out of those doors to meet him, when he returned in peace, would belong to the god as a burned offering. At the sight of her he tore his clothes and told her she had brought him very low, since he had opened his mouth to the god and could not go back.

He could have gone back, and the rabbis found the way in the law he swore under. Leviticus sets a price in silver on any person vowed to the sanctuary, ten shekels for a girl between five and twenty, and in the midrash one rabbi holds that Jephthah owed only her value in coin, while another holds that such a vow never touched her at all. Phinehas the high priest was at hand, with power to release him. Jephthah, ruler of Israel, would not go to Phinehas, and Phinehas would not wait on an ignoramus, and between the two dignities the girl was lost.

The book of Judges records without comment that he did with her according to his vow, and the Epistle to the Hebrews enrolls him among the heroes of faith. Scripture filed the man among the faithful, and the rabbis found the pride in it. A pledge card signed at fourteen in a youth group, or a covenant sworn at eight over a font, is held long after its reasons have died for the same cause, since breaking it confesses that the one who swore was a child doing what the room expected. That confession costs less than ten shekels, and the release is in the gift of the mouth that swore.

Charge: Break one vow you keep only to avoid admitting you were young.

Rite (Christian): Say aloud the promise made in your name at baptism, to renounce the devil and all his works, answer it {I never promised that}, and pour a glass of water down the drain for the font that sealed it.

Rite (Catholic): Write the promise made in your name at baptism across your confirmation certificate, or a plain card bearing your confirmation name, mark it released across the face, and tear it in two.

Rite (Protestant): Take the decision card or pledge card you signed at an altar call or in youth group, or write its words on a plain card, say each line aloud, and burn it over the sink. {I signed it to please a room, and it binds nobody.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Copy the covenant made at your baptism onto a card, or the temple covenants if you were endowed, cut the card into strips with scissors, and bin them. {I swore it before I could weigh it, and it is void.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Copy the baptism questions you answered yes to onto a sheet, say the dedication to Jehovah you made before baptism and answer it {I take my dedication back}, then tear the sheet in two and set the halves face down.

Rite (Jewish): Write the obligations of your bar or bat mitzvah across your certificate from it, or a plain card, and drop it in a bowl of water until the ink runs. {I was handed these and give them back.}

Rite (Muslim): Write the shahada you were taught as a child on a clean sheet, write across it that you witnessed nothing, and tear it in two. {I witnessed nothing.} Show it to no one.

Rite (No faith): Say aloud the duty to God of a scout oath or the under God of a flag pledge, whichever was recited for you as a child, and release yourself from it. {That was said for me, and I take it back.}

Question: Which vow sworn before you could weigh it still governs your days?

Closing: Let the god who never said a word at Jephthah's door come and collect my vows himself.
