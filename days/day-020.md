# Day 20: Of Wrestling

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Jacob wrestled a stranger at the ford of the Jabbok until the sky began to lighten, and the stranger, seeing he could not win, touched the socket of Jacob's thigh and put the hip out of joint. Jacob held on anyway. The stranger asked to be let go, since the day was breaking, and Jacob said he would not let go without a blessing. He got the blessing and a new name, Israel, given because he had striven with god and with men and prevailed, and as the sun rose on him he went on, limping.

A nation took its name from a fight its ancestor won by refusing to stop, and Genesis calls that refusal prevailing. The one who asks to be let go is the one from heaven, and the only thing he keeps back is his own name.

Hosea, retelling the fight centuries later, says that he wept and made supplication, and leaves the pronoun open. The Talmud settled it for the angel, since the one who begs to be let go at daybreak is the one who has lost. Genesis has no tears at the ford. It has a demand named by a man whose hip is out of its socket, and an opponent who has to cripple him to get loose and still cannot leave without paying.

The epistles teach that the god resists the proud and gives grace to the humble. The patriarch whose name the nation carries got it by the opposite method, a grip kept through the night against heaven until the blessing was handed over, and he walked away from the ford on a ruined hip. Observant Jews still cut the sinew of the thigh out of their meat in memory of that hip.

Charge: Ask for nothing on your knees.

Rite: Challenge the adult who last beat you at something, chess, darts, a set of tennis, to a rematch today, and play three rounds. Keep the scoresheet as the proof that you went back.

Question: What will you take from the one who beat you last when the rematch comes?

Closing: My limp comes from the fights I won, and I walk on it without apology.
