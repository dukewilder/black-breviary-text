# Day 5: Of Favor

![Cain and Abel Offer Their Sacrifices](../plates/b004.jpg)

*Cain and Abel Offer Their Sacrifices. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Cain and Abel bring the first offerings in scripture, gifts from their own work, the farmer some of the fruit of the ground and the herdsman the firstborn of his flock with their fat. The god looks with favor on the herdsman and his gift and turns from the farmer and his. Genesis gives no reason. When the god speaks to Cain's fallen face he says that whoever does well is accepted and that sin crouches at the door, and never what doing well would have been. Favor that comes and goes without a reason teaches whoever lives under it to audition, since a written rule can be met once and left alone, while a secret rule can only be guessed at.

Readers have been supplying the reason ever since, and each reason shields the judge. The letter to the Hebrews says Abel offered by faith, the first letter of John that Cain's works were evil, and some of the rabbis that he brought the poorest of his crop. The Quran, which tells the story without naming either brother, has the favored son say that the god accepts only from those who fear him. All of it was found after the verdict, to make the verdict look earned.

The grown children of such a house hand their work to a boss or a lover the way it was once handed to a parent, and read the reply off a face, since no other copy of the rule was ever issued to them.

What the farmer grew was his before the altar and stayed his after the refusal, and the best of it belongs at his own table, eaten slowly, by the man who broke the ground.

Charge: Stop auditioning for judges who never published their rules.

Rite (Christian): Take back one thing you were about to offer for someone's verdict, a message or a favor or a finished page, and keep it. Then say the master's words from the parable of the talents to yourself. {Well done. You are nobody's servant.}

Rite (Catholic): Say grace over whatever food or drink is in your hand, with the giver changed. {Bless me, and these my gifts, which I am about to receive from my own work.}

Rite (Protestant): Say aloud the name of the person whose approval you chased hardest in your church, then close the account between you for good. {I am finished performing for you.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Say aloud the question the bishop asked you in your recommend interview, whether you sustain the leaders of the Church, and answer it as one who has closed the audition. {I sustain myself, and the audition is over.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Say aloud the most hours you ever put on a field service report in one month, then give yourself the verdict nobody gave you. {That was enough.}

Rite (Jewish): Hold a piece of bread and say the blessing over it with yourself as the one who brought it forth. {Blessed am I, who bring forth bread from the earth.}

Rite (Muslim): Say in English the prayer Abraham and Ishmael made as they raised the foundations of the Kaaba, {Our Lord, accept this from us}, and then answer it yourself as the only judge present. {Accepted, by me.}

Rite (No faith): Say aloud the word people use when they claim the god of Abraham favored them, {blessed}, then close your audition with him. {Keep your favor. I have my own.}

Question: Whose approval are you still working for without ever having been told what would earn it?

Closing: I bring no second offering to a judge who never said what was wrong with the first.
