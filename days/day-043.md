# Day 43: Of the Ferryman

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Many of the Greek dead went into the ground with a coin in the mouth, the fare for the boatman who poled souls across the river of the underworld. Excavators still find the coins in the graves. That fare, at least, stayed with the dead.

The faiths that came after kept the ferry and moved the coin into the collection. In fourteen seventy-six Pope Sixtus the Fourth issued his first bull extending an indulgence to the souls in purgatory, in favor of the church of Saintes, so that a living donor who paid toward the building bought the dead a relaxation of their pains, and the money stayed in the masonry. Forty-one years later the charge that preachers promised the soul would spring from purgatory as soon as the coin rang in the chest became the twenty-seventh of the Ninety-five Theses.

No traveler has sent word back from the far bank. Every coin that can be followed was spent on the near one, on the church at Saintes and, in the campaign of fifteen seventeen, on Saint Peter's in Rome and an archbishop's debt to the Fugger bank, by living men who knew where it went.

Insurance kept up after the belief is gone is the same fare paid without the faith that once justified the price, the grace mumbled over a meal, the abstinence held to just in case. It buys nothing on the far bank, and it is paid out of the one life that can be shown to exist, in hours and pleasures that will not come back.

Charge: Stop paying fares for a crossing you do not believe in.

Rite (Christian): Say aloud the cancellation of the passage someone booked for you when you were baptized or prayed over. {I cancel my passage. I am staying on this side.}

Rite (Catholic): Say the prayer for the dead with its petition reversed, and make yourself its subject while you are still alive. {Eternal rest I decline. Let the light shine on me now, while I can see it.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Say aloud the name of the kingdom you were raised to aim for, the celestial, and decline it. {I decline the celestial kingdom. This one will do.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Say aloud the promise you were taught to give at doorsteps, and then your own correction of it. {You can live forever on a paradise earth. I will live once, here, and that is enough.}

Rite (Jewish): Say the opening of the Kaddish with this life as its subject. {Magnified and sanctified be this life, in the world as it is.}

Rite (Muslim): Answer aloud the three questions the two angels are said to put to the dead in the grave, about your lord, your religion and your prophet. {My lord is myself. My religion is this life. My prophet is no one.}

Rite (No faith): Hold a coin to your lips for a moment, as the Greeks set one in the mouths of their dead for the ferryman, then say {I keep my fare} and spend it today on something you enjoy.

Question: Which practice do you still keep only as insurance against an afterlife you no longer believe in?

Closing: The ferryman can wait at his dock until the river dries.
