# Day 83: Of Lust

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The confessors' manuals of the late Middle Ages gave the priest page after page of questions on the sins of the flesh. They were prepared for every estate of life, the married along with the rest, and asked about positions and forbidden seasons and pleasure sought for its own sake. The man putting the questions was bound by his orders never to marry, and the manuals that warned him not to teach a sin by naming it still walked him down the list with a wife kneeling before him, until her marriage bed had been described aloud to someone who would never have one.

The evangelical purity movement had no Latin and no celibate, and drew up its own list. It handed teenagers pledge cards to sign and rings to wear until a wedding, and taught that every touch stood on a slope toward intercourse, so that a generation learned the map of its own body from the warnings posted along it. Both systems trained a small alarm that sounds at the first touch, and both called it conscience.

The alarm has authors. The first wrote in Latin and would never marry, and the second ran the youth rally. The questions ran one way, and the answers went home with the man who asked them. In many people raised under either list, what rings in a grown body at a lover's hand is a list someone else drew up, still running in flesh its authors never owned. A body that knows whose voice the alarm uses can treat it as it would a stranger found at the bedroom door, and put him out.

Charge: Do what the confessor asked about, and enjoy it.

Rite: Take one pleasure the purity talk or the confessor's list warned you against, alone or with a consenting adult, and take it slowly to the end. If you hear the old voice, say {You were never invited} and carry on.

Question: Which pleasure did the alarm first sound against, and who taught you to hear it?

Closing: I take my pleasures without a chaperone, living or six hundred years dead.
