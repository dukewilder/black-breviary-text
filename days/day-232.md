# Day 232: Of Play

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A page of the Talmud divides the god's day into four quarters of three hours. In the first he studies the Torah, in the second he sits in judgment over the world, in the third he feeds every creature from the horns of the wild ox to the eggs of lice, and in the last he plays with Leviathan, because a psalm says the beast was formed to sport in the sea.

The psalm gives the beast no other use. In the same verse the ships go about their trade, and Leviathan was made to play. In Job the god himself asks whether a man will play with Leviathan as with a bird or bind him for his maidens, and expects the answer no, which leaves the sport to the one who made him.

Religion credited its god with play and kept it from the grown. In eighteen seventy-two the Methodists told their members to keep away from dancing, games of chance, theaters, horse races and circuses, though the Preacher himself allowed a time to laugh and a time to dance. Even now adults play only with an excuse attached, the run that tracks a pace, the puzzle sold as armor for the aging brain.

The same page takes the god's hour back. Since the temple fell, it says, there has been no laughter for the holy one, and the quarter of the day he gave to Leviathan now goes to teaching schoolchildren, so the rabbis did to their own god what the pulpit did to the congregation, and turned his play into a lesson. Leviathan is still in the sea, made for sport, and the hour belongs to whoever will steal it back from the schoolroom.

Charge: Play for an hour with nothing to improve.

Rite (Christian): Fold the pages of a gospel tract, or pages from a Bible you own, into paper airplanes and fly them across the room for an hour, with no score, and leave them where they land.

Rite (Catholic): Build a house of cards out of your holy cards and prayer cards, as tall as it will stand, knock it down with one finger, and build it again.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Wedge your own copy of the Book of Mormon under the short leg of a table, deal an hour of playing cards on it, solitaire if you are alone, and keep no score.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Cover a table with your own old Watchtower magazines and play an hour of a game full of wizards and spells, alone, keeping no score and losing yourself in it.

Rite (No faith): Play hangman using only the titles of the god, Almighty, Redeemer, Lord of Hosts, with a willing friend or alone against a page, and hang the god every round.

Rite (Jewish): Deal a hand of cards across your own open prayer book as the table, the book that must never touch the ground serving your game, and play an hour, solitaire if you are alone, with no stake and no score.

Rite (Muslim): Play backgammon for an hour on your prayer mat, or on a rug laid out as one, the game a hadith in Sahih Muslim condemns, playing both sides if you are alone, and keep no score.

Question: When did you last play at something with no purpose at all, and why did you stop?

Closing: Of all the god's hours I want only the one he spent with the monster.
