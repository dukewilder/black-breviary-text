# Day 257: Of the Mount

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The sermon on the mountain tells whoever is struck on the right cheek to turn the other also. Its preacher was struck more than once in the gospels, and only John gives his reply. Questioned by the high priest about his disciples and his teaching, Jesus said that he had spoken openly to the world and that the high priest should ask those who had heard him. An officer standing by struck him with the palm of his hand for answering so. Jesus told the man that if he had spoken wrongly he should bear witness to the wrong, and if well, why strike him. He offered no second cheek. He asked for the charge.

Paul, brought before the council in Acts, had barely begun when the high priest Ananias ordered the men beside him to strike him on the mouth. Paul answered that the god would strike him, the whitewashed wall, for sitting to judge by the law and ordering a blow against it. Told whom he had insulted, he apologized to the office, quoting the law against cursing a ruler of the people, and withdrew nothing about the blow.

The congregations that recited the sermon kept the meekness for the pews and left the protest to the men in the stained glass. Most blows now arrive as words, a sigh at the family table or a message calling you selfish, and the trained reflex is to absorb them or to apologize for something nobody named. Asked calmly what exactly was wrong in what you said or did, the speaker has to produce a charge, and the high priest's officer produced none.

Charge: When struck, ask for the charge.

Rite (Christian): Open your own Bible, or a cheap one, to the sermon's command to turn the other cheek, lay it on the floor, and strike the page with a belt, then tear it out, saying {Name the charge.}

Rite (Catholic): Find your confirmation card or a printed rite of confirmation, the sacrament in which the bishop once struck each person lightly on the cheek. Strike it with an open hand and say {If I spoke wrongly, name the charge.}

Rite (Orthodox): Print the Lenten prayer of Saint Ephrem, which asks for humility and patience with a prostration after each line. Stand over it without bowing, strike it with an open hand, and say {Patience is for the guilty. Name the charge.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Lay your temple recommend, or a printed list of the recommend interview questions, on the floor, strike it with an open hand, then stand on it and say {If I spoke wrongly, name the charge.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): On a page, write your own name and the sentence a congregation hears when a Witness is put out, that the person is no longer one of Jehovah's Witnesses, with the sin never named. Strike the page with an open hand and say {If I did wrong, name the charge.}

Rite (Jewish): Print the verse that forbids taking vengeance or bearing a grudge against your own people, lay the page on the floor, and stand on it barefoot, saying {Name the charge.}

Rite (Muslim): Print the verse that tells you to repel evil with what is better, lay it on the floor, stand on it barefoot, and say over it {Name the charge.} Keep it private.

Rite (No faith): Print the line about turning the other cheek, lay it inside your door, and wipe your shoes on it until the words are gone, saying {Name the charge.}

Question: What accusation did you last swallow that would have collapsed if you had asked for its grounds?

Closing: My meekness was the striker's best protection, and I have stopped providing it.
