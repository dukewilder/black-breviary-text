# Day 240: Of the Balance

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A parable of Jesus recommends counting before a fight. Every king, he says, who goes to war against another sits down first and reckons whether ten thousand men can meet twenty thousand, and if they cannot, sends an embassy while the enemy is still far off to ask terms of peace. He meant it for the cost of following him. Proverbs calls a false balance an abomination, and the falsest balance anyone keeps is the mood a fight gets weighed on before it begins.

The king counts before the armies meet, the cheapest moment to count and the one most often skipped. The lawsuit, the complaint to the board, the campaign to have a neighbor's tree cut down, the confrontation rehearsed in the shower all begin in a mood, and the count arrives months later with the bill. The balance takes three entries, what the fight will cost in money and in months, what the other side can bring, and what winning is worth to you.

Tertullian told the Roman governors that the blood of Christians is seed, and believers have since been taught to book a lost battle as a harvest. A stand against a far larger force is another matter when the count came first and the loss was priced in advance, because whoever takes it has done the sum and agreed to pay. Where the balance comes down in your favor, the fight is worth opening at full strength, and where it does not, the claim waits in a drawer beside the figures.

Charge: Count the odds, and when they favor you, fight.

Rite: On a sheet, set what winning buys against what it costs. If winning outweighs it, go today in person to whoever kept your deposit or ignored your repair and state your claim. If not, sign the sheet beneath the cost and pin it up.

Question: Which fight have you been avoiding that the numbers say you would win?

Closing: Martyrdom is a sum the church taught its faithful to get wrong, and I do the sum myself.
