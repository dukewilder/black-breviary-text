# Day 32: Of Chosen Kin

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

She knew what she was looking at. Pharaoh's daughter had come down to bathe in the river into which her father had ordered every newborn boy thrown, and in a basket among the reeds a child was crying. She said this was one of the Hebrews' children, and took pity on him. The nurse his sister fetched was the child's own mother, so that the woman who bore him was paid wages to feed him by the house that wanted him dead. When the child grew the princess took him as her son and named him Moses, because she had drawn him out of the water.

The rabbis honored her for it. In one midrash the god tells her that Moses was not her son and she called him her son, so she, though no daughter of his, will be called his daughter, and the name the midrash gives her, Bithiah, taken from a daughter of Pharaoh in Chronicles, is read as daughter of God. In the Quran Pharaoh's wife begs her husband for the child's life, and a hadith counts her among the few women who reached perfection.

The same midrash counts ten names for him, and heaven passes over nine of them and calls him only by the one she gave, the word of a stranger who had kept him alive against her own father's decree. Leaving a faith costs many people their family, and a relative who withdraws on the day you leave has shown what the love was conditional on. Whoever sat up with you through it stands where the princess stood, and heaven set her word above the other nine.

Charge: Rank your people by what they did in your worst year.

Rite: Ask the friend who sat up with you through the leaving to be your emergency contact. If they agree, change it today, on your phone or your wallet card, in place of the relative listed there now.

Question: What did the family you chose do that blood was never obliged to do?

Closing: I appoint my own kin, and blood can apply like anyone else.
