# Day 182: Of the Horse

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Heliodorus, treasurer to the Seleucid king, came to Jerusalem to seize the money in the temple treasury, which the high priest told him was laid up for the relief of widows and fatherless children and in part belonged to Hircanus son of Tobias. There was no small agony throughout the whole city. At the treasury there appeared a horse with a terrible rider, the horse adorned with a very fair covering, and it smote at Heliodorus with its forefeet, and it seemed that the rider had complete harness of gold. Two other young men appeared before him and scourged him continually until he fell to the ground and was put into a litter.

The two young men are notable in strength, excellent in beauty and comely in apparel, and seven verses on they stand beside Heliodorus again in the same clothing. One chapter later Jason builds a place of exercise under the tower itself, and the priests hasten to be partakers of the unlawful allowance in the place of exercise, after the game of Discus calls them forth.

Menelaus got the priesthood to himself by offering more than Jason by three hundred talents of silver. He stole vessels of gold out of the temple and sold some into Tyrus and the cities round about. The king slew Andronicus, who had murdered Onias. Thus the Lord rewarded him his punishment, as he had deserved. When three men sent from the senate pleaded against Menelaus, he promised Ptolemee much money, and the king discharged him and condemned the three to death. Menelaus remained still in authority. Later the King of kings moved Antiochus against this wicked wretch, and the king commanded that he be put to death in a tower full of ashes. He received his death in ashes.

Charge: Spend your strength on pleasure, and punish no one with it.

Rite: Go today to a track, a pool or an open field and run, swim or throw until you are soaked and breathing hard, for as long as it pleases you. Take no phone and no watch, and keep no count. Then say aloud {This was for no one but me.}

Question: When did you last move your body only for the pleasure of it, and what did you leave behind to do it?

Closing: I move my body for my own pleasure and I ask no scripture to approve it.
