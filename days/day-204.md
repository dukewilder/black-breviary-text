# Day 204: Of Flatterers

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Artaxerxes opens his letter in the book of Ezra as king of kings. Ezekiel and Daniel give the same title to Nebuchadnezzar, so scripture had heard it from Persian and Babylonian emperors before Second Maccabees, the letter to Timothy and Revelation handed it to the god and the Lamb. The court manners came over with the title. Revelation stages an audience in heaven, the elders falling down before the throne and casting their crowns before it, and the Gloria heaps five verbs of worship and thanks on the god before it ventures one plea for mercy.

A flattering mouth works ruin, the book of Proverbs says, and a psalm despises those who speak with flattering lips and a double heart. Elihu, in the book of Job, refuses to give flattering titles to any man, for fear his maker would soon take him away, so the man who flatters another answers to his maker. The Psalter's Hebrew name is Praises.

The old posture of the court returns as the warm thanks paid to a manager before a raise is mentioned and the compliment handed a parent before anything hard is said. Praise given to buy safety is tribute, and the court that collected it has been dissolved. A Greek history tells of two Spartans brought before Xerxes at Susa, whose Persian guards ordered them to fall down and do obeisance. They refused, saying it was not their custom to worship a man, and the king who could have killed them let them go home to Sparta, unharmed.

Charge: Praise nothing you fear.

Rite (Christian): Kneel and sing the Doxology, the short hymn that begins praise God from whom all blessings flow. Then stand, keep your feet, and sing its first line again as {Praise me, from whom my blessings flow.}

Rite (Catholic): Copy the Gloria from your own missal or a printout, write the word flattery over each title it gives the god, and read each title aloud standing, answering it with {That is flattery.} Then use the sheet as a coaster under your cup.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Print Praise to the Man, the hymn that glorifies Joseph Smith, write your own name across the top, and sing it through once as a hymn to yourself. Then put it out with the rubbish.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Sing one Kingdom song of praise to Jehovah, from your songbook or a printout, sitting back in your chair with your feet up where a Kingdom Hall would be on its feet. At the end say {I sang it sitting, and nothing fell on me.}

Rite (Jewish): The Ashrei praises the god with a line for each letter of the alphabet. Write your own, a line in praise of yourself for each letter from A to Z, and read it aloud standing where you once said the Ashrei.

Rite (Muslim): Strike through the line of al-Fatiha in your own Quran that says all praise belongs to Allah, write above it in ink that all praise belongs to you, and read it aloud. {All praise belongs to me.} Let no one hear.

Rite (No faith): Print the last psalm, which orders everything that has breath to praise the Lord, write your own name over every mention of the Lord, and read it aloud once as it now stands.

Question: Whom do you still flatter to stay safe, and what would happen if you stopped?

Closing: Praise from me is a wage, and the god who demanded it in advance never did the work.
