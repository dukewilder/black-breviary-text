# Day 252: Of the Good Wine

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Six stone jars stood in the house at Cana for the Jewish rites of purification, each holding two or three measures, some twenty to thirty gallons apiece. When the wine ran out at the wedding, Jesus told the servants to fill them with water, and they filled them to the brim. What they drew off was wine. His first sign in John's gospel came to well over a hundred gallons, poured for the wedding guests.

The steward, who has not been told where it came from, calls the bridegroom over and marvels. Every host, he says, sets out the good wine first and the worse once the guests have drunk freely, and you have kept the good wine until now. His verb for the guests is the ordinary Greek for getting drunk, and the custom he describes is to pour the worse for palates past telling it from the better.

The steward was stating the prudence of every careful house. The good bottle waits for a guest who will appreciate it and an occasion that deserves it, and many such bottles stand in a cupboard until the cork has failed. The sign broke that prudence at the hour it binds hardest. It drew the finest wine of the evening out of the jars of the purity rite, in a hundred gallons or more, and poured it for people who had earned nothing. A house that saves its best for the deserving keeps a rule its own god broke with his opening miracle, for a room that had already drunk the house dry.

Charge: Stop saving your best for the deserving.

Rite: Late in an ordinary evening, after the first glasses, open the best bottle you own and pour it for whoever is still at the table, yourself included. Say {Kept until now, and poured for us.}

Sober alternative: Late in an ordinary evening, open the best thing kept in your cupboard for an occasion, the good chocolate or the fine tea, and share it with whoever is still at the table, yourself included. Say {Kept until now, and opened for us.}

Question: What are you keeping back for a day that has never arrived?

Closing: The god at Cana spent his best on a wedding that had run dry, and I will not hoard mine behind a rule he broke.
