# Day 72: Of Love

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Jonathan stripped off his own robe and put it on David, with his armor, his sword, his bow and his belt. It was the day his soul was knit to David's, and the text says he loved him as his own soul. He was the king's son and the heir. At a feast of the new moon his father turned on him, told him he had chosen the son of Jesse to his own shame and to the shame of his mother's nakedness, and threw a spear at him.

Saul gave his reason in the same breath. As long as the son of Jesse lived on the earth, he said, neither Jonathan nor his kingdom would be established. Jonathan rose from the table in fierce anger and ate nothing that day. In the morning he walked out to the field where David was hiding, shot the arrows they had agreed on as a signal, sent the boy back to the city with his weapons, and went to the man his father wanted dead. They kissed and wept together, David the longer, and years afterward David's lament for him called his love wonderful, passing the love of women.

The spear was thrown for an inheritance, and queer children of believing families know that meal, where the son's love is the threat to every portion. In the wilderness of Ziph, Saul's son went to David in the woods and told him that he would be king over Israel and Jonathan second to him, and that Saul his father knew it too. The heir gave the crown away with his own mouth and went home. The book has him dead on Gilboa beside the man who threw the spear, and the narrator never calls the love a mistake.

Charge: Love openly, and let the spear-thrower keep his table.

Rite: Give the person you love something of yours that you have worn, a jacket, a ring, a belt, as Jonathan gave David his robe, and watch them put it on. {Keep it. I will not ask for it back.}

Question: Whom do you love that some father would throw a spear over?

Closing: Call it my mother's shame. It is the best thing I ever chose.
