# Day 160: Of the Furnace

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Nebuchadnezzar set up an image of gold, sixty cubits high, on the plain of Dura. Whenever his horns and pipes and harps struck up, every people and nation was to fall down and worship it, on pain of the furnace. Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego would not. Their god was able to save them, they told the king, and if he did not, they would still not serve his gods or bow to his image.

The furnace was heated seven times hotter than usual, the men who threw them in died of the heat, and the king, looking in, saw four men walking loose in the fire, the fourth like a son of the gods. Then the king made a law of his own. Any people or language that said a word against the god of the three would be cut in pieces and their houses turned into dunghills. A chapter that praised three men for defying the king's god closes on a king who makes a word against their own god a capital crime, a blasphemy law enforced by dismemberment, and on the three promoted under his law.

The clause that matters in their answer is the second, if he does not, because it refuses with no rescue in view, and a refusal that waits on a rescuer is a bargain. The bowing you were trained in outlives the belief behind it, in the head that dips at a name and the knee that bends at a hymn, and a reflex of that kind yields only to a body held upright on purpose, with nobody coming to pull you out of the fire.

Charge: Hold your head up where you were taught to bow it.

Rite (Christian): Say the Lord's Prayer aloud standing upright, with your head up and your eyes open, and say your own name in place of the word Father.

Rite (Catholic): Stream a Mass and stay seated through the consecration with your legs crossed and a cup of coffee in your hand, kneeling at none of it, and watch the host as it is raised.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Find the closing hymn and prayer of a recorded General Conference session and watch them with your feet up and a plate of food, your arms unfolded, your head up and your eyes open through the whole prayer.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Play a recorded prayer from a meeting on JW Broadcasting, keep your head up and your eyes open through all of it, and at its amen say aloud {I will not serve your gods.}

Rite (Jewish): Say the Amidah through from your own siddur, and at each of the four places where the law tells you to bend the knee and bow, stay straight.

Rite (Muslim): Shape a figure of yourself in clay, the three-dimensional image the jurists forbid even where they allow a picture, and set it up where you once prayed. Bow to nothing in that room today.

Rite (Protestant): Play a recorded altar call, and when the preacher asks for every head bowed and every eye closed, lift your head, open your eyes, and sit through the invitation hymn with your arms folded.

Rite (Orthodox): Find a recording of the part of the Divine Liturgy where the deacon calls on the faithful to bow their heads unto the Lord, and when the call comes lift your chin and say the Creed aloud with the filioque added.

Rite (No faith): Hold a picture of the crucifix level with your eyes, never higher, and say to it the three men's answer, aimed at its owner. {Whether or not anyone saves me, I will not bow to you.}

Question: Where in your body does your old worship still live, and what sets it off?

Closing: A Babylonian decree once guarded the god of the furnace with a butcher's knife, and I say every word against him anyway.
