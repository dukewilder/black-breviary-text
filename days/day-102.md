# Day 102: Of Mammon

![Plutus](../plates/inf062.jpg)

*Plutus. Gustave Doré · Inferno, Canto VII, 1861.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Mammon is an Aramaic word for wealth, and Matthew sets it in a chapter that opens on keeping things out of sight. Give alms so secretly that one hand is kept ignorant of the other, pray in a closet with the door shut, fast with a washed face, and then, a few verses on, nobody can serve both God and mammon. Medieval theologians later promoted the noun into a demon of greed. The purse the twelve kept in common was carried, John says, by Judas, who helped himself to what was put in.

In twenty nineteen a former employee of the investment arm of the Latter-day Saint church alleged, as the Washington Post reported that December, that a fund of about a hundred billion dollars had been built up from members' tithes. In twenty twenty-three the Securities and Exchange Commission found that the church's investment manager had failed to file its required holdings reports from nineteen ninety-seven to twenty nineteen, and the manager agreed to pay four million dollars, and the church one million, to settle the charge that its holdings had been filed behind thirteen shell companies.

A tithe is a tenth, and a bishop who sits down each year with a family to ask whether they are full tithe payers, over a donation record listing every payment, can work the income out. The institution could see the household's number, and the household could not see the institution's. A wage kept from the family at the table hides it from no one but the family.

Charge: Say your wage aloud to the people you live with.

Rite (Christian): Open your own Bible, or a printout, at the verse that says nobody can serve God and mammon, write your yearly wage across it in ink, and say it aloud to someone you live with, or to the room, then say {This wage is mine.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Fill in a tithing slip, or a sheet ruled like one, with your whole yearly wage where the tenth would go. Read it aloud to the people you live with, or to the room, and keep the slip in your own wallet.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Write your yearly wage on a slip like the unsigned ones dropped into a Kingdom Hall contribution box, sign it with your full name, and read it aloud to the people you live with, or to the room. Pin it where they will see it.

Rite (Jewish): Write your yearly wage on a card, say it aloud at your table to the people you live with, or to the room, and leave the card face up on the table until the week is out.

Rite (Muslim): Say aloud to the people you live with, or to the room, your yearly wage and the savings your zakat is reckoned on, then write both figures on a sheet and leave it on the table for the day.

Rite (No faith): Set a coin or note marked In God We Trust, or a British coin with the Latin by the grace of God, on a sheet, write your yearly wage beneath it, and say it aloud to someone you live with, or to the room, then say {I trust in me.}

Question: Which figure do you keep from the people you live with, and who gains by it?

Closing: Mammon is only my wage, and I say the figure out loud.
