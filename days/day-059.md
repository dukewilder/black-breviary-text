# Day 59: Of the Temple

![The Death of Samson](../plates/b066.jpg)

*The Death of Samson. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Samson was blind and grinding at a mill in the Philistine prison. They brought him out to entertain the lords of the Philistines at a sacrifice to their god Dagon, in a house so full that three thousand men and women were watching from the roof alone. He asked the boy who led him to let him feel the pillars, and took hold of the two on which the house stood, one with each hand.

Then he called on the god to remember him and strengthen him this once, to be avenged on the Philistines for his two eyes, and he said, let me die with the Philistines, and pushed. The house came down on the lords and on everyone inside it, and the dead Samson killed at his death outnumbered all he had killed in his life. His request named the Philistines without exception, and the roof carried the crowd, come to see a blind man made into sport, down with the lords.

Before you could read you were taught to address the same listener, for the sick to recover and the test to go well, and the asking survives at the edge of sleep and in the minute before the results come back. A wish put in that ear is held to the letter of its asking. At Gaza the narrator records no reply from heaven, only that the house fell on the words Samson spoke, and the letter covered three thousand people who had come for a show. Kept in your own hands, a wish can reach only as far as those hands.

Charge: Keep your next wish in your own hands.

Rite (Christian): Say aloud the thing you most often ask the god for, then write on a card who it would hurt if it were granted to the letter, and do the first step of it yourself today.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Say aloud the thing you most often ask Heavenly Father for, stop without closing in the name of Jesus Christ and without amen, and do the first step of it yourself today.

Rite (Jewish): Eat something first, then say the line of the daily standing prayer that blesses the god who hears prayer, {Blessed are you, who hears prayer}, in the order the Talmud says casts him behind your back. Then do the first step of what you were about to ask for, yourself.

Rite (Muslim): Raise your open palms as in supplication, name the wish you most often ask Allah for, then wipe the empty hands down your face as the prayer ends. {No one will grant this but me.} Do its first step today.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Write the thing you most often ask Jehovah for at the top of a Christmas wish list, the holiday Witnesses refuse, address it to yourself, sign it with your own name, and do the first item today.

Rite (No faith): Before a meal you love, say aloud the thing you most often wish for and name who will do the work of it. {I will see to it, and no god will hear of it.}

Question: What do you still ask for by reflex, and whom would it hurt if it were granted to the letter?

Closing: I ask no god for anything, and no crowd pays for my wishes.
