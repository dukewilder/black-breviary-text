# Day 15: Of Blessing

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The theft of the blessing was Rebekah's plan before it was Jacob's. Isaac, who was old and could not see, had called for his elder son Esau and asked for a dish of game so that he could bless him before he died, and she overheard. While Esau was out hunting she cooked two young goats the way the old man liked them and dressed Jacob in his brother's clothes, with goatskins over his hands and the smooth of his neck. Isaac noticed that the hands and the voice did not match, asked again, took the lie for an answer, and blessed him, with the dew of heaven and the fat of the earth and lordship over his brothers.

When Esau came in with the real dish, Isaac trembled violently and said the blessing was given and would stand. Words spoken over a head had done their work whoever the head belonged to, and the line of promise ran from then on through the son who lied.

The priesthoods took the form for their own mouths. The blessing in the book of Numbers went to the sons of Aaron, absolution went to the ordained, and the catechism leaves the laity only the lesser blessings, ranked by their distance from the altar, below a form a blind old man once handed out for a plate of goat.

Whatever force a blessing has, it had in a tent with no priest in it, in the mouth of a father fooled by goatskin. The men who fenced the form behind ordination were guarding a sentence that had already done its work once for a thief, and ordination has added nothing to it since but the fence.

Charge: Refuse any blessing that comes with an office attached.

Rite (Christian): Lay your right hand on your own head, as the dying Jacob laid his on Joseph's younger son, and bless yourself in Isaac's words. {The dew of heaven and the fat of the earth are mine, and I will rule my rivals.}

Rite (Catholic): Raise your right hand as the priest does at the end of Mass, trace the cross over your own head, and give the blessing in your own name. {I bless myself, and no priest need be in the room.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Read aloud the line of your patriarchal blessing that declares your lineage, or say from memory the lineage you were told you came from, then pronounce your own in its place, naming what you want. {I am of no tribe, and I bless myself.}

Rite (Jewish): Raise your hands with the fingers parted in the priests' sign, which only a descendant of Aaron may make over the congregation, and give the priestly blessing to yourself. {May I be blessed and kept, by my own hand.}

Rite (Muslim): Say the blessing you were taught to give others, {may God bless you}, then say your own name and bless yourself with God left out. {I bless myself, and I ask no one's leave.}

Rite (No faith): Write your own blessing in Isaac's form, what you want of heaven's dew and the earth's fat and whom you mean to outrank, sign it, read it aloud, and pin it above your bed.

Question: What would you wish for yourself if you pronounced it over your own head?

Closing: Isaac's words held over a liar in goatskin, and they hold over me in my own coat.
