# Day 116: Of Guides

![The Descent Begins](../plates/inf026.jpg)

*The Descent Begins. Gustave Doré · Inferno, Canto I, 1861.*

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A Latter-day Saint bishop is a layman chosen from the ward, with a day job and no training required, and he sits across a desk from members to judge their worthiness and hear the confession of serious sin. A Witness elder arrives for a shepherding call appointed, his own literature says, by holy spirit. In the nineteen seventies an American movement of biblical counseling began teaching pastors that scripture is sufficient for nearly every trouble of the mind, so that much depression was to be traced to sin and repented of.

Each of these offices is filled without training in medicine or psychology, and the word for the work comes from the shepherd. The Witnesses call the elder's visit shepherding, and the shepherd of that psalm leads beside still waters and through the valley of the shadow of death, and the flock ends by dwelling in his house for ever. A mountain guide's work ends at the trailhead, and a shepherd's work never ends. The flock is the purpose.

Ezekiel, speaking for the same god, already knew where that claim leads, and charged the shepherds of Israel with ruling the flock by force and cruelty, the sick never healed and the broken never bound up. The desk interview and the shepherding call both assume a sheep that stays a sheep. The inside of your head was never a pasture, and anyone who takes a shepherd's office over it unasked is owed the notice a flock cannot give, a sentence in your own voice and the door.

Charge: Release every shepherd you never asked for.

Rite (Christian): Say the first lines of the shepherd psalm aloud with yourself in the shepherd's place. {I am my own shepherd. I lead myself beside still waters.}

Rite (Catholic): Say aloud the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit as you learned them for confirmation, and at the gift of counsel stop and take it back. {Counsel is mine, and I take it from whom I choose.}

Rite (Protestant): Alone, stand and speak to the pastor whose counsel you never asked for, as though he sat in the empty chair across from you. {You may step down. I keep my own counsel now.}

Rite (Orthodox): Stand before any icon of a holy elder, a printed picture of one, or a bare wall, and ask its blessing as a monk asks his elder's, {Father, bless}, then give it the answer no elder gives. {Bless yourself, and think for yourself.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Alone, take a temple recommend or calling letter you were given, or a sheet with your bishop's name on it, write the word released across it in large letters, and say {I release you from any office over me.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Alone, say aloud to the chair where an elder once sat {You may stop shepherding. I am not your sheep.} then throw out any service-hours slip or territory card you still have.

Rite (Jewish): Alone, say aloud the teaching of the fathers, {Appoint for yourself a teacher}, and add your own line for the rabbi you never chose. {I appoint my teachers, and I did not appoint you.}

Rite (Muslim): Alone, write the name of a cleric whose counsel you never hired on a slip of paper, fold it, throw it out with the day's rubbish, and say {You were never my imam.}

Rite (No faith): Write down the names of everyone who ever counseled you unasked, a pastor, a parent, a pundit, then burn the list and say aloud {Your term is over.}

Question: What will you say to the person who still counsels you without ever having been asked?

Closing: Nobody was appointed to my head, so I took it back and left the crook at the door.
