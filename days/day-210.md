# Day 210: Of Quarrels

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

John Mark first appears as the son of a Jerusalem woman named Mary, in whose house the believers met to pray, and Barnabas and Saul took him along on the first mission as their assistant. At Perga in Pamphylia he left them and went home to Jerusalem, and Luke records the desertion in one clause and goes on with the voyage.

Paul kept the clause. When a second mission was proposed, Barnabas wanted to take his cousin again, Paul refused a man who had abandoned the work once, and the quarrel ran so hot that Luke names it with the Greek word English later borrowed as paroxysm. They parted. Barnabas took Mark and sailed for Cyprus, and Paul took Silas overland through Syria and Cilicia.

The letters record no apology from Mark and no pardon from Paul. Mark comes back as a name in the greetings, a fellow worker in the note to Philemon, a man the church at Colossae is told to welcome if he arrives, and in the last letter carrying Paul's name the old refusal has become an instruction to bring him along, since he is useful for the ministry. Useful is the whole of the reference.

Pulpits tell the story as a reconciliation, and the text has Paul send for Mark the way a craftsman sends for a tool that has proved itself. A partnership broken by a quarrel can run on the same terms. The partner who walked out is refused while the walking out is fresh, and any return is bought with work and needs no apology on either side. Forgiveness would have asked Paul to forget Perga. Luke kept Perga in the book, and Paul kept Mark on the road.

Charge: Take back the useful, and leave the apology unasked.

Rite: Name someone you parted from in a quarrel who is now good at something you need. Send them a message today offering paid work or a part in your next project, and say nothing about the quarrel.

Question: Who did you cut loose once who has since become worth hiring?

Closing: Let the pulpits stage their reconciliations, and I will go on hiring.
