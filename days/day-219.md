# Day 219: Of Boils

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The god first hands the adversary everything Job owns and forbids him only to touch the man. At their second meeting, after the herds are driven off and the servants put to the sword, he widens the license to Job's flesh and reserves nothing but his life. The adversary goes out and does what was permitted, from the soles of the feet to the crown of the head, and Job sits down among the ashes and scrapes himself with a potsherd.

The agent stands astride the fallen man with his arms flung wide, arrows in one hand and a vial emptying from the other, and he has the build of a god because he acts on a god's warrant. Job's wife kneels with her face in her hands.

Job never names the adversary. He begs his friends for pity because the hand of God has touched him, and the god tells the adversary, you moved me against him, to destroy him without cause. When Job's kin come at the end to console him, the narrator says they comforted him over all the evil that the lord had brought upon him, and by then the accuser has been gone from the story for forty chapters.

In Job the striker could not move without the leave, so the harm is first the grantor's. A parent who had the power to stop it and kept the peace, or an elder who heard and filed it away, gave the same leave without a word, while the hand with the arrows took all the blame the family had to give.

Charge: Bill the one who signed before the one who struck.

Rite: Pick the person who permitted a harm you suffered, the signer and not the striker, and from today stop one thing you supply them without return, the lift, the loan, the cooked meal. Spend the hour it used to take on yourself.

Question: Whose permission stands behind the harm you still blame on someone else?

Closing: Job's god admitted that he ruined a man for nothing, and no such god gets the word fair from me.
