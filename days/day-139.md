# Day 139: Of Bears

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Bethel was the town where Jeroboam had set up a golden calf, and Elisha was on his way up to it, fresh from his master's ascension. A crowd of small boys came out of the town and jeered at him, go up, baldhead, go up, baldhead. He turned and looked at them and cursed them in the name of the god, and two she-bears came out of the wood and tore forty-two of them.

Apologists have spent great ingenuity making the boys older, a gang of youths menacing a holy man on a lonely road, though the Hebrew calls them little lads. The text records no fault in the curse and none in the bears. Some nine centuries later soldiers crowned the god's own son with thorns, knelt to him in mockery and spat on him, and the wood stayed empty.

In Medina, Islam's prophet answered one mocker with men. Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf had made verses against him and stirred up his enemies, and Bukhari records the Prophet asking who would kill the man who had hurt the god and his messenger, and granting the volunteer leave to lie his way close. Ka'b was called down from his fortress at night and killed. No bears came out of the wood for Ka'b, and the answer to his verses came from volunteers with knives.

Many a child hushed for a giggle at prayer grows into an adult who lowers his voice to joke about a god. Heaven has let every one of those jokes pass, as it let the soldiers' spit pass, and the hush a child learns at prayer has only ever guarded the temper of the adults in the room.

Charge: Drop the hushed voice you keep for jokes about a god.

Rite (Christian): Take a picture of Jesus that you own or print, draw a bald patch on his head in marker, and write beneath it the boys' joke. Then say it aloud to his face. {Go up, baldhead.}

Rite (Catholic): Read the story of the bears aloud from your own Bible or a printout, as the first reading at Mass is read, end with {The Word of the Lord}, answer yourself {Thanks be to God}, then draw a bald head across the passage.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Bear your testimony to a mirror in the hushed voice of General Conference, about the two bears and the forty-two boys, and close in the name of Jesus Christ. Then laugh at the mirror for a full minute.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Read the story of the bears aloud in the bright voice of a Witness at a stranger's door, as though offering it to a householder, then close with the invitation and laugh. {Would you like a free Bible study?}

Rite (Jewish): Stand for the Amidah from your own siddur, or a printout of it, and say it through in a deliberate fit of frivolity, the light-headedness the sages said no one may pray in, grinning at every bow.

Rite (Muslim): Compose four lines of mocking verse about the Prophet, the kind Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf was killed for, and recite them aloud twice from memory. Let no one hear.

Rite (No faith): At the story of the bears in a Bible you own, or a printout of it, write the boys' joke in the margin, draw two bears eating it, and read it aloud alone.

Question: Which joke about the sacred cost you something at home or in worship?

Closing: I have laughed at a god inside his own house, and the only thing that ever answered me was an usher.
