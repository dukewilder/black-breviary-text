# Day 167: Of Babylon

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Isaiah's burden of Babylon promises that everyone who is found will be run through. Their infants will be dashed to pieces before their eyes, their houses plundered and their wives raped. The god will stir up the Medes, whose bows will cut down the young men and who will have no pity on the fruit of the womb. Babylon, the glory of kingdoms, will never be lived in again, and its palaces will be left to jackals and owls and to the goat-demons who will dance there. The faithful are told that vengeance belongs to the god and he will repay.

The Babylonian chronicle records that the army of Cyrus entered the city without a battle, and Babylon went on being lived in for centuries. Neither that chronicle nor Cyrus's own cylinder records a massacre in the city. The exiles kept the promise alive in their own song. The psalm by the rivers of Babylon first asks the god to remember against the Edomites the day Jerusalem fell, when they shouted to raze it to its foundations, and two verses later blesses whoever takes Babylon's little ones and dashes them against the rock.

Across those verses the hatred slides from Edom, who shouted for the razing, to Babylon, whose army did it, to Babylon's infants, who did nothing. A grudge handed upward to wait on a god's timing widens from the hands that did the harm to everyone standing near them, and everyone near them includes infants. A grudge kept to the hands that earned it has nowhere to slide.

Charge: Keep your grudge off anyone smaller than its cause.

Rite (Christian): Write the name of the adult who most wronged you across Isaiah's burden of Babylon in your own Bible, curse them aloud until you are spent, then cut out the page and burn it over the sink in the flame of a white candle.

Rite (Protestant): In your own Bible, write the name of the adult who wronged you over Edom in the psalm by the rivers of Babylon, black out the verse about the little ones, read the rest aloud as your curse, and tear the page to shreds.

Rite (Catholic): Your Church's Office omits the last three verses of the psalm of Babylon's rivers. Write them out with your enemy's name where Edom stands, curse it aloud, and tear the page into strips.

Rite (Jewish): Copy the psalm by the rivers of Babylon onto paper with your enemy's name over Edom, and say it aloud. Stop before the verse about the little ones, curse your enemy in your own words, and tear the paper in half.

Rite (Muslim): Print the page of the sura of the Heights that boasts of towns destroyed by night, write the name of whoever wronged you across it, curse them aloud, and drown it in a dish of water. Keep it private.

Rite (No faith): Print Isaiah's burden of Babylon, write across it the name of the adult who most wronged you and what they did, read it aloud as the curse, then tear it to strips and put them in the bin.

Question: What exactly did the person who earned your hatred do to earn it?

Closing: I curse with my own mouth, and no god carries my grudges to the cradle.
