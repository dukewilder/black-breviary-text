# Day 86: Of Disdain

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Acts lists the bench by name, Annas the high priest and Caiaphas and John and Alexander and the rest of the high priest's family, the house that had handed Jesus over to Pilate not long before. In front of them stood Peter and John, held overnight for preaching in the temple after healing a lame man at its gate. The question put to them was by what power, or by what name, they had done it.

Peter told the rulers and elders that the man stood whole by the name of the one they had crucified, so that the accused charged the court with a killing in its own chamber. The council saw their boldness, Acts says, perceived that they were unlearned and ignorant men, and marveled. Ordered never again to speak in that name, the two told the council to judge for itself whether God wanted them to obey men in place of him, and after more threats they were let go. A court reduced to marveling at the accused has stopped doing the work of a court.

The engraving above shows a damned man risen to the waist from a burning tomb, holding hell in contempt. Every accusation carries the accuser's standing inside it, and an answer given with lowered eyes grants that standing before it disputes a word. Peter gave them their titles and then named their deed. Whoever shames you for a body or a pleasure that harmed no one is asking by what right you live so, and the question presumes a bench with clean hands, the one thing Peter's bench lacked.

Charge: Keep your eyes level before anyone who would shame you.

Rite: Write to the person who still tries to shame you one sentence naming a thing they did that you can prove and they would rather forget. Send it with no greeting and no apology. Skip it if that person could be dangerous to you.

Question: Who still tries to shame you, and what have they done that they never mention?

Closing: Whoever wants my head bowed can bow his own first.
