# Day 101: Of Thrift

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A man in the crowd asks the teacher to make his brother divide the inheritance with him. The teacher answers that a life does not consist in the abundance of what a man possesses, and tells a parable. The ground of a certain rich man brought forth plentifully. He thought within himself what he should do, because he had no room where to bestow his fruits. He said that he would pull down his barns, and build greater, and there bestow all his fruits and his goods. He would say to his soul, Soul, thou hast much goods laid up for many years, take thine ease, eat, drink, and be merry. But God said unto him, Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee, and then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided. So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God.

Later in the same chapter the same teacher tells his hearers to sell what they have and give alms and lay up a treasure in heaven.

Whoever pays their own way and carries what they owe to the people who depend on them has earned the rest, the good coat or the long dinner, and owes nobody an apology for it. Luke's god calls the saver a fool and collects his soul before one new barn stood. Every plan the man made for his goods was in the future tense, I will pull down my barns, I will say to my soul, and the reply he got named this night.

Charge: Do today what your future tense has been promising.

Rite: Write down one thing you keep saying you will do when the barns are full, a trip, a dinner, a purchase. Pick the version you can afford today. Do it before the day ends, and say {This night.} when you start.

Question: Which of your I wills is waiting on a barn?

Closing: I pay what I owe and then enjoy my evening, with no sermon attached to it.
