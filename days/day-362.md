# Day 362: Of the Other Road

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Narrow is the way that leads to life, the sermon says, and few find it. Wide is the gate and broad the way to destruction, and many go in thereat. The saying gives their widths, their traffic and where each ends, and leaves the reader to supply the country.

Luke sets it as the reply when someone asks whether few are saved. Strive to enter, for many will seek to enter and not be able. Many take the wide road, and apart from its width, its crowd and where it ends the sermon tells you nothing about it.

The Book of Mormon fills it. The prophet Lehi dreams a tree whose fruit is sweeter than any he has tasted, a mist of darkness that takes the path from people already on their way, and a fountain where many drown. On the far side of the river stands a great and spacious building full of old and young in exceedingly fine clothes, pointing and mocking at those who eat. Those who leave the path are shown only at a distance, lost in forbidden paths and strange roads, and the one life off the path drawn in any detail is a crowd in fine clothes, in a building Lehi's son later watches fall.

Every map of the wide road was drawn from the narrow one, and shows what can be seen from there, a crowd mocking, a mist, a fall promised at the end. Whoever goes through the wide gate finds the rest, an evening at cards or on a dance floor, a Sunday morning spent in bed, and finds destruction where the sermon placed it, at the far end, where both roads go into the ground.

Charge: Go through the wide gate and look around.

Rite (Christian): Read aloud the verse about the wide gate, then spend two hours at what your church warned would lead you through it, a dance, a card game or an evening at a bar with a soft drink if you prefer, and enjoy it.

Rite (Catholic): Say {Many go in, and I go with them}, then spend an hour reading your own palm from a library book or a printed chart, a divination the Catechism rejects, and enjoy it.

Rite (Protestant): Pick one amusement your church called worldly, a dance, a film or a deck of cards, and give it an hour today. At the door say {Many go in, and I go with them}.

Rite (Orthodox): Spend an hour at something your priest would call a sin to enjoy, a dance hall, a card table or a film he would forbid, and say aloud {I go without a blessing} at your own door before you step out.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Spend an hour somewhere crowded and well dressed, a mall, a market or a busy restaurant, the great and spacious building of the dream. Buy yourself one fine thing to wear and say {I take the other road}.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Buy or borrow a novel about wizards, a thing the Society warns against, and spend an hour reading it with pleasure, saying {I take the other road} before you begin.

Rite (Jewish): Read aloud the commandment in Numbers against following your own heart and your own eyes. Then spend an hour at whatever your eyes and heart choose first, and say {The other road begins here} before you set out.

Rite (Muslim): Spread your prayer mat, or a towel if you have no mat, so that it points away from Mecca, recite the line of the opening chapter that asks to be guided on the straight path, then stand and walk off the mat the way it points.

Rite (No faith): Copy the verse about the broad way onto a sheet, take it to any place you were told was worldly, a dance hall, a tattoo parlor or a late-night diner, enjoy an hour there, and bin the sheet on your way out.

Question: Which pleasure of the broad way do you know only from the warnings against it?

Closing: I was warned off the wide road by people who never walked it, and I walked it.
