# Day 203: Of Wounds

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

One psalm under David's name complains of a lying tongue turned against him and hatred repaid for his love, then curses the man responsible in order. Let his prayer be counted as sin, let his days be few and another take his office, let his children be fatherless and beg, let his name be blotted out in the generation after. Benedict's Rule gave monks the whole psalter every week from the sixth century, and the Roman Breviary kept the psalm in its cycle until nineteen seventy.

That year the reform of the hours struck it, with two other psalms whose curses ran as hot. The instruction explaining the cut concedes that scripture's last book prays the same way, where the souls under the altar cry out how long until their blood is avenged, and it gives as its reason a certain psychological difficulty. The psalm had not changed. The Church's nerve had, and a prayer the hours had carried for fourteen hundred years became a difficulty to spare the people praying the hours.

The clergy who preached love of enemies from the gospel had been saying this psalm, so whoever was raised to treat hatred as a stain was raised by men who prayed it. The psalm states the injury before it asks for anything, and one of its curses follows the deed, as he loved cursing, let cursing come to him. Its one failure is the family, five of thirty-one verses that strike a wife and children who had done nothing and charge him with his parents' sins. The same reform cut single verses from other psalms. It cut all thirty-one, and a curse aimed at one man went out of the breviary with the five that missed.

Charge: Fit every curse to the deed it answers.

Rite: Print Psalm 109 and strike out verses nine, ten, twelve, thirteen and fourteen, the ones aimed at the man's family. Write your enemy's name in the margin, read what is left aloud standing, as monks read it for centuries, and say {Every line left here is yours.}

Question: What loss would match exactly what your enemy did to you?

Closing: The god's own psalm taught me to answer an injury line for line, and the Church that prayed it for fourteen hundred years struck it out.
