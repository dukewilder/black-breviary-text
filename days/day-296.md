# Day 296: Of Mourning

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The women who had watched the cross from a distance went home and prepared spices and ointments before sundown. Then the sabbath began, and Luke says they rested on the sabbath day according to the commandment, while the body they meant to anoint lay in the rock. They came back at first light with their spices, and the tomb was empty. Luke gives them no body to anoint.

The rabbis of tractate Sanhedrin ask outright whom a eulogy is for, whether the speech honors the living or the dead, and settle it for the dead, so that a man who asked for no eulogy is obeyed after he is buried. Ben Sira, in a book the Catholic and Orthodox Bibles still print, had already priced grief for the mourner, telling him to weep bitterly for a day or two, to avoid criticism, and then to take comfort, because there is no coming back, and grief does the dead no good and injures the one who grieves.

The rabbis took it on faith that the dead man listens, and Rav told the man who would speak over him to speak warmly, since he would be standing there. Ben Sira wrote that thanksgiving has ceased from the dead as from one who does not exist, and by his count the speech has no hearer. The friend who sat up at the hospital bed through the bad nights and the brother who drove all night to get there can be given now what the funeral would have said of them, while they are in the room to hear it. Held back for the funeral, the same words go to a closed lid, and the friend who sat up through the bad nights never hears them.

Charge: Say the eulogy to the living.

Rite: Write the eulogy you would give for one living person who has earned it, and read it to them today, in person or on the phone. Bring or send something good to eat with it.

Question: Whose eulogy are you saving for a day they will not hear it?

Closing: I will take one compliment to my face over every sermon preached above my coffin.
