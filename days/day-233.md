# Day 233: Of Abominations

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Stork and heron are abominations. Leviticus lists them among the birds that may not be eaten, with the eagle and the vulture and the lapwing and the bat, and spends the word most freely on the water. Whatever lives in the seas and the rivers without fins and scales, the lobster and the crab and the oyster on its rock among them, is an abomination, and the King James text says so four times in three verses.

Genesis had ruled on all of them first. When the god had finished the sea creatures and the birds on the fifth day and the creeping things on the sixth, he looked over everything he had made and found it very good, and the Law, arriving later in the same scroll, overruled him on the oyster. The older verdict was given by the maker in person, with every creature in front of him, and the newer one was handed down through Moses and Aaron as a menu. After the flood the same god had handed Noah every moving thing that lives as meat, as freely as the green herb, and the rabbis still leave every gentile on that menu, oyster included.

A lobster is dark as slate when it is caught and scarlet when it is cooked, an oyster opens on its own measure of sea water, and a crab laid out on ice looks like armor enameled for a parade. Set on the best plate in the house among linen and lemons, the Law's abominations come to the table as ornament before they are eaten with the hands, and whoever cracks the claw over good china has taken the older verdict over the newer.

Charge: Feast on what the priests called filth.

Rite: Buy a cooked lobster, crab or a dozen oysters and lay them on your best plate with a linen napkin and cut lemons. Say aloud {This is very good.} and eat with your hands. If shellfish would harm you, eat smoked eel, which the rabbis forbid too.

Question: Which creature you were taught was unclean would you eat first, and in whose company?

Closing: What Leviticus called an abomination I serve on linen, and I lick the butter off my fingers.
