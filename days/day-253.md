# Day 253: Of the Samaritan Woman

![Jesus and the Woman of Samaria](../plates/b171.jpg)

*Jesus and the Woman of Samaria. Gustave Doré · La Grande Bible de Tours, 1866.*

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

At Jacob's well, at about noon, Jesus asked a Samaritan woman to give him a drink, and she asked how a Jew could ask that of her. John adds in his own voice that Jews had no dealings with Samaritans, and the Mishnah, written down about a century after the gospel, counts the daughters of the Samaritans as women unclean from the cradle. A few lines on he told her to fetch her husband, and when she said she had none, he counted five husbands and the sixth man who was none of them.

Preachers have made her a harlot for centuries, and the text gives her no such name. Under the law of Moses only a husband could write the bill of divorce, so each of the five marriages ended in a death or in a man's decision. Jesus asked her for no sorrow and no vow. She turned the talk to where the god should be worshiped, and afterward she left her water jar at the well to tell the town she had met a man who told her everything she ever did.

You were taught to keep a count like hers hidden, or to tell it with your eyes down and a confession attached. Many Samaritans of that town believed, John says, because of what the woman said, so the first person in his gospel to bring a whole town out was a woman with six men behind her. The repentance preached over her since is the pulpit's addition to a scene in which her unrepented word was enough to empty a town onto the road.

Charge: Tell your history with your eyes up.

Rite (Christian): Say aloud at your own table, in order, the name of everyone you have slept with or wanted to, the things Ephesians says it is a shame even to speak of, and end with {I repent of none of it.}

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Say your history aloud at your own table, in order, naming every act the law of chastity forbids, and end with {I repent of none of it.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Say your history aloud at your own table, in order and in full, the account a judicial committee of elders would demand of you behind a closed door, and end with {No committee will ever hear this.}

Rite (Jewish): Say your history aloud at your own table, in order and in plain words, the sins the Yom Kippur confession has you strike your chest for, and end with {I strike nothing, and I repent of none of it.}

Rite (Muslim): Say aloud to the room, in the first person and in order, your whole romantic history, the open telling a hadith says puts a sinner outside forgiveness, and end with {I did that, and I am not ashamed.}

Rite (No faith): Say your history aloud at your own table, in order and in plain words, the version you would never tell a parent or a pastor, and end with {I repent of none of it.}

Question: For whose comfort do you still tell your history with the facts rearranged?

Closing: My history is told standing, and nobody who taught me to kneel for it gets to hear it.
