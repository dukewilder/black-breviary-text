# Day 309: Of Work

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The first Soviet constitution, adopted in nineteen eighteen, set down in its article on labor the rule that whoever does not work shall not eat, a sentence a letter in Paul's name had carried long before. The letter to the Thessalonians says it of believers who had stopped working and were living as busybodies off the community, after Paul had worked night and day among them so as to be a burden to none. It was written to shame a few idle members of one congregation.

Captain John Smith made it law at Jamestown in sixteen oh eight, telling the colonists that the labor of thirty or forty industrious men would no longer feed a hundred and fifty idle loiterers, and he later threatened to banish across the river anyone who gathered less than he did, until the man mended or starved. Proverbs had already sent the sluggard to school with the ant, which has no guide, overseer or ruler and still gathers its food, so the scripture's own model worker answers to no one. The two who wrote it into law, the president of a starving fort and the drafters of a workers' republic, each spoke it from above a workforce.

Guilt on an empty afternoon keeps the rule now, and so does rest defended as a way to work better later, the foreman's voice still talking after the shift has ended. An hour of idleness neither earned nor saved for later use answers the whole theology, and the dinner eaten in that hour is the one meal of the week that answers to no shift bell.

Charge: Eat one dinner you have not earned.

Rite: Cook a slow dish, one that wants an hour of simmering and a stir now and then, and eat it at an hour you ought to be working, with your feet on a chair. If guilt arrives, keep eating.

Question: Whose voice tells you that rest must be justified, and what did it ever pay you?

Closing: No overseer has a claim on the time I waste, and I waste it at the table.
