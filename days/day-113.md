# Day 113: Of the Spectacle

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

A Christian of Carthage, writing around the year two hundred to warn believers away from the circus and the theater, promised them a better show. On the day of judgment, he wrote, they would watch kings groaning in the lowest dark, magistrates melting in hotter flames than they had ever lit for Christians, philosophers burning beside the pupils they had taught, tragedians louder-voiced in their own tragedy, and actors made nimbler by the fire. He asked what praetor, consul or priest could ever put on such a sight.

Not everyone in the Church could manage the view. Origen of Alexandria wrote that the goodness of God would bring all creatures to one and the same end, and in five hundred forty-three, nearly three centuries after his death, the emperor Justinian condemned him as a heretic and his writings with him. The Carthaginian ranked his promised show above the circus, both theaters and every racecourse.

A congregation with a ban or a shunning keeps a smaller gallery. The name read out from the front, the excommunicated cousin, the woman discussed over the meal after the service, each has an audience, and the Witnesses' platform announcement that a person is no longer one of them is read to a full hall, which then goes home to its dinner. The psalm the Church sang says the righteous man rejoices when he sees the vengeance, and that joy was the reward on offer. A room that sits through a shaming in silence has taken a seat in that gallery the Carthaginian promised, and whoever crosses the floor to stand beside the shamed has left the gallery before heaven raises the curtain.

Charge: Speak up the next time your people shame someone.

Rite (Christian): In your own Bible, black out the holy angels and the Lamb in the verse of Revelation where the damned are tormented in their presence. Then send someone your church shamed two plain sentences on their side.

Rite (Catholic): Cut every saint out of the upper half of a print of the Last Judgment, one you own or print, and paste them face down among the damned. Then send someone your church shamed two plain sentences on their side.

Rite (Protestant): In your own Bible, cross out every alleluia Revelation sings over a city burning for ever, and write the word Shame beside each one. Then send someone your church shamed two plain sentences on their side.

Rite (Orthodox): Make three full prostrations, forehead to the floor, before a sheet bearing the name of someone your church shamed, the bow the Liturgy gives the holy things. Then send someone your church shamed two plain sentences on their side.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): In your own Book of Mormon, or a printout, write your ward's name across Lehi's great and spacious building and black out the verse where those below are ashamed. Then send someone your church shamed two plain sentences on their side.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Print one picture of the saved and one of the destroyed at Armageddon, or use any pictures of the saved and the lost. Paste the smiling saved among the dead. Then send someone shunned by your congregation two plain sentences on their side.

Rite (Jewish): Print the psalm in which the righteous wash their feet in the blood of the wicked, soak the page in a basin of water, and wash your own feet in it. {My feet are clean of their joy.} Then send someone your community shamed two plain sentences on their side.

Rite (Muslim): In your Quran, in the sura of those who stand in ranks, black out the words of the man in paradise looking down at his old friend in the fire. Then send someone your community shamed two plain sentences on their side. Do it unseen.

Rite (No faith): Write on a card a public shaming you once watched in silence, and burn it. Then send the person who was shamed, or anyone a church cast out, two plain sentences on their side.

Question: What would you say now to the person whose public shaming you once watched in silence?

Closing: Heaven promised its saints a free view of the burning, and I spit on the ticket.
