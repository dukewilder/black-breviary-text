# Day 251: Of the Kingdoms

The Black Breviary, Book III, Assent. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Luke has the devil show Jesus all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time and state his terms in one breath. Their power and glory have been handed over to him, he says, to give to whomever he likes, and all of it will belong to Jesus if he will worship. It is the clearest offer in the gospels, the goods and the price named together by a seller who claims the title. Jesus refuses with a line from Deuteronomy and never contests the title, and the gospel of John afterward calls the adversary the ruler of this world three times.

The Church preached the refusal and kept a price for itself. The gospels bless the poor and send the disciples out with neither gold nor silver in their purses, and the bishops of Gaul, meeting at Mâcon in five hundred eighty-five, demanded a tenth of every harvest and backed the demand with excommunication. The letter to Timothy had already told the rest to be content with food and raiment.

Those raised on that letter learn to treat asking as greed and to call the silence humility, and whoever sets their wage profits from it. The raise never requested and the invoice still sent at last year's rate are prices someone else set while the worker kept quiet. Work has a figure that can be found out, from people who do it and from published pay data. At the table where your pay is set, the one party who has never named a figure is the one doing the work.

Charge: Name your price in writing, today.

Rite: Look up what your work pays elsewhere, then send whoever sets your pay a message naming a higher figure, and add nothing. With no pay to ask for, price something you own above what you would have taken and offer it for sale.

Question: When did you last ask to be paid more, and what has stopped you since?

Closing: The devil quoted his terms to the son of God's face, and I quote mine to the payroll office.
