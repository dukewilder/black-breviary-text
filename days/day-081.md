# Day 81: Of Contempt

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Amos called the rich women of Samaria cows of Bashan. They crush the needy, he said, and tell their husbands to bring them drink, and they will be hauled out through the breaches in the wall on hooks. Luke has Jesus, warned that Herod meant to kill him, send word back to that fox, and Paul told the Galatians he wished the men unsettling them would go the whole way with the knife and, on the usual reading, castrate themselves.

The psalm that marvels at being fearfully and wonderfully made has its singer, near the end, hating the god's enemies with a perfect hatred and counting them his own. Every one of these insults has an address, and every one is answering a deed. Amos himself turned from the women of Samaria to Bethel with the words come to Bethel, and transgress, an address of its own.

The Church handed the pew the Sermon on the Mount's love for enemies and prayer for persecutors. The older law had put the two in order. Leviticus forbids hating your brother in your heart and in the same verse commands you to rebuke your neighbor, and only in the next does it forbid the grudge and ask for love.

Contempt with a name and a deed attached is the scriptural kind, the kind the prophets spoke aloud and the law turned into rebuke. Hatred kept in the heart, the only kind the pew was left and the kind Leviticus forbids, has no one to be said to and no place to end.

Charge: Spend your contempt on the office and its deed.

Rite: Write the name of an institution or office whose deed you despise and, beneath it, what it did. Read both aloud in an empty room, say {Contempt is owed here}, and pin the page to a wall for a day before you bin it.

Question: Which office or institution has your contempt by right of what it did, and what act of yours would finish it?

Closing: My contempt has an address and a deed, and I owe that office no respect.
