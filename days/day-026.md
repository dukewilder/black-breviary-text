# Day 26: Of Limbo

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

The Council of Florence decreed in fourteen thirty-nine that the souls of those who die in original sin alone, which means unbaptized infants, go down into hell with those who die in mortal sin, to be punished unequally. Earlier theologians had allowed that such infants would receive the mildest punishment in hell, and the medieval schools softened it further into a border region where unbaptized babies would feel no pain and never see the face of God. The Church's ordinary teaching went on mentioning the theory until the Second Vatican Council.

Limbo was never defined as dogma. The parishes acted on the old sentence anyway. For centuries the bodies of unbaptized babies were buried outside consecrated ground, and in Ireland the burial grounds set apart for them lay at townland boundaries and inside old ringforts, the earliest written mention of them dating from sixteen nineteen. In two thousand seven the Vatican's International Theological Commission, with the pope's approval, found serious grounds to hope that such infants are saved.

The code of canon law in force today lets a bishop permit a church funeral for a child who died before baptism, provided the parents had meant to have the child baptized, so a dead infant's funeral still turns on a bishop's permission and on what its parents had in mind. The same code orders an infant in danger of death baptized without delay, which keeps the old sentence in force as a deadline.

Parents meet that deadline beside a hospital cot, where in necessity anyone at hand may baptize a dying infant, and earlier parents met the older sentence in a field. In the Irish burial grounds many graves were marked only by a ring of small stones laid in the outline of the body, on ground no bishop had consecrated.

Charge: Owe nothing to the church that damned the unbaptized.

Rite (Christian): Take your own baptism certificate, or a sheet on which you write its date, the paper the Church says saved you from the fate it invented for the unbaptized, and tear it to pieces. Put the pieces in the bin.

Rite (Catholic): Print the canon that orders an infant in danger of death baptized without delay, or copy it out by hand, nail the page to a scrap of wood, and drive the nail through the word delay.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Take the card or record of a baptism for the dead you performed, or write on paper the name of one dead person you were baptized for, and tear it in half. {You were never mine to save.}

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Go to the children's graves in a cemetery near you and lay one small stone on a grave, with no tract or verse left beside it. {You rest here, and no one decides who wakes.}

Rite (Jewish): Say the mourner's Kaddish aloud to its end for an infant who died before thirty days, a child the law sets outside formal mourning, yours or a friend's.

Rite (Muslim): Copy out the hadith in which the Prophet, asked where the dead children of unbelievers go, answers that God knows best what they would have done, then strike through the answer and write your own beneath it. {They had done nothing.}

Rite (No faith): Print the Florence decree that sends those who die in original sin alone down into hell, fold it into a paper boat, and sink it in a basin of tap water until the ink runs.

Question: Who taught you the doctrine that was laid on top of a death you mourned?

Closing: My dead lie where I put them, and no font decides which ground will have them.
