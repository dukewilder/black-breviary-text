# Day 64: Of Amalek

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before the Lord at Gilgal. The god had told Saul to strike Amalek and spare none of it, down to the camels and the asses, in payment for an ambush laid on Israel's stragglers in the wilderness. Saul killed the people and kept their king alive with the best of the cattle, so the old prophet finished the work with his own hands, on a king who had come to him saying that surely the bitterness of death was past.

Well over sixteen centuries later another prophet closed a siege the same way. A Jewish tribe of Medina surrendered and accepted an arbiter, who ruled that the men be killed and the women and children taken captive, and the most trusted collection of the Prophet's sayings has him approving the verdict as the judgment of the god. The earliest biography puts the trenches in the market and the men beheaded into them in batches, six or seven hundred by its count, and a report in another collection says the boys were sorted by whether their pubic hair had grown.

Both records keep the killing as a credit to the god, and Deuteronomy turns the same sword inward. The brother, the wife or the friend who secretly urges the worship of other gods is to be stoned, the hearer's own hand first upon him, and the hearer is told not to spare him in the very word Samuel used against Amalek, the only other verse in the Bible where it stands in that form. Saul lost his kingdom for sparing Agag. A leaver whom a brother hears urging him after other gods stands in that book under the same order, and the brother's is the hand it names to throw the first stone.

Charge: Read the sentence your old book passes on you.

Rite (Christian): Black out Samuel's order against Amalek in your own Bible, infant and suckling included, then find the law in Deuteronomy that orders the straying brother stoned and black that out too.

Rite (Protestant): Read aloud Samuel's order to spare neither infant nor suckling in the bright voice of a youth pastor announcing a memory verse, then black the verse out in your own Bible and write your name in the margin beside it.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Black out Samuel's order against Amalek in the King James Bible you were given, and write beside it Mountain Meadows, the massacre in which Mormon militia killed some hundred and twenty emigrants and spared only the small children.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Black out Samuel's order to spare neither infant nor suckling in your own New World Translation, and write your name in the margin beside the blank.

Rite (Jewish): Read aloud the law in Deuteronomy that orders the brother who urges you toward other gods stoned, with your own hand first upon him. Black out the words that tell you not to spare him, in your Chumash or a printout, and say {I spare him.}

Rite (Muslim): Read aloud the verses on the people brought down from their fortresses in the chapter of the Confederates, then black them out in your own Quran with a thick marker. Post nothing.

Rite (No faith): Print the verse in which Samuel hews Agag in pieces before the Lord, black out every word of it with a thick marker, and throw the page away.

Question: How would you read your old book if you read it as the court that sentenced you?

Closing: The word that doomed Amalek was written for me too, and I strike it out wherever I find it.
