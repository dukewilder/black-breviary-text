# Day 206: Of Simony

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Simon the magician had already been baptized when he made his offer. The book of Acts says he believed and stayed close to Philip, and when he watched Peter and John lay hands on the converts of Samaria and give them the Holy Spirit, he held out money and asked for the same power. Peter told him his silver could perish with him. The crime of selling holy things has carried the magician's name ever since, though he was the one man in the story who named what he wanted and offered to pay for it.

The Church kept the curse and the price in separate ledgers. Council after council condemned simony, while the papal chancery granted dispensations from the Church's own rules by fixed scales of fees, printed in Rome before the Reformation, so that a cousin could marry a cousin or a bastard be ordained once the tax was paid. Paid at the papal desk, the money was a fee for the clerks' labor, and the same money in a magician's hand was the sin that bears his name.

The desks have multiplied. A retreat charges a month's rent for a weekend of fasting and breathing, a cleanse ships its powders every thirty days, and an app bills monthly to count the days you stayed pure. Each sells the feeling of being washed, whatever name the label gives it, and the billing cycle counts on the feeling fading before the renewal date. Simon at least asked for a power he could watch work on other people. Twelve charges a year for the same washing are twelve notices a year that you are still dirty, and the seller writes every one.

Charge: Owe nothing to the sellers of clean hands.

Rite: Cancel one payment whose real product is the feeling of being clean, the cleanse, the retreat deposit, the purity app or the church pledge. Give the same sum today to someone who has earned it from you, and say as it goes {Paid where it was earned.}

Question: Who profits each month from your believing you are dirty?

Closing: Every priest who sells a clean conscience can wait in line behind my plumber.

Sources quoted:

- asked for the same power (Acts 8:14–19)
