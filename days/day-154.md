# Day 154: Of Ruined Walls

The Black Breviary, Book II, Refusal. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Nehemiah had been cupbearer to the king of Persia, and he came to Jerusalem with the king's letters and for three days said nothing. Then he rode out in the night, by the Valley Gate past the Dragon Well to the Dung Gate, looking at the broken walls and the burned gates, and where the King's Pool left his horse no room to pass he went up the gully and came back in by the gate he had left from. The officials of the city never knew where he had been.

Sanballat and Tobiah were displeased that anyone had come to seek the good of Israel, and when the building began, so did the mockery. Tobiah said a fox climbing on it would knock their stone wall down. The builders worked with swords belted on.

When the wall was almost up, his enemies sent four times asking him down to a village on the plain of Ono, meaning him harm, and four times he sent the same answer, that he was doing a great work and could not come down. The fifth message came as an open letter charging him with building the wall to make himself king. He replied that they had made it up out of their own hearts.

They meant to frighten the builders, Nehemiah writes, so that their hands would drop from the work, and when the open letter failed they hired a prophet to scare him into hiding in the temple. Whoever waits on your collapse, the relative who predicted the bankruptcy or the partner who left before it, will send the same invitation down to the plain, and every hour given to that meeting is an hour taken off the wall. It stood in fifty-two days, and his enemies were cast down in their own eyes.

Charge: Answer every summons from the plain with the same refusal.

Rite: Walk the ruin you keep describing instead of mending, the debt or the half-built thing, and measure it alone, on paper. Make the one repair you can finish today, and answer whoever asks after it. {I am doing a great work and cannot come down.}

Question: Who would most enjoy watching your ruin stay a ruin, and what will they see when the wall stands?

Closing: The man who laid no stone gets no vote on my wall, and no look at it until the gates hang.
