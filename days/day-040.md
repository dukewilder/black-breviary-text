# Day 40: Of the Bronze Serpent

The Black Breviary, Book I, Burden. All 365 days: https://blackbreviary.app/llms/days.md

Hezekiah broke a bronze snake that Moses had made. The book of Kings lists the breaking among his reforms, because the children of Israel had been burning incense to it down to his own day, and then says that none of Judah's kings trusted the god as he did. Kings says he called it Nehushtan, a lump of bronze.

In the book of Numbers the people complain of the manna, the god sends fiery serpents among them and many die, and when they repent he prescribes the remedy, a serpent cast in bronze and raised on a pole, so that whoever is bitten and looks at it will live. Somewhere in the centuries between the desert and the hammer, the antidote acquired a congregation.

The Mishnah ruled that the serpent could neither kill nor heal, and that the bitten lived when they turned their hearts to their father in heaven. The Gospel of John made the pole a prophecy, so that the son of man must be lifted up as Moses lifted the serpent in the wilderness. Each reading moved the cure's power somewhere else, into trust in the one and into a crucifixion in the other, and left the serpent standing as a prop.

The medicine that got you through, or the book that broke a spell, is a tool in your hand. Defend it with the heat once given to a creed and it has begun to own you. The incense in Hezekiah's day was burned by people who had never been bitten, to a cure that had cured no one living.

Charge: Use what healed you, and owe it no devotion.

Rite (Christian): Read the verse in John that makes Moses' serpent a figure of the crucified. Take the cross from your wall, or tie two sticks into one, use it once as a straightedge or a wedge, and put it in the drawer with the tools.

Rite (Latter-day Saint): Take any olive oil and consecrate it aloud as the priesthood consecrates oil for the sick, though no one ordained you. {I consecrate this oil.} Then use it as plain oil on a squeaking hinge or a stiff lock.

Rite (Jehovah's Witness): Wear a cheap cross on a chain for an hour at home, the shape the Society calls pagan, teaching that Jesus died on an upright stake, and while it hangs on you read John's verse lifting the son of man as Moses lifted the serpent.

Rite (Jewish): Murmur over any sore spot, or over the back of your own hand, the verse in Exodus that names the god your healer, {I am the Lord who heals you}, the charm the Mishnah says forfeits the world to come. Then see to the body yourself.

Rite (Muslim): Hang a blue charm against the evil eye on your wall, bought for a few dollars or drawn on card, the kind of amulet a hadith says it is shirk to hang, and sit beneath it for ten minutes.

Rite (No faith): Draw the bronze serpent on its pole as Numbers describes it, write beneath it the creed you have made of your cure, and tear the drawing in two as Hezekiah broke the original. Keep using the cure.

Question: What cure in your life has started asking you for loyalty?

Closing: My cure works or goes in the drawer, and no one collects incense on it.
